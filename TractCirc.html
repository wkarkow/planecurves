Tractrix of a Circle

Path of a point pulled by a taut leash of fixed length \( a \) while the other end travels on a circle of radius \( R \).

Tractrix of a Circle differential equation:
\[ \frac{d\theta}{dr} = \frac{\sqrt{4R^{2}r^{2} - (r^{2} + R^{2} - a^{2})^{2}}}{r(r^{2} - R^{2} + a^{2})} \]

History and Applications

The tractrix of a circle1 is the path traced by a point that is pulled by a taut leash of fixed length \( a \) while the other end of the leash (the “puller”) moves steadily along a circle of radius \( R \). When \( 0 < a < R \), two distinct branches exist: an inner branch that begins at distance \( R - a \) from the center and spirals inward toward the same caustic circle of radius \( \sqrt{R^{2} - a^{2}} \), and an outer branch that begins at distance \( R + a \) and spirals inward toward the same caustic. When \( a > R \), the curve crosses inside the pulling circle and generates multiple sheets indexed by an integer parameter \( k \), with a characteristic angular offset \( 2k\theta_{0} \) where \( \theta_{0} = \pi\left(\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{a^{2} - R^{2}}} - 1\right) \). The curve belongs to the broader family of tractrices and pursuit curves whose study began with Christiaan Huygens in 1692 for the classic (straight-line) tractrix.2 The same notes include the special circular case in which the string equals the radius of the directing circle; the 1905 editors remark that Huygens stated, without a calculation, that this spiral approaches the center of that circle.2 The circular variant appears in later classical differential geometry as an example of a curve possessing a circular caustic and as a roulette generated by a circle rolling inside or outside another circle under appropriate length constraints. It also arises naturally in kinematics as the path of a trailer being towed by a vehicle that travels in a circular arc. In the following code, when \( a \) is small enough that the curve fails to complete at least a full circular arc, it may be necessary to add in the caustic (asymptotic) circle whose radius \( = \sqrt{R^{2} - a^{2}} \) for visual completeness due to the exponentially shortening segments that are drawn by the code. When choosing options, 'Long' is one petal at an arbitrary angle, which may or may not close when upon repeating various $k$ branches. The Symmetric option draws a petal with the leash chosen so the copies close.

In engineering the tractrix of a circle models the motion of wheeled trailers or towed vehicles when the towing point moves along a curved path, aiding the design of steering and path-planning algorithms for articulated vehicles.3 In geometry and optics the inner caustic circle is the envelope of the leash positions; this property has been used in the study of reflection caustics and in the design of certain curved mirrors or reflective surfaces. The multi-sheeted forms that appear when \( a > R \) provide visual examples of curves with rotational symmetry and have been employed in decorative patterns and in the graphical exploration of roulettes and epicycloidal motion.
