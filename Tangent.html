Tangent Curve

Graph of \( y = a \cdot \tan(bx - c) \).

Tangent Cartesian equation:
\[ y = a \cdot \tan(x) \]
Trigonometric identity:
\[ \tan(x) = \dfrac{\sin(x)}{\cos(x)} \]
Generalized Cartesian equation:
\[ y = a \cdot \tan(bx - c) = \dfrac{a \cdot \sin(bx - c)}{\cos(bx - c)} \]

History and Applications

The tangent curve is the graph of the scaled and phase-shifted function \( y = a \cdot \tan(bx - c) \). Its historical roots lie in practical measurement rather than abstract angle theory. In the sixth century BCE the Greek philosopher Thales employed ratios of shadow lengths to heights to determine the height of the Egyptian pyramids, establishing the proportional relationship that later became the definition of the tangent.1,2 Arab mathematicians around 860 CE produced the earliest known tables of “shadows” (umbra recta and umbra versa), systematically tabulating values for what are now recognized as the tangent and cotangent; these tables supported astronomy, sundial construction, and surveying.1 During the Islamic Golden Age scholars such as al-Battani and Abū al-Wafā' refined these tables and expressed the tangent explicitly as the ratio of sine to cosine. The modern name “tangent” (Latin tangens, “touching,” from the geometric tangent line to the unit circle) was introduced by the Danish mathematician Thomas Fincke in his 1583 treatise Geometriae Rotundi.2 The abbreviation “tan” first appeared in the work of Albert Girard in 1626.1 With the rise of analytic geometry and calculus in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, the function was placed on a firm analytic footing and its graph constructed, displaying the periodic branches separated by vertical asymptotes at odd multiples of \( \pi/2 \). The derivative of the tangent, \( \sec^{2}(\theta) \), is always positive away from the discontinuities, confirming that each branch is strictly increasing, an observation central to understanding rates of change.

Real-life applications of the tangent function and its graph are well documented in engineering and the natural sciences. Surveyors and architects have used the relation height = distance × tan(elevation angle) since antiquity to measure buildings, trees, and terrain features; the method remains standard in modern land surveying and construction layout.3 In civil engineering the slope (grade) of a road, ramp, or roof is expressed directly as \( m = \tan(\theta) \), allowing precise conversion between angular inclination and rise-over-run for drainage design, accessibility compliance, and vehicle dynamics.4 Aircraft performance calculations employ the same relation to convert flight-path angle into vertical speed given groundspeed. In architectural practice the tangent of the solar altitude angle determines the required length of roof overhangs for seasonal shading and passive energy control.5 The function also appears in mechanics through the angle of repose of granular materials, where the tangent of the critical angle equals the coefficient of static friction.

(defun C:TANGENT (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xspan paramstep restartflag
                    id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 maxdraw fa fb fc fd fe ff fg fh p1 p2)
  (setq id "Tangent Curve"
        iq "y=a·tan(b·x-c)"
        dcl "TANGENT"
        opt '("a,b values" "Zero pt, a value" "Asymptote, a,b values" "Asymptote pt, zero pt" "Phase shift, a value" "a value, 1 pt on curve" "Asymptote point, 1 pt on curve" "Zero pt, 1 pt on curve" "Wavelength, 1pt on curve")
        kw 0
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions to division point, (pt close to origin -> higher y extensions)"
        dct 500
        dpt '(5.0 0.0)
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (cond
   ((eq kw 0)
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (vertical scaling)" 1.0)
          fb (get_real 2 "b (angle multiplier)" 1.0)
          fd (get_keyword 0 "Yes No" "Divide period by π? (if not, period = b*π )" "Yes")
          fb (if (eq fd "Yes") (* fb pi) 1.0)
          fc 0.0))
   ((eq kw 1)
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (vertical scaling)" 1.0)
          p1 (get_point 0 "Zero point" divpt basept)
          fd (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          fb (/ pi (* 2 fd))
          fc (- (/ pi 2.0))))
   ((eq kw 2)
    (setq p1 (get_point 0 "Point on asymptote" divpt basept)
          fa (get_real 2 "a (vertical scaling)" 1.0)
          fb (get_real 2 "b (angle multiplier)" 1.0)
          fd (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          fc (- (* fb fd) (/ pi 2.0))))
   ((eq kw 3)
    (setq p1 (get_point 0 "Point on asymptote" divpt basept)
          p2 (get_point 0 "Zero point" divpt basept)
          fa (get_real 2 "a (vertical scaling)" 1.0)
          fd (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          fc (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p2)
          fb (/ pi (* 2 (abs (- fc fd))))
          fc (- (* fb fd) (/ pi 2.0))))
   ((eq kw 4)
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (vertical scaling)" 1.0)
          fb (get_real 2 "b (angle multiplier)" 1.0)
          fc (* (get_real 2 "phase shift in degrees" 0.0) (/ pi 180))))
   ((eq kw 5)
    (setq p1 (get_point 0 "Curve point off x axis" divpt basept)
          fa (get_real 2 "a (vertical scaling)" 1.0)
          fd (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          fb (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          fc 0.0
          fe (atan (/ fb (avoid_zero fa)))
          fe (if (< fb 0.0) (+ pi fe) fe)
          fb (/ fe fd)))
   ((eq kw 6)
    (setq p1 (get_point 0 "Point on asymptote" divpt basept)
          p2 (get_point 0 "Curve point off x axis" divpt basept)
          fd (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          fe (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p2)
          ff (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p2)
          fa (get_real 2 "a (vertical scaling)" 1.0)
          fg (avoid_zero (- fe fd))
          fh (atan (/ (- fa) (avoid_zero ff)))
          fb (/ fh fg)
          fb (if (and (< fb 0.0) (> ff 0.0)) (/ (- fh pi) fg) fb)
          fb (if (and (< fb 0.0) (< ff 0.0)) (/ (+ fh pi) fg) fb)
          fc (- (* fb fd) (/ pi 2.0))))
   ((eq kw 7)
    (setq p1 (get_point 0 "Zero point" divpt basept)
          p2 (get_point 0 "Curve point off x axis" divpt basept)
          fa (get_real 2 "a (vertical scaling)" 1.0)
          fd (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          fe (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p2)
          ff (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p2)
          fg (avoid_zero (- fe fd))
          fh (atan (/ ff (avoid_zero fa)))
          fb (/ fh fg)
          fb (if (and (< fb 0.0) (< ff 0.0)) (/ (- fh pi) (avoid_zero fg)) fb)
          fb (if (and (< fb 0.0) (> ff 0.0)) (/ (+ fh pi) (avoid_zero fg)) fb)
          fc (* fb fd)))
   ((eq kw 8)
    (setq p1 (get_point 0 "Curve point off x axis" divpt basept)
          fd (get_real 2 "wavelength between zero crossings, will flip curve depending on curve pt" pi)
          fe (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          ff (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          fb (/ pi (avoid_zero fd))
          fa (* ff (cot (* fb fe)))
          fc 0.0)))
  (setq fe (rem fc pi)
        fe (if (> fe (/ pi 2.0)) (- fe pi) (if (<= fe (- (/ pi 2.0))) (+ fe pi) fe))
        fe (/ fe (avoid_zero fb))
        x0 (+ x0 (* fe cosangle))
        y0 (+ y0 (* fe sinangle))
        fc 0.0
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ",c=" (rtos fe 2 4))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 50.0)
        paramstep (/ xspan divcount)
        xc '(eval a2)
        yc '(* fa (tan (- (* fb (eval a2)) fc)))
        skm '(T T nil)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
