Quintic Serpentine Curve

A plane algebraic curve of degree five with an S-shaped profile that suggested the homemade name “right serpentine 2” on MathCurve.

Quintic Serpentine Curve polar equation:
\[ r^{2} = a^{2}\cot\!\left(\dfrac{\theta}{2}\right) \]
Equivalent Cartesian quintic:
\[ y(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2} - 2a^{2}x(x^{2}+y^{2}) - a^{4}y = 0 \]

History and Applications

The Quintic Serpentine Curve is a plane algebraic curve of degree five. It appears in Mathcurve.com1 under the name “right serpentine 2,” described there as a homemade name chosen for its snake-like shape. It is a special case of the focal circular cubic curve. No specific historical figure is credited with first studying or naming this exact polar form in the primary references consulted. It belongs to the general class of higher-degree algebraic curves explored after the foundational classifications of cubics in the late 17th and early 18th centuries.

No real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences were identified for this curve in available mathematical literature. General serpentine forms (typically cubics) are used in surveying for S-curve transitions in railways and roads, and certain nonholonomic mechanical trajectories such as those of the Chaplygin sleigh are also described as serpentine in the literature. None of these match the specific quintic polar equation \(r^{2}=a^{2}\cot(\theta/2)\) of the curve whose code was finalized above.

(defun C:QUINTICSERPENTINE (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt

  (setq id "Quintic Serpentine Curve"
        iq "r²=a²·cot(θ/2)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 180
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4))
        rval '(* fa (alt_expt (if (< (abs (cos (/ (eval a2) 2.0))) 1e-12) 0.0 (/ (cos (/ (eval a2) 2.0)) (avoid_zero (sin (/ (eval a2) 2.0))))) 0.5 nil))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" (* fa 20.0))
 ;       skm '(nil nil T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
