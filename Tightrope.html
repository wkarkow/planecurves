Tightrope Walker Curve

Locus of a tightrope walker’s feet on a weightless, frictionless rope fixed at one end, passed over a pulley at the same height, and held by a counterweight.

Tightrope Walker Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ y = -\frac{x(a-x)}{\sqrt{b^{2}-x^{2}}},\quad b = ka \]

History and Applications

The Tightrope Walker Curve (also known as the curve of the tightrope walker or danseur de corde) was studied and named by the French mathematician J.-B. Bérard in his 1810 work Opuscules mathématiques, contenant plusieurs méthodes nouvelles de construire l’équation aux sections coniques. In Problem XV, Bérard models the path traced by the feet of a tightrope dancer walking along a weightless, frictionless rope fixed at one end (B), passing over a small pulley (A) at the same height, and tensioned at the free end by a suspended counterweight (R).1,2 Bérard considers the dancer of mass \( m \) (represented by the vertical segment \( Mm \)) in equilibrium at a point M on the rope. The weight is resolved into two components, \( Ma \) and \( Mb \); the component \( Mb \) is balanced by the reaction at the fixed support B, while \( Ma \) must exactly equal the counterweight force \( r \) for equilibrium. Using similar triangles formed by the geometry (APM, MaK, BPM, and Mak) and substituting the lengths \( AP = x \), \( PM = y \), \( BP = a - x \), and \( AM = \sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}} \), Bérard eliminates the auxiliary segments and obtains the relation \( r = Ma = \dfrac{m(a-x)\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{ay} \). Solving for the ordinate then yields the explicit form of the locus \( y = \pm \dfrac{mx(a-x)}{\sqrt{a^{2}r^{2}-m^{2}(a-x)^{2}}} \).3 The shape of the curve depends on the mass ratio \( k = r/m \) (counterweight to dancer). When \( k < 1 \), the expression under the radical becomes imaginary near \( x = a \), so the curve possesses a vertical asymptote and the dancer cannot reach the pulley; when \( k \ge 1 \) the curve forms a continuous arc from the fixed end to the pulley. The special case \( k = 1 \) reduces to a right strophoid. It is amusing to note that in real life if \( k = 1 \), the final step would require nearly vertical ascent and is mechanically unattainable. As \( k \to \infty \) the curve approaches a kappa curve.3 Mathcurve presents an algebraically equivalent Cartesian form in which the horizontal coordinate is measured from the fixed support rather than from the pulley: \( y = \dfrac{x(a-x)}{\sqrt{b^{2}-x^{2}}} \), \( b = ka \), where \( a \) is the horizontal span and \( k \) is the mass ratio of counterweight to dancer (the negative sign in some implementations simply orients the curve below the x-axis).3

Applications in engineering and the natural sciences are limited. The curve serves primarily as a classic didactic example in classical mechanics and analytic geometry, illustrating the resolution of concurrent forces in static equilibrium, the use of similar triangles to derive a locus, and the effect of a movable load on cable geometry. It appears in historical treatments of conic sections and loci problems but has no documented widespread use in modern engineering design or natural-science modeling beyond its role as an illustrative statics exercise.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:TIGHTROPE (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                     divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc skm)
  (setq id "Tightrope Walker"
        iq "y = x·(a-x)/sqrt(b²-x²), b=k·a, k=(counterweight)/(man)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions in span"
        dct 500
        dpt nil
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (horizontal span)" 10.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "k (counterweight/man)" 1.0)
        fc (* fa fb)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", k=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fc 2 4))
        span fa
        paramstep (/ span divcount)
        xc '(eval a2)
        yc '(- (/ (* (eval a2) (- fa (eval a2))) (avoid_zero (sqrt (max 0.0 (- (* fc fc) (* (eval a2) (eval a2))))))))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:TIGHTROPE)
