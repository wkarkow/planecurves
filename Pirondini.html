Pirondini Pseudospiral

The plane curve whose curvature is proportional to the n-th power of arc length; a generalisation of the clothoid (n = 1).

Pirondini Pseudospiral parametric integral equations:
\[ x = a \int_{0}^{t} \cos\left( \frac{u^{n+1}}{n+1} \right) \, du , \quad y = a \int_{0}^{t} \sin\left( \frac{u^{n+1}}{n+1} \right) \, du \]
Intrinsic equation (defining property):
\[ s^{n} R_{c} = a^{n+1} \quad (n \neq -1,\ s = at) \]

History and Applications

The Pirondini pseudospiral1 (also called the pseudo-spiral of Pirondini) is the plane curve whose curvature is proportional to the \(n\)-th power of its arc length \(s\), where the real index \(n\) is different from \(-1\). It was examined by Victor Puiseux in a 1844 memoir2 on the evolutes and involutes of plane curves and later by Geminiano Pirondini (1857–1914) in papers of 1892 and 1905; the curve is named after the Italian mathematician. Pirondini, who taught analytic and descriptive geometry at the University of Parma, published extensively on surfaces of revolution, helicoids, and intrinsic properties of curves.

The defining relation may be written \(R_c = a^{n+1}/s^n\) (or equivalently \((n+1)\varphi=(s/a)^{n+1}\)), where \(R_c\) is the radius of curvature and \(\varphi\) is the tangential angle. Integration of the unit tangent then supplies the parametric form used in the AutoLISP code. Special values of the index recover familiar curves: \(n=1\) is the clothoid (Cornu spiral), \(n=0\) is a circle, \(n=-1\) is the logarithmic spiral (but is forbidden by these equations that would result in division by zero), \(n=-1/2\) is an involute of a circle, and \(n=-2\) is a limiting case of an alysoid. An important geometric property is that the evolute of a pseudospiral of index \(n\) is again a pseudospiral (of a related index).

No engineering or scientific applications have been found that are peculiar to a general index \(n\). The clothoid special case (\(n=1\)) is widely used for transition curves in highway and railway alignment, where curvature must change linearly with arc length in order to avoid abrupt changes in lateral acceleration. In contemporary computer-aided geometric design the broader family of curves with monotonic curvature (of which the pure-power pseudospirals form a subclass) appears in aesthetic-curve and fair-curve literature for shape modeling, but these uses rest on the monotonicity property rather than on the specific power law.3

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:PIRONDINI (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt
                      restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd fe ff fg fh ft)
  (setq id "Pirondini Pseudospiral"
        iq "x=a∫₀ᵗ cos(u^{n+1}/(n+1)) du, y=a∫₀ᵗ sin(u^{n+1}/(n+1)) du"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in t-span"
        dct 1440
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb -1.0
        fb (while (equal fb -1.0 1e-8) (setq fb (get_real 0 "n (index, ≠ -1)" 1.0)))
        fc (+ fb 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", n=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        fd 0.0
        fe 0.0
        ff 0.0
        xc 'fe
        yc 'ff
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0.0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(progn (setq fg fd fd (+ fd paramstep) fh (+ fg (* 0.5 paramstep))) (setq ft (/ (alt_expt fh fc nil) (avoid_zero fc)) fe
            (+ fe (* fa (cos ft) paramstep)) ff (+ ff (* fa (sin ft) paramstep))) fd)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
