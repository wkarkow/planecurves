Doppler Spiral

Orthogonal projection of a point in uniform Archimedean spiral motion while its plane translates uniformly.

Doppler Spiral parametric equations:
\[ x(t) = a \cdot t \cdot (\cos(t) + k),\quad y(t) = a \cdot t \cdot \sin(t) \]

History and Applications

The Doppler Spiral is a plane curve that represents the orthogonal projection onto a fixed plane of a point undergoing uniform spiral motion while the plane itself undergoes uniform linear translation.1,2,3 It is mathematically obtained by taking an Archimedean spiral and translating it horizontally with constant speed proportional to the parameter k. When the translation coefficient k = 0, the curve reduces exactly to the Archimedean spiral. The Doppler spiral is equivalently the two-dimensional projection of the conical Pappus spiral. The name was given by Alexander Heinz and arises from the analogy with the Doppler effect for a wave source in relative motion to an observer. Pappus of Alexandria (4th century) studied the three-dimensional conical spiral whose projection yields this curve. The underlying Archimedean spiral was originally investigated by Archimedes (c. 287–212 BC) in his work On Spirals.

Real-life applications specifically of the Doppler spiral curve are not documented in engineering or the natural sciences literature. It remains a mathematical construct used to illustrate kinematics of combined uniform rotation and linear translation.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:DOPPLERSPIRAL (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt
                          xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc)
  (setq id "Doppler Spiral"
        iq "x=a·t·(cos(t)+k), y=a·t·sin(t)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "k (x-drift factor)" 0.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", k=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        xc '(* fa (eval a2) (+ (cos (eval a2)) fb))
        yc '(* fa (eval a2) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T nil)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:DOPPLERSPIRAL)
