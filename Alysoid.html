Alysoid Curve

A parametric curve generalizing the catenary, defined by differential equations from its Whewell intrinsic form; reduces to catenary when k=1 and plotted via stateful numerical integration.

Alysoid Curve differential equations:
\[ \frac{dx}{dt} = a k^{2} \frac{\cos(t)}{\cos^{2}(k t)}, \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = a k^{2} \frac{\sin(t)}{\cos^{2}(k t)} \]
Integration domain (stateful midpoint):
\[ t \in \left[0, \frac{\pi}{2k} - \epsilon\right] \quad (k = b/a > 0) \]

History and Applications

The alysoid was first studied by Italian mathematician Ernesto Cesàro in 1886, as documented in Nouvelles Annales de Mathématiques (p. 75)1. Cesàro (1859–1906) contributed to differential geometry and is known for Cesàro summation in series convergence, but his work on the alysoid focused on its intrinsic properties, such as the Whewell equation \( s = b \cdot \tan(k \cdot \phi) \), where \( s \) is arc length, \( \phi \) is the tangential angle, and \( k = b/a \). The name derives from the Greek "alusion," meaning "little chain," reflecting its resemblance to a hanging chain generalized beyond the standard catenary1.

When \( a = b \) (\( k = 1 \)), the alysoid reduces to the catenary, the curve formed by a uniform chain under gravity, with equation \( y = a \cdot \cosh(x/a) \)1. For \( a = 2b \) (\( k = 1/2 \)), it has a specific parametrization involving integrals of secant functions1. If \( b \) approaches 0, it becomes the antiloga or Pirondini pseudo-spiral2.

Real-life applications center on the special catenary case: suspended cables in power lines and suspension bridges (e.g., Golden Gate Bridge cables follow catenary shapes for load distribution); catenary arches in architecture, like Antoni Gaudí's designs in Casa Milà for structural stability without tension; and steel catenary risers in offshore oil platforms for flexible seabed connections resisting waves and currents. No distinct applications for the general alysoid (\( k \neq 1 \)) were found beyond these.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:ALYSOID (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt xspan restartflag
                    id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd fe ff fg fh fi fj fk fl fm)
  (setq id "Alysoid Curve"
        iq "dx/dt=a·k²·cos(t)/cos²(kt), dy/dt=a·k²·sin(t)/cos²(kt), k=b/a"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw 0
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 500
        dpt nil
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "k (shape ratio)" 1.0)
        fc (/ pi (* 2.0 (abs fb)))
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", k=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        paramstep (/ (* 0.999999 fc) (float (max 1 (1- divcount))))
        fd 0.0
        fe 0.0
        ff 0.0
        fg 1
        xc 'fe
        yc 'ff
        skm '(nil T T)
        a1 0.0
        a2 '(progn
              (if (= fg 1)
                (progn (setq fg 0) fd)
                (progn
                  (setq fh fd
                        fi (cos (* fb fh))
                        fj (avoid_zero (* fi fi))
                        fk (+ fh paramstep)
                        fl (cos (* fb fk))
                        fm (avoid_zero (* fl fl))
                        fe (+ fe (* 0.5 paramstep (+ (* fa fb fb (/ (cos fh) fj)) (* fa fb fb (/ (cos fk) fm)))))
                        ff (+ ff (* 0.5 paramstep (+ (* fa fb fb (/ (sin fh) fj)) (* fa fb fb (/ (sin fk) fm)))))
                        fd fk)
                  fd)))
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
