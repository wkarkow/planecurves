Focal Circular Cubic (Van Rees)

A special circular cubic that passes through its singular focus F; the locus of the foci of all conics inscribed in a given quadrilateral.

Focal Circular Cubic Cartesian equation:
\[ (x-2a)(x^{2}+y^{2})+b^{2}(\cos(2\alpha)\,x+\sin(2\alpha)\,y)=0 \]
Focal Circular Cubic polar equation:
\[ r=\dfrac{a\pm\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2}\cos\theta\cos(\theta-2\alpha)}}{\cos\theta} \]

History and Applications

The focal circular cubic (Van Rees)1,2,3, also called the focal of Van Rees, isoptic cubic, or Apollonius cubic, is a special circular cubic that passes through its singular focus F (the intersection of the tangents to the curve at the two circular points at infinity). It was first studied by G. Van Rees in 1829, then independently by J. Steiner in 1852, and later by É. Laguerre in 1868. Gomes Teixeira noted the equivalence of the Van Rees and Steiner results in 1915. The name “circular cubic with an axis” was introduced in 2002 by Dominique Roux and Michel Tixier. Geometrically it is the locus of the foci of all conics inscribed in a given quadrilateral (i.e., tangent to its four sides). It is also the isoptic curve consisting of points from which two directed line segments subtend equal angles. Van Rees himself gave an elegant three-dimensional construction: the curve is the locus of the foci of conics tangent to a fixed quadrilateral. With the singular focus at the origin and the axis of symmetry along the x-axis, the Cartesian form below is obtained; the line x = 2a is the vertical asymptote.

No real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences appear in the primary references. The curve remains a purely theoretical object in classical geometry.
