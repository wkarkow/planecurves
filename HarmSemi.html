Harmonic Semiweak Damping

Generalization of the critically damped free harmonic oscillator response: the product of a power-law term and exponential decay that belongs to the Gamma/Erlang family.

Harmonic Semiweak Damping Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ y = A (x - x_{0})^{n-1} e^{-\kappa (x - x_{0})} \]

History and Applications

The Harmonic Semiweak Damping (generalized Gamma form) curve is a generalization of the semi-weak damping case \( y(x) = x \cdot e^{-x} \) in the free damped harmonic oscillator,1 matching the critically damped solution to the second-order linear differential equation \( y'' + a \cdot y' + b \cdot y = 0 \) with appropriate initial conditions, where the response is the product of a linear term and an exponential decay.2 This case sits at the boundary between underdamped and overdamped non-oscillatory motion, and is a special case of the more general Gamma/Erlang family, where \( A \) is a scale factor, \( x_{0} \) a time shift, \( n \) a shape parameter, and \( \kappa \) a damping rate. The case \( n = 2 \), \( A = 1 \), \( x_{0} = 0 \), \( \kappa = 1 \) reduces to the semiweak damping curve above. Larger values of \( n \) yield progressively delayed and broadened pulses, while \( n = 1 \) recovers the simple exponential decay. For statisticians, the canonical Erlang probability density function uses \( \frac{\kappa^{n}}{(n-1)!} \) to normalize the area under the curve to one; here the scale parameter \( A \) is left free, since normalization is not required in physical damping applications. The Erlang distribution uses integer \( n \), but the Gamma distribution extends it to real \( n \).

Real-life applications appear in systems that must return to equilibrium as quickly as possible without oscillation. Automotive shock absorbers tune near critical damping to control body motion over road irregularities. Door closers and cabinet hardware use the same principle for smooth, rapid closure. Analog instrument pointers (scales, galvanometers, tachometers) employ critical damping so the needle settles to the true reading without overshoot or ringing. Equivalent behavior governs critically damped RLC circuits in electrical measurement and control equipment.3 In chemical kinetics and radioactive decay chains it models the concentration or probability density of the nth intermediate in a sequence of consecutive first-order reactions or decays with equal rate constants \( \kappa \), where the output of one stage feeds the next with exponential waiting times.4

(defun C:HARMSEMI (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt xspan

  (setq id "Harmonic Semiweak Damping (generalized Gamma form)"
        iq "y=A·(x-x₀)^{n-1}·e^{-κ·(x-x₀)}"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions to division point"
        dct 1000
        dpt '(10.0 0.0)
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "A (scale)" 10.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "x₀ (shift)" 0.0)
        fc (get_real 2 "n (shape exponent)" 2.0)
        fd (get_real 6 "κ (damping rate)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\nA=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", x₀=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", n=" (rtos fc 2 4) ", κ=" (rtos fd 2 4))
        xc '(eval a2)
        yc '(* fa (alt_expt (- (eval a2) fb) (- fc 1.0) nil) (exp (* -1.0 fd (- (eval a2) fb))))
        paramstep (/ xspan divcount)
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
