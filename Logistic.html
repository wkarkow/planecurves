Logistic Curve

The sigmoid solution of Verhulst’s constrained-growth equation involving carrying capacity, growth rate and midpoint.

Logistic Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ y = \dfrac{L}{1+e^{-k(x-x_{0})}} \]

History and Applications

The logistic growth curve was introduced in 1838 by the Belgian mathematician and astronomer Pierre-François Verhulst1,2 while studying population dynamics. Verhulst was dissatisfied with the purely exponential growth models of his time, which predicted unbounded population increase. He proposed a modified differential equation that incorporated a carrying capacity, representing environmental limits such as food supply, space, or resources. In modern notation, Verhulst’s equation is \(\frac{dy}{dx} = ky\bigl(1 - \frac{y}{L}\bigr)\), whose closed-form solution is the logistic function \( y(x) = \frac{L}{1+e^{-k(x-x_{0})}} \). This formulation introduces three concepts that were novel at the time: initial exponential growth when \( y \ll L \), self-limiting behavior as \( y \) approaches \( L \), and a well-defined inflection point at \( y = L/2 \), where the growth rate is maximal.

Although originally developed for population studies, the logistic curve proved useful well beyond biology. In engineering the positive logistic is a smooth saturation curve that models actuator saturation and soft limits without hard clipping, approximates transistor transfer curves and amplifier soft-limiting behavior, describes strain hardening of materials where deformation accelerates initially but asymptotically approaches a limit, models autocatalytic chemical reactions and diffusion-limited processes, and describes technology diffusion, product adoption, and infrastructure rollout where growth slows as capacity is reached. From a CAD and geometric standpoint the positive logistic is monotonic, smooth, and curvature-continuous, has a single controllable inflection, and approaches its asymptotes without discontinuities. Those properties make it useful as a design curve rather than merely a data-fit curve.

The same mathematics applies when the growth-rate parameter \( k \) is negative. The solution then represents decay toward extinction rather than growth toward saturation, and the asymptote \( y = 0 \) becomes the stable state. Historically this “negative logistic” was not emphasized early on because most nineteenth-century studies focused on population growth. By the mid-twentieth century it was clear that inhibitory, damping, and decay processes obey the same structure. It is used where an initial rapid decline transitions into long-tail decay, such as decreasing product failure rates after burn-in or conditioning, aerodynamic tapering, and inhibitory biological and chemical responses.

(defun C:LOGISTIC (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xspan restartflag
                     paramstep id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd fe ff p1 p2)
  (setq id "Logistic Growth Curve"
        iq "y=L/(1+e^{-k·(x-x₀)})"
        dcl "LOGISTIC"
        opt '("Known values" "Unknown growth rate" "Unknown growth & midpoint")
        kw 0
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions to division point"
        dct 500
        dpt '(5.0 0.0)
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        paramstep (/ xspan divcount))
  (cond
   ((eq kw 0)
    (setq fa (get_real 6 "L (carrying capacity): " 1.0)
          fb (get_real 2 "k (growth rate): " 1.0)
          fc (get_real 2 "x₀ (midpoint location): " 0.0)
          paramstep (if (eq (get_keyword 0 "Positive Negative" "Positive or Negative direction of curve?" "Positive") "Negative") (- paramstep) paramstep)))
   ((eq kw 1)
    (setq fa (get_real 6 "L (carrying capacity): " 1.0)
          fc (get_real 2 "x₀ (midpoint location): " 0.0)
          fd nil)
    (while (not fd)
      (setq p1 (get_boundpt "Curve point" nil nil basept nil nil nil 0.0 fa nil)
            x1 (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
            y1 (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p1))
      (if (<= (abs (- x1 fc)) 1e-9) (prompt "Cannot solve k from a point with x = x₀ (midpoint). Pick another point.") (setq fd T)))
    (setq fb (- (/ (log (- (/ fa y1) 1.0)) (- x1 fc)))
          paramstep (if (eq (get_keyword 0 "Positive Negative" "Positive or Negative direction of curve?" "Positive") "Negative") (- paramstep) paramstep)))
   ((eq kw 2)
    (setq fa (get_real 6 "L (carrying capacity): " 1.0)
          paramstep (if (eq (get_keyword 0 "Positive Negative" "Positive or Negative direction of curve?" "Positive") "Negative") (- paramstep) paramstep)
          fd nil)
    (while (not fd)
      (setq p1 (get_boundpt "First curve point" nil nil basept nil nil nil 0.0 fa nil)
            x1 (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
            y1 (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p1)
            p2 (get_boundpt "Second curve point" nil nil basept nil nil nil 0.0 fa nil)
            x2 (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p2)
            y2 (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p2))
      (cond
        ((<= (abs (- x2 x1)) 1e-9)
         (prompt "\nSecond point must have different x from first point; pick again."))
        (T
         (setq fe (log (- (/ fa y1) 1.0))
               ff (log (- (/ fa y2) 1.0)))
         (if (<= (abs (- fe ff)) 1e-9)
           (prompt "\nPoints lead to k ≈ 0 (almost flat logistic); pick different points.")
           (setq fb (- (/ (- fe ff) (- x1 x2)))
                 fc (+ x1 (/ fe fb))
                 fd T)))))))
  (setq eq_display (strcat "L=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", k=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", x₀=" (rtos fc 2 4))
        xc '(eval a2)
        yc '(/ fa (+ 1.0 (exp (* -1.0 fb (- (eval a2) fc)))))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
