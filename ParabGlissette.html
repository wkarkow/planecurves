Parabola Glissette

The path of a fixed point attached to a parabola that slides without rolling against fixed guides (a line and a point).

Parabola Glissette implicit equation:
\[ (x^{2} + y^{2} + 3a^{2})x^{2}y^{2} = a^{6} \]
Parabola Glissette parametric equations:
\[ x = a\dfrac{\cos^{2}(t)}{\sin(t)},\quad y = a\dfrac{\sin^{2}(t)}{\cos(t)} \]

History and Applications

The Parabola Glissette1,2 is a specific glissette generated by the constrained sliding motion of a parabola (or a line segment attached to it) while maintaining contact with fixed bases such as a straight line and a point. A glissette is the path traced by a fixed point attached to a moving curve that slides (glides) without rolling between two fixed curves or guides; this differs from a roulette, which involves rolling contact. Such loci were known in elementary form to ancient Greek geometers through constructions like the trammel of Archimedes, but systematic mathematical treatment of glissettes began in the nineteenth century with Belgian-French mathematician Eugène Charles Catalan in 1865 and English mathematician William Henry Besant in his 1869 Notes on Roulettes.3 The given parametric equations describe the trajectory for the configuration in which one base is a straight line and the sliding element is related to a parabola. Many glissette variants involving a parabola (such as the locus of its focus or vertex under fixed-point constraints) produce well-known curves like the Kampyle of Eudoxus, cissoid of Diocles, or cardioid, illustrating how these motions convert simple sliding into algebraic paths useful for geometric analysis.

In engineering and the natural sciences, glissettes appear in the kinematic analysis and synthesis of mechanisms. They model the exact paths traced by points in sliding linkages and constrained devices, supporting the design of slider-crank systems and other automation components that require precise trajectory generation under linear guides. Their study aids mechanical engineers in linkage optimization for instruments and machinery where sliding constraints define motion envelopes.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:PARABGLISSETTE (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                           divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa maxdraw)		
  (setq id "Parabola Glissette"
        iq "x=a·cos²(t)/sin(t),y=a·sin²(t)/cos(t)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4))
        xc '(/ (* fa (alt_expt (cos (eval a2)) 2.0 nil)) (avoid_zero (sin (eval a2))))
        yc '(/ (* fa (alt_expt (sin (eval a2)) 2.0 nil)) (avoid_zero (cos (eval a2))))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 8.0)
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
