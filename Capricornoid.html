Capricornoid

A quartic algebraic plane curve resembling the horns of a Capricorn goat.

Capricornoid implicit equation:
\[ a^{2} x^{2} (x^{2} + y^{2}) - b (a y - x^{2} - y^{2})^{2} = 0 \]
Capricornoid polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta + a} \quad (a > 1) \]

History and Applications

The Capricornoid1,2,3 is a quartic plane curve named for its resemblance to the horns of a male Capricorn goat or the zodiac symbol for Capricorn. It appears in collections of special algebraic curves and is documented in handbooks such as E. V. Shikin’s Handbook and Atlas of Curves (1995). The curve features a tacnode at the origin (where two branches touch with the same tangent direction) and a crunode at (0, a) for parameter a > 0 (where branches cross)1.

Real-life applications are found in computational geometry and mechanical engineering. The Capricornoid serves as a benchmark test case for algorithms that approximate implicit algebraic curves on triangulated meshes, supporting adaptive refinement methods used in surface modeling and numerical simulation4. In kinematics, the curve (sometimes referred to as Poncelet’s Capricornoid) is generated by Artobolevskii linkage mechanisms for analysis and design of mechanical systems that trace specific paths5.

  (setq id "Capricornoid"
        iq "r = sin(θ)/(cos(θ)+a)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 0 "a (controls pole location and overall width; values >1 avoid vertical asymptotes)" 2.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 10.0)
        rval '(* (sin (eval a2)) (/ 1.0 (avoid_zero (+ (cos (eval a2)) fa))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
