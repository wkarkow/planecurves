Bullet Nose Curve

A unicursal quartic plane curve with pointed tips resembling a bullet nose.

Bullet Nose Curve implicit equation:
\[ a^{2} y^{2} - b^{2} x^{2} = x^{2} y^{2} \]
Bullet Nose Curve parametric equations:
\[ x = a \cos t,\quad y = b \cot t \]
Generalized parametric equations:
\[ x = a \left[ \cos (b t - c) \right]^{d},\quad y = e \left[ \cot (f t - g) \right]^{h} \]

History and Applications

The bullet-nose curve1, named for the pointed tips that resemble the nose of a bullet, is a unicursal quartic plane curve2 first studied by Dutch mathematician Pieter Hendrik Schoute (1846–1923) in 18833. Schoute later became professor of mathematics at the University of Groningen and contributed to the geometry of polytopes and higher-dimensional figures. He described the curve geometrically as the image of a rectangular hyperbola under biaxial inversion with respect to the coordinate axes of the hyperbola; it is also the locus of the points where tangents to that hyperbola intersect the axes. The curve is a rational quartic of genus zero with three inflection points and three double points in the real projective plane.

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences were not identified in standard mathematical references. The curve appears primarily in classical algebraic geometry and calculus textbooks as an example for parametric equations and tangent-line computations.
