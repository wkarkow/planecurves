Involute of the Exponential Curve

An involute of the exponential curve; the path of the free end of a taut string unwound from \( y = a e^{x/a} \).

Exponential Involute parametric equations:
\[ x = a\left(t - \frac{I}{\sqrt{1+e^{2t}}}\right),\quad y = a e^{t}\left(1 - \frac{I}{\sqrt{1+e^{2t}}}\right) \quad \text{where} \quad I = \int_{t_0}^{t}\sqrt{1+e^{2s}}\,ds \]
Closed-form integral:
\[ I = \bigl[\sqrt{1+e^{2t}} - \ln(1+\sqrt{1+e^{2t}}) + t\bigr] - \bigl[\text{same at }t_0\bigr] \]
Asymptote of superior branch:
\[ y_{\text{asymp}} = a\bigl(\sqrt{1+e^{2t_0}} + \ln(\sqrt{1+e^{2t_0}}-1)\bigr) \]
Stopping point of inferior branch:
\[ x_{\text{stop}} = a\bigl(\sqrt{1+e^{2t_0}} + \ln(\sqrt{1+e^{2t_0}}-1) - t_0 + \ln 2\bigr) \]

History and Applications

The curve is presented on MathCurve under the homemade English heading “Involute of the exponential curve” (French: développante de l’exponentielle). No earlier named appearance in the classical literature of special curves has been identified. The general notions of involute and evolute were introduced by Christiaan Huygens in 1673 in connection with the cycloidal pendulum; an involute is the path traced by the free end of a taut inextensible string as it is unwound from a given curve (the evolute of that involute). The parent exponential curve, \( x = a t \), \( y = a e^{t} \) is smooth for all real \( t \). Attachment may therefore be made at any point. The inferior branch (parameter decreasing from the contact value) approaches a finite stopping point on or near the positive \( x \)-axis; the superior branch (parameter increasing) tends to a horizontal asymptote.

No engineering or scientific applications peculiar to this particular involute have been recorded. Involutes in general appear in the design of gear-tooth profiles (constant pressure angle), in cam mechanisms, and in the study of parallel curves and caustics, but those uses rely on the involute of a circle or of other standard profiles rather than on the involute of the exponential curve.
