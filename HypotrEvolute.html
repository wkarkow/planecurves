Hypotrochoid Evolute

The locus of the centers of curvature of a hypotrochoid; the envelope of its normals.

Hypotrochoid Evolute parametric equations:
\[ x = (R-r)\cos\theta + d\cos\left(\frac{R-r}{r}\theta\right) - \dfrac{r\bigl(r^{2}-2rd\cos(\frac{R}{r}\theta)+d^{2}\bigr)}{r^{3}+(r-R)d^{2}+rd(R-2r)\cos(\frac{R}{r}\theta)} \left[(R-r)\cos\theta - \frac{R-r}{r}\,d\cos\left(\frac{R-r}{r}\theta\right)\right] \]
\[ y = (R-r)\sin\theta - d\sin\left(\frac{R-r}{r}\theta\right) - \dfrac{r\bigl(r^{2}-2rd\cos(\frac{R}{r}\theta)+d^{2}\bigr)}{r^{3}+(r-R)d^{2}+rd(R-2r)\cos(\frac{R}{r}\theta)} \left[(R-r)\sin\theta + \frac{R-r}{r}\,d\sin\left(\frac{R-r}{r}\theta\right)\right] \]

History and Applications

The evolute of a hypotrochoid1 is the locus of the centers of curvature of the hypotrochoid curve. The general theory of evolutes was first examined by Apollonius of Perga around 200 BC and later developed by Christiaan Huygens in 1673 in connection with the design of pendulum clocks and the study of caustics in optics2. Hypotrochoids themselves are roulette curves traced by a point attached to a circle of radius r that rolls without slipping inside a fixed circle of radius R, with the tracing point at a distance d from the center of the rolling circle.3,4 These curves were investigated in the 17th century by mathematicians including Girard Desargues, Philippe de La Hire, Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, and Isaac Newton, who analyzed properties of rolling circles and cycloidal motions.3 For the special case of a hypocycloid (when d = r), the evolute is another similar hypocycloid, scaled and rotated.4 The general hypotrochoid evolute, however, leads to a considerably more complicated parametric expression.1

No prominent real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences are documented specifically for the evolute of a general hypotrochoid. The parent hypotrochoid curves are used in mechanical engineering, for example in the rotor housing profile of the Wankel rotary engine and in gerotor pumps and cycloidal gear designs.3,4
