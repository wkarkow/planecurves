2D Basin Curve

Projection of a 3D basin traced on a paraboloid of revolution; associated with Lissajous curves.

2D Basin Curve parametric equations:
\[ x = a \cdot \cos(t + \phi) \cdot \cos(n t),\quad y = b \cdot \cos^{2}(n t) \]

History and Applications

The 2D basin curve1,2 (French: vasque 2D; also Brunnenbecken 2D) is a homemade name introduced by Robert Ferréol on the MathCurve site (French page 20113, English translation 2017). It is not a classical named curve associated with an earlier mathematician. The name refers to the basin-like shape obtained as the orthogonal projection, onto any plane containing the vertical Oz axis, of a 3D basin curve traced on a paraboloid of revolution. Geometrically, the parent 3D basin is the image of a cylindrical sine wave under the map that sends a point to the corresponding point on the paraboloid \( z = (x^{2} + y^{2})/(2a) \); equivalently, it is the central projection from the origin of that cylindrical wave onto the paraboloid. The projection of the same 3D curve onto the horizontal xOy plane is a rose curve. For the special case \( n = 1 \) the 3D basin reduces to an ellipse lying in a plane through the origin.

The parametric equations show that every 2D basin lies between the parabola \( y = b \) (more precisely, the curve is traced on or between a parabola of that form and the horizontal line \( y = b \)). Special algebraic cases appear for integer or half-integer frequencies: when \( n = 2 \) and \( \phi = 0 \) the curve is a rational quartic; when \( n = 3 \) and \( \phi = \pi/2 \) it is a rational sextic. Each 2D basin is associated with a Lissajous curve of the same frequency parameter; the two curves share the underlying cylindrical-sine-wave construction and can be displayed together for comparison.

No documented applications in engineering or the natural sciences have been found for this parametric family. The curve remains a modern geometric construction catalogued for its relation to the paraboloid projection, the cylindrical sine wave, roses, and Lissajous figures.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:2DBASIN (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                    divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd)
  (setq id "2D Basin Curve"
        iq "x=a·cos(t+φ)·cos(n·t), y=b·cos²(n·t)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt '(5.0 0.0)
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 6 "a (x-scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 6 "b (y-scale)" 1.0)
        fc (get_real 6 "n (frequency)" 2.0)
        fd (get_real 0 "φ (phase in °)" 0.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4)
                          " b=" (rtos fb 2 4)
                          " n=" (rtos fc 2 4)
                          " phi=" (rtos fd 2 4))
        xc '(* fa (cos (+ (eval a2) (* fd (/ pi 180.0)))) (cos (* fc (eval a2))))
        yc '(* fb (expt (cos (* fc (eval a2))) 2))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:2DBASIN)
