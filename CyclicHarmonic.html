Cyclic Harmonic Curve

A family of polar curves given by r = a + b·cos(nθ), producing rose-like or limaçon-derived patterns.

Cyclic Harmonic Curve polar equation:
\[ r = a + b \cdot \cos(n \theta) \]

History and Applications

Cyclic-harmonic curves, also known as Moritz curves, geometric petals, botanical curves, conchoids of roses, or circular sinusoids,1 were systematically studied by the American mathematician Robert E. Moritz (1868–1940) beginning in 1917. Moritz published “The General Theory of Cyclic-Harmonic Curves” in the Annals of Mathematics in 19212 and a comprehensive book on the subject in 1923. He constructed a mechanical linkage device to physically generate these curves for different values of the parameters. These curves are mathematically the conchoids of rose curves taken with respect to the pole and can also be obtained as the image of Pascal’s limaçon under the Brocard transformation.1

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences are limited. When n = 1 the equation reduces to the classical limaçon, which is used in the design of limaçon rotary machines such as pumps, compressors, and expanders for power generation and fluid processing systems.3 For general rational n = p/q the curves correspond to projections of certain Turk’s head knots and represent toroidal solenoids viewed from above, components used in electromagnetism for generating uniform magnetic fields within their core.1

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:CYCLICHARMONIC (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                           restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc)
  (setq id "Cyclic Harmonic Curve"
        iq "r=a+b·cos(nθ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 0 "a (radial offset)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "b (cos amplitude)" 0.25)
        fc (get_real 2 "n (theta multiplier)" 8.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", n=" (rtos fc 2 4))
        rval '(+ fa (* fb (cos (* fc (eval a2)))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:CYCLICHARMONIC)
