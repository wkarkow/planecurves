Quadratrix of Hippias

A transcendental curve generated by two uniform motions inside a square of side 2a: a rotating radius and a translating line.

Quadratrix of Hippias Cartesian equation:
\[ x = y \cdot \cot\left(\dfrac{\pi y}{2a}\right) \]
Quadratrix of Hippias polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{2a\theta}{\pi\sin\theta} \]
Quadratrix of Hippias parametric equations:
\[ x = \dfrac{2a\cdot t\cdot\cot(t)}{\pi},\quad y = \dfrac{2a\cdot t}{\pi} \]

History and Applications

The Quadratrix of Hippias1,2,3, also known as the trisectrix of Hippias or quadratrix of Dinostratus, is a transcendental plane curve discovered around 420 BC by the Greek sophist Hippias of Elis. Hippias devised the curve to trisect an arbitrary angle or, more generally, to divide any angle into n equal parts. About 70 years later, Dinostratus used it to square the circle, which accounts for the alternative name “quadratrix.” The curve arises from the kinematic intersection of two uniform motions within a square of side length 2a: one line segment rotates at constant angular speed from the positive x-axis through a right angle about one endpoint, while a second line segment translates downward at constant linear speed across the square’s vertical side during exactly the same time interval. This equal-time proportionality converts angular division directly into linear division along the vertical, a concept accessible through basic trigonometry: the cotangent term emerges from the ratio of the adjacent side (remaining horizontal projection) to the opposite side (vertical displacement) at parameter t. Greek mathematicians including Proclus, Pappus of Alexandria, and Nicomedes later discussed its properties; Isaac Newton evaluated the area beneath the curve using trigonometric series.

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences remain undocumented beyond the curve’s original role in classical geometry.

(defun C:HIPPIAS (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                    divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 maxdraw fa fb fc)
  (setq id "Quadratrix of Hippias"
        iq "x=(2a/π)·t·cot(t), y=(2a/π)·t"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions per band"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 180
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 6 "2a (vertical spacing between bands)" 2.0)
        fb (get_integer 6 "N bands" 4)
        fc (/ fa pi)
        eq_display (strcat "\n2a=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", N=" (itoa fb))
        xc '(if (< (abs (eval a2)) 1e-9) fc (* fc (eval a2) (/ (cos (eval a2)) (avoid_zero (sin (eval a2))))))
        yc '(* fc (eval a2))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 1e3)
        divcount (1+ (* divcount fb))
        skm '(T nil T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
