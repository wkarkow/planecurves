Kappa Curve

A classical rational quartic plane curve amounting to a radial cotangent.

Kappa Curve implicit equation:
\[ (x^{2} + y^{2})y^{2} = a^{2}x^{2} \]
Kappa Curve polar equation:
\[ r = a \cdot \cot(\theta) \]
Generalized polar equation:
\[ r = a \cdot \cot^{b}(c\theta) \]

History and Applications

The Kappa curve1,2,3,4, also known as Gutschoven’s curve, was first studied by Gérard van Gutschoven around 1662. It is remembered as one of the early examples in which Isaac Barrow applied rudimentary methods of the developing calculus to determine the tangent to a curve. The curve was subsequently studied by Isaac Newton and Johann Bernoulli, as well as by René François de Sluze. Its Greek-letter name comes from its resemblance to a handwritten κ when plotted in Cartesian coordinates. Geometrically, the classical form is the locus of a point P such that the distance from the origin to P equals the length of a segment cut by a rotating line through the origin intersecting a fixed horizontal line at a constant distance a from the origin.2 It is a rational quartic algebraic curve and the radial curve of the tractrix; it is also a special case of a nodal curve. The AutoLISP code generalizes the classical polar equation to the powered and scaled form shown above, allowing exploration of variants by varying the exponent b and multiplier c.

No prominent real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences were identified for the Kappa curve in standard mathematical references. Its significance remains primarily historical and pedagogical in the development of algebraic curves and early calculus.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:KAPPA (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                  restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span rval xc yc skm maxdraw a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 eq_display fa fb fc)
  (setq id "Kappa Curve"
        iq "r=a·cotᵇ(cθ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "b (exponent)" 1.0)
        fc (get_real 2 "c (θ multiplier)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", c=" (rtos fc 2 4))
        rval '(* fa (alt_expt (/ (cos (* fc (eval a2))) (avoid_zero (sin (* fc (eval a2))))) fb nil))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 50)
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:KAPPA)
