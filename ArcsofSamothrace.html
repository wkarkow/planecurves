Arcs of Samothrace

A rare modern sextic algebraic curve with distinctive arc-like lobes, first catalogued in online mathematical curve collections; extends to generalized sine and tangent polar families.

Arcs of Samothrace sine polar equation:
\[ r = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta (3 \cos^{2} \theta - \sin^{2} \theta)} \]
Arcs of Samothrace tangent polar equation:
\[ r = \frac{\tan \theta}{1 + 2 \cos 2\theta} \]
Generalized sine polar equation:
\[ r = \frac{a \sin^{b} \theta}{c \cos^{d} \theta \left( e \cos^{f} \theta - g \sin^{h} \theta \right)^{i}} \]
Generalized tangent polar equation:
\[ r = \frac{\tan (a \theta)}{b + c \cos (d \theta)} \]

History and Applications

The Arcs of Samothrace1 is a sextic algebraic curve named after the Greek island of Samothrace in the northern Aegean Sea. The island was the site of an important ancient sanctuary dedicated to the Great Gods and is best known as the discovery location in 1863 of the Hellenistic sculpture the Winged Victory of Samothrace (Nike of Samothrace), carved around 190 BC and now in the Louvre Museum in Paris. Unlike classical curves with roots in ancient mathematics, the Arcs of Samothrace has no known connection to antiquity or classical authors. The curve and its equations do not appear in historical mathematical texts, MathWorld, mathcurve.com, or any other major reference. It was first documented in the online collection of two-dimensional curves at 2dcurves.com. The dedicated page1 was last updated August 1, 2003, where the curve is presented as “a rather rare curve to find in math” with a form somewhat resembling a hyperbola although it is algebraically distinct. No earlier publication of the specific equation or the name “Arcs of Samothrace” has been located in any source. Consequently, the precise mathematician or exact date of first derivation remains unidentified; the curve appears to be a modern construction catalogued in this digital resource. The site also notes that the curve can be extended to a generalized family.

The two equivalent polar forms and the parameterized generalizations in sine and tangent styles were developed around the same period2 or shortly afterward to make plotting and exploration straightforward in polar coordinates.

No real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences have been documented for this curve in the available literature.

(defun C:ARCSOFSAMOTHRACE (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                           divpt restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd
                           fe ff fg fh fi rval)
  (setq id "Arcs of Samothrace"
        iq "r=a·sinᵇθ / [c·cosᵈθ·(e·cosᶠθ−g·sinʰθ)ⁱ] or r=tan(aθ)/(b+c·cos(dθ))"
        dcl "ARCSOFSAMOTHRACE"
        opt '("Sine form" "Tan form")
        kw 0
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (cond
    ((= kw 0)
     (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (overall scale)" 1.0)
           fb (get_real 2 "b (power of sin θ)" 1.0)
           fc (get_real 2 "c (scale of first cos term)" 1.0)
           fd (get_real 2 "d (power of first cos θ)" 1.0)
           fe (get_real 2 "e (coeff inside parenthesis)" 3.0)
           ff (get_real 2 "f (power of inner cos)" 2.0)
           fg (get_real 2 "g (coeff of inner sin)" 1.0)
           fh (get_real 2 "h (power of inner sin)" 2.0)
           fi (get_real 2 "i (power of parenthesis)" 1.0)
           eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ",b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ",c=" (rtos fc 2 4) ",d=" (rtos fd 2 4) ",e=" (rtos fe 2 4)
                              ",f=" (rtos ff 2 4) ",g=" (rtos fg 2 4) ",h=" (rtos fh 2 4) ",i=" (rtos fi 2 4))
           rval '(/ (* fa (alt_expt (sin (eval a2)) fb nil)) (avoid_zero (* fc (alt_expt (cos (eval a2)) fd nil)
                 (alt_expt (- (* fe (alt_expt (cos (eval a2)) ff nil)) (* fg (alt_expt (sin (eval a2)) fh nil))) fi nil))))))
    (T
     (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (multiplier of θ inside tan)" 1.0)
           fb (get_real 2 "b (constant in denominator)" 1.0)
           fc (get_real 2 "c (coeff of cos inside)" 2.0)
           fd (get_real 2 "d (multiplier of θ inside cos)" 2.0)
           eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ",b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ",c=" (rtos fc 2 4) ",d=" (rtos fd 2 4))
           rval '(/ (sin (* fa (eval a2))) (avoid_zero (* (cos (* fa (eval a2))) (+ fb (* fc (cos (* fd (eval a2)))))))))))
  (setq xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
