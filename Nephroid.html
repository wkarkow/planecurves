Nephroid

A two-cusped epicycloid formed when a circle of radius \(r\) rolls externally around a fixed circle of radius \(2r\).

Nephroid parametric equations:
\[ x = 3r\cos\theta - r\cos(3\theta),\quad y = 3r\sin\theta - r\sin(3\theta) \]

History and Applications

The nephroid1,2,3 is a two-cusped epicycloid formed when a circle of radius \(a\) rolls externally around a fixed circle of radius \(2a\). Christiaan Huygens demonstrated in 1678 that the nephroid is the catacaustic envelope of parallel light rays reflected from the interior of a circle; he published the observation in Traité de la lumière in 1690. The curve was also studied by Tschirnhausen in 1679, Jacques Bernoulli in 1692, and Daniel Bernoulli in 1725. Richard A. Proctor first applied the name “nephroid” (from the Greek for kidney-shaped) to this specific curve in 1878. George Biddell Airy supplied the theoretical explanation using wave optics in 1838.

In optics and the natural sciences, the nephroid appears as the bright caustic curve visible on the surface of liquid inside a cylindrical cup (such as a coffee cup) when illuminated by parallel rays such as sunlight reflecting from the curved inner wall.

In the parametric equations and in the AutoLISP routine, \(r\) is the rolling-circle radius (the same quantity as \(a\) in the construction above). The fixed circle then has radius \(2r\). The curve is the two-cusp case of the epicycloid family treated separately under Epicycloid.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:NEPHROID (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                     restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa)
  (setq id "Nephroid Curve"
        iq "x=3r·cos(θ)−r·cos(3θ), y=3r·sin(θ)−r·sin(3θ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "r (rolling circle radius)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\nr=" (rtos fa 2 4))
        xc '(- (* 3.0 fa (cos (eval a2))) (* fa (cos (* 3.0 (eval a2)))))
        yc '(- (* 3.0 fa (sin (eval a2))) (* fa (sin (* 3.0 (eval a2)))))
        skm '(T T T)a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:NEPHROID)
