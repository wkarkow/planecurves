Hippopede

Bicircular rational quartic also known as the horse fetter, Booth’s oval (ε > 0) or Booth’s lemniscate (ε < 0).

Hippopede polar equation:
\[ r^{2} = a^{2} \cos^{2}(\theta) + \varepsilon \cdot b^{2} \sin^{2}(\theta) \]
Hippopede Cartesian equation:
\[ (x^{2} + y^{2})^{2} = a^{2} x^{2} + \varepsilon \cdot b^{2} y^{2} \]

History and Applications

The hippopede1,2,3, known as the horse fetter from the Ancient Greek ἱπποπέδη due to its resemblance to a shackle placed on a horse’s foot, is a bicircular rational quartic algebraic curve symmetric about both axes. Proclus (c. 410–485 AD) investigated related spiric sections of the torus, giving the plane curve his name. Eudoxus of Cnidus (c. 408–355 BC) employed the spherical version (sphere-cylinder intersection) in his model of planetary retrograde motion using nested rotating spheres. Algebraic study of the plane form advanced with Fagnano (1750), Euler (1751), and especially British mathematician James Booth (1810–1878).

The curve is a special spiric section of a torus cut by a plane parallel to the axis and tangent to the interior. When ε > 0 it forms a closed oval (Booth’s oval); when ε < 0 it forms a figure-eight lemniscate (Booth’s lemniscate). Special cases yield the lemniscate of Bernoulli.

In astronomy, Eudoxus and Proclus used the hippopede to represent the apparent planetary paths in the homocentric-sphere system—a forerunner to Ptolemaic epicycles. As a Watt’s curve, it traces the midpoint locus in three-bar linkage mechanisms. In physics it models radial spin waves propagating from the core in asymmetric magnetic nanostructures.4

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:HIPPOPEDE (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                      restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 rval fa fb fc)
  (setq id "Hippopede"
        iq "r²=a²·cos²(θ)+ε·b²·sin²(θ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (half-x-span)" 0.5)
        fb (get_real 0 "b (y-constriction)" 0.5)
        fc (get_real 2 "ε (-1=lemniscate, +1=oval)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "a=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", ε=" (rtos fc 2 4))
        rval '(if (< (abs (+ (* fa fa (expt (cos (eval a2)) 2.0)) (* fc (* fb fb) (expt (sin (eval a2)) 2.0)))) 1e-9) 0.0
                  (sqrt (max 0.0 (+ (* fa fa (expt (cos (eval a2)) 2.0)) (* fc (* fb fb) (expt (sin (eval a2)) 2.0))))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
