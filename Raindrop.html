Raindrop Curve

A modern parametric teardrop obtained by a 90° rotation of a special case of the antiparallelogram coupler curve.

Raindrop Curve parametric equations:
\[ x = a\cdot\dfrac{3(\cos t + 1)\sin t}{4\cos t + 5},\qquad y = -a\cdot\dfrac{6\cos^{2}t + 14\cos t + 7}{2(4\cos t + 5)} \]

History and Applications

The Raindrop Curve is a modern parametric plane curve constructed by the author to produce a teardrop-shaped profile using trigonometric functions. These equations arose serendipitously from the Antiparallelogram Coupler Curve for particular parameter values, after applying a 90° rotation, yielding a shape that aligns closely with the commonly perceived raindrop silhouette. No other source for this equation can be found in the broader searches of mathematical literature. It does not correspond to the classical teardrop (piriform) curves1,2 or related quartic forms studied by earlier mathematicians such as Wallis (1685) or Bonnet (1844).

No real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences were identified for this exact parametric form. It was created as a likeness for an artist’s raindrop, suited to graphics, logos, icons, and stylized depictions of droplets, rather than a model of steady-state raindrop aerodynamics. Real raindrops in atmospheric free fall are typically oblate. The only time this sharply tipped silhouette occurs is briefly during pinch-off, when a droplet separates from the liquid above, or in the case of a hanging balloon as pictured above.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:RAINDROP (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                     divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa)
  (setq id "Raindrop Curve"
        iq "x=a·[3(cos(t)+1)sin(t)/(4cos(t)+5)], y=-a·[(6cos²(t)+14cos(t)+7)/(2(4cos(t)+5))]"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4))
        xc '(* fa (/ (* 3.0 (+ (cos (eval a2)) 1.0) (sin (eval a2))) (+ (* 4.0 (cos (eval a2))) 5.0)))
        yc '(* -1.0 fa (/ (+ (* 6.0 (cos (eval a2)) (cos (eval a2))) (* 14.0 (cos (eval a2))) 7.0) (* 2.0 (+ (* 4.0 (cos (eval a2))) 5.0))))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:RAINDROP)
