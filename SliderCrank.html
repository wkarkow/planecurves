Slider-Crank Mechanism Curve

Path of a point attached to the connecting rod of a slider-crank linkage, where Plus and Minus offset the point on either side of the slider.

Slider-Crank Mechanism Curve equations:
\[ \begin{aligned} x &= b\cos(t)+k\bigl(a-b\cos(t)\bigr)-l\cdot e\cdot\sqrt{c^{2}-\bigl(a-b\cos(t)\bigr)^{2}}\\ y &= b\sin(t)+k\cdot e\cdot\sqrt{c^{2}-\bigl(a-b\cos(t)\bigr)^{2}}+l\bigl(a-b\cos(t)\bigr) \end{aligned} \]

History and Applications

The slider-crank coupler curve1,2 is the path traced by a point attached to the side of a connecting rod of the classic linkage in which one link slides along a straight guide. The mechanism itself traces its roots to practical engineering needs for converting rotary and linear motion. By the 12th century the Persian engineer Ismail al-Jazari had already incorporated a crank and connecting rod in his twin-cylinder water pump. The arrangement became indispensable during the Industrial Revolution when it appeared in steam-engine crossheads and later in every internal-combustion piston engine. The equations given below locate an arbitrary point on the rod: distance k along the rod from the crank pin, with perpendicular offset l scaled by the sign e. The square-root term enforces the fixed length c of the rod. For a given perpendicular offset between the crank center and the slider line, the slider can be assembled on either side of that offset; in the code that follows, these two configurations are selected by the “Plus” and “Minus” options.

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences are direct and numerous. Every automobile, truck, motorcycle, and stationary reciprocating engine uses the first inversion of the slider-crank to turn piston travel into crankshaft rotation.1,2,4 Reciprocating compressors, refrigeration pumps, and hydraulic presses employ the identical linkage in reverse. Offset and inverted forms generate the quick-return stroke required in shaper machines and the linear actuators of backhoes, cranes, and automatic doors. In mechanism synthesis the coupler curve supplies prescribed trajectories for packaging equipment, textile looms, and assembly robots when a simple straight or curved path is needed without additional links. No purely mathematical or artistic extensions are documented in the engineering literature.
