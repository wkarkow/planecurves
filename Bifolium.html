Bifolium

A quartic plane curve; the pedal curve of the deltoid (tricuspoid). Includes regular, Longchamps, and generalized forms.

Regular Bifolium (polar):
\[ r = a \cdot \cos^{2}(\theta) \cdot \sin(\theta) \]
Longchamps Bifolium (implicit):
\[ (x^{2} + y^{2})^{2} = 4 a x^{2} y \]
Longchamps Bifolium (polar):
\[ r = \cos^{2}(\theta) \cdot (a \cdot \cos(\theta) + b \cdot \sin(\theta)) \]
Generalized Longchamps (polar):
\[ r = a \cdot (\sin^{b}(c\theta) - d) \cdot (\cos^{e}(f\theta) - g) \]

History and Applications

The bifolium curve, also known as the double folium or bifoliate curve, was studied by French mathematicians Gaston Albert Gohierre de Longchamps1 in 1886 and Henri Brocard1 in 1887. It is a quartic plane curve2 and the pedal curve of the deltoid (tricuspoid) with the pedal point at the midpoint of one of the three curved sides.2,3 The regular bifolium is the special case obtained when the pedal point is at a cusp of the deltoid4; it can also be generated as the locus of points M on the perpendicular to a fixed diameter OA through variable points N on the circle of diameter OA, where NM = ON.4

The Longchamps bifolium introduces separate scaling coefficients for the cosine and sine terms, while the generalized form further extends the equation with variable exponents, frequencies, and offsets. Another “bifolium” in the sense of a two-lobed, leaf-like shape is the “half-quadrifolium” (r = a · sin(2θ), 0 ≤ θ ≤ π), included in the implementation for comparison.

No real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences have been documented in the primary references. The generalized form can be adjusted to resemble a peacock’s tail or a sea scallop.

(defun C:BIFOLIUM (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                     restartflag rval xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 eq_display fa fb fc fd fe ff fg fh fi)
  (setq id "Bifolium"
        iq ""
        dcl "BIFOLIUM"
        opt '("Half-quadrifolium" "Regular Bifolium" "Longchamps Bifolium" "Generalized Longchamps")
        kw 0
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (cond
  ((eq kw 0)
    (princ "r=a·(sin(2θ)")
    (setq paramstep (/ paramstep 2)
          fa (get_real 2 "a (size)" 1.0)
          eq_display (strcat "\nr=" (rtos fa 2 4) "·sin(2θ)   [θ 0 to π]")
          rval '(* fa (sin (* 2 (eval a2))))))
  ((eq kw 1)
    (princ "r=a·cos²(θ)sin(θ)")
   (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (size)" 1.0)
          eq_display (strcat "\nr=" (rtos fa 2 4) "sin(θ)·cos²(θ)")
          rval '(* fa (sin (eval a2)) (cos (eval a2)) (cos (eval a2)))))
  ((eq kw 2)
    (princ "r=cos²(θ)(a·cos²(θ)+b·(sin(θ))")
    (setq fa (get_real 0 "a (larger = more asymmetric)" 1.0)
            fb (get_real 0 "b (larger = more symmetric)" 1.0)
            eq_display (strcat "\nr=cos²(θ)(" (rtos fa 2 4) "·cos(θ)+" (rtos fb 2 4) "·sin(θ))")
            rval '(* (alt_expt (cos (eval a2)) 2 nil) (+ (* fa (cos (eval a2))) (* fb (sin (eval a2)))))))
  ((eq kw 3)
    (princ "r=a·(sinᵇ(cθ)−d)·(cosᵉ(fθ)−g)")
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (amplitude)" 1.0)
          fb (get_real 0 "b (sin exponent)" 2.0)
          fc (get_real 0 "c (sin frequency)" 12.0)
          fd (get_real 0 "d (sin offset in °)" 0.0)
          fe (get_real 0 "e (cos exponent)" 1.0)
          ff (get_real 0 "f (cos frequency)" 1.0)
          fg (get_real 0 "g (cos offset in °)" 0.0)
          fh (* fd (/ pi 180.0))
          fi (* fg (/ pi 180.0))
          eq_display (strcat "\nr=" (rtos fa 2 4) "·(sin^" (rtos fb 2 4) "(" (rtos fc 2 4) "θ)-" (rtos fd 2 4) "°)·(cos^"
                                    (rtos fe 2 4) "(" (rtos ff 2 4) "θ)-" (rtos fg 2 4) "°)")
          rval '(* fa (- (alt_expt (sin (* fc (eval a2))) fb nil) fh) (- (alt_expt (cos (* ff (eval a2))) fe nil) fi)))))
  (setq xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
