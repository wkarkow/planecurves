Hypotrochoid

A roulette traced by a point attached to a circle of radius r rolling without slipping inside a fixed circle of radius R, the point lying at distance d from the rolling-circle center.

Hypotrochoid parametric equations:
\[ x=(R-r)\cos\theta + d\cos\left(\dfrac{R-r}{r}\theta\right),\quad y=(R-r)\sin\theta - d\sin\left(\dfrac{R-r}{r}\theta\right) \]
Generalized parametric equations:
\[ x=a\cos\theta + b\cos^{c}(d\theta),\quad y=e\sin\theta - f\sin^{g}(h\theta) \]

History and Applications

The hypotrochoid1,2,3 is a roulette traced by a point attached to a small circle of radius r rolling without slipping inside a fixed circle of radius R, with the tracing point at distance d from the center of the rolling circle. These curves belong to the trochoid family and become a hypocycloid when d equals r. Mathematicians Philippe de La Hire, Girard Desargues, Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, and Isaac Newton studied the curves in the context of rolling circles and mechanical linkages during the 17th century.3 An earlier special case that produces straight-line motion (the Tusi couple, a hypocycloid with ratio 1/2) was known to the Persian mathematician Nasir al-Din al-Tusi in the 13th century.4 The Spirograph toy later made various hypotrochoids accessible through geared wheels.

Real-life applications appear in mechanical engineering. Hypotrochoid profiles form the basis for internal cycloidal gearing and gear belts that deliver smooth positive displacement in compact pumps used for lubrication, fuel delivery, and power steering systems. Certain square watch mechanisms employ the curve (when the fixed-to-rolling radius ratio is 4) to approximate straight-line segments. Special cases also appear in rotary engine rotor paths and drilling mechanisms that produce multi-edged holes.
