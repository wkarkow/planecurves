Visiera Curve

A right rational circular cubic named by Giuseppe Peano in 1887 by analogy with the versiera; it is the antihyperbolism of the versiera of Agnesi and possesses an isolated point.

Visiera polar equation:
\[ r = a \left( \frac{1}{\sin \theta} + \sin \theta \right) \]
Visiera Cartesian equation:
\[ x(x^{2} + y^{2}) = a(x^{2} + 2y^{2}) \]
Explicit form:
\[ x = \pm y \sqrt{\frac{2a - y}{y - a}} \]

History and Applications

The visiera1,2, from the Italian word for “visor,” was named by Giuseppe Peano (1858–1932) in 1887 in his treatise Applicazioni geometriche del calcolo infinitesimale (p. 87). Peano introduced the curve by direct analogy with the versiera (witch of Agnesi), using the same generating circle that appears in the construction of the versiera. In Peano’s construction one draws an arbitrary transversal through the point A (the intersection of the circle with the diameter used for the versiera). The transversal meets the tangent line at the opposite end of the diameter in T and the circle again in U; the midpoint N of the segment TU traces the visiera as the transversal rotates.2 The resulting locus is a right rational circular cubic possessing an isolated point (related to the Sluze cubic).1

The curve is the antihyperbolism of the versiera with respect to the origin and the line \( x = a \). Equivalently, it may be obtained as the cissoid of a circle and a tangent to that circle at A, with pole at the diametrically opposite point, or as the inverse of a certain ellipse with respect to one of its secondary vertices. It can also be generated by the Newton set-square method or by the Kiernan construction. The name “visiera” was chosen to echo “versiera” while distinguishing the two curves; Loria later suggested the form “visiera di Peano” to make the distinction explicit.3 The visiera must not be confused with the conchoid of Nicomedes.1

No applications in engineering or the natural sciences have been identified for the visiera. Searches of mathematical catalogues, engineering literature, and scientific databases yield only the geometric characterizations above; the curve remains a purely algebraic specimen studied for its relation to the versiera and to the class of rational circular cubics.

(defun C:VISIERA (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                         divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa)
  (setq id "Visiera Curve"
        iq "r=a·(1/sin(θ)+sin(θ))"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4))
        rval '(* fa (+ (/ 1.0 (avoid_zero (sin (eval a2)))) (sin (eval a2))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
