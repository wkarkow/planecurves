Kiss Curve

Symmetric recurved lip profiles generated by three distinct equation families.

Classic parametric equations:
\[ x = \cos t, \quad y = a \sin^3 t \]
Classic Cartesian equation:
\[ a^4 y^2 = (a^2 - x^2)^3 \]
Mating parametric equations:
\[ \rho = k - 1 - \cos t, \quad \theta = t - 2\sqrt{\frac{k}{k-2}}\arctan\left(\sqrt{\frac{k}{k-2}}\tan\frac{t}{2}\right), \quad x = \frac{\rho\sin\theta}{2k}, \quad y = \frac{\rho\cos\theta}{2k} \]
Generalized Bicorn parametric equations:
\[ x = \sin(t/2), \quad y = \frac{\cos^3(t/2)\cdot(2 + \cos t)}{g} \]

History and Applications

The symmetric recurved profile examined here arises from three distinct equation families.

The simplest is the rational sextic given by the parametric equations \(x=\cos t\), \(y=a\sin^3 t\) (or the equivalent Cartesian form \(a^4 y^2=(a^2-x^2)^3\)). On the MathCurve site the curve is presented under the homemade name “kiss curve” (French bouche), with the more precise description “puckering, duckface.” The same parametric form appears in other curve catalogues as the “mouth curve.” No earlier historical attribution is recorded; the equations themselves are elementary and require no special construction beyond a power of the sine function.1,2

A second realization is obtained as the opposite-direction mating profile of a cardioid whose hub lies at the cusp. With centre-distance ratio \(k=d/a=18/5\), the polar parametric equations for the opposite-direction mating profile of a cardioid (hub at the cusp) that are given on the MathCurve engrenage page produce a closed outline which, after a 90° rotation, yields a lip-like contour. The construction belongs to the classical theory of mating gear profiles; the particular ratio \(k=18/5\) is the value chosen on that page to illustrate the “mouth with the heart.” Other nearby values of \(k\) continue to generate usable artistic lip shapes even though they no longer satisfy the strict mating condition.3

The third family is a specialization of the generalized bicorn \[x=a\sin(b\theta),\qquad y=a\frac{\cos^c(d\theta)\,(e+\cos(f\theta))}{g+h\sin^i(j\theta)}.\] The classic bicorn (cocked-hat curve) is the rational quartic first studied by Sylvester in 1864 and later examined by Cayley and de Longchamps; its standard trigonometric parametrization is one of the forms recorded in the modern catalogues.4,5,6 The multi-parameter extension used here is a contemporary generalization that permits independent control of frequency, envelope shape and asymmetry. Fixing all coefficients except the denominator base \(g\) (and setting the sine coefficient in the denominator to zero) produces a one-parameter family of lip profiles whose recurve is slightly stronger than that of the pure sextic.

No significant applications in engineering or the natural sciences are attached to any of the three equations. The profiles remain of interest chiefly as compact illustrations of how recognizably similar visual forms can be generated by algebraically unrelated expressions—the same phenomenon already encountered with the various egg and heart curves.

  (setq id "Kiss Curve"
        iq ""
        dcl "KISS"
        opt '("Classic" "Mating" "Bicorn")
        kw 0
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (cond
    ((eq kw 0)
     (princ "\nx=cos(t), y=sinᵃ(t)")

           eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ")")

           yc '(* fa (expt (sin (eval a2)) 3))
           skm '(T T T)))
    ((eq kw 1)
     (princ "\nρ=k-1-cos(t), θ=t-2·√(k/(k-2))·atan(√(k/(k-2))·tan(t/2))")

           xc '(* fe (* (- fa 1.0 (cos (eval a2))) (sin (- (eval a2) (* 2.0 fd (atan (* fd (tan (/ (eval a2) 2.0)))))))))
           skm '(T T T T)))
    ((eq kw 2)
     (princ "\nx=sin(t/2), y=cos^3(t/2)·(2+cos(t)) / g")
					(setq fa (get_real 2 "a (1.5=puckered, 12=compressed)" 1.5)
											eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4))
											xc '(sin (* 0.5 (eval a2)))
											yc '(/ (* 0.5 (expt (cos (* 0.5 (eval a2))) 3) (+ 2 (cos (* 1.0 (eval a2))))) fa)
											skm '(nil T T))))
  (setq a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))

(if (eq kw 1)
    (progn
      (command "_.move" plineents "" '(0 0) (list 0 (- fc)))
