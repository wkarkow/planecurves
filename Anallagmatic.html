Anallagmatic Spiral

A plane curve invariant under inversion in a circle of radius \( a \) with outer and inner branches asymptotic to Archimedean spirals.

Anallagmatic Spiral polar equation:
\[ 2n\theta = \left(\dfrac{r}{a}\right)^n + \left(\dfrac{a}{r}\right)^n \]
Equivalent explicit form:
\[ r^n = a^n\left(n\theta \pm \sqrt{n^2\theta^2 - 1}\right) \]
Polar parametrization (\( r > 0 \)):
\[ r = a\,e^{t/n},\qquad n\theta = \cosh t \]

History and Applications

The anallagmatic spiral1 was studied and named by C. Masurel in 2013. The term “anallagmatic” (from the Greek allagma, “change”) means “without change” and denotes a curve that is globally invariant under inversion; the general class was examined by Mannheim in 1862.2 For the spiral the inversion is centered at the origin with power \( a^2 \): the substitution \( \rho \to a^2/\rho \) leaves the polar equation unaltered, so the outer and inner branches are images of each other. Both branches meet the circle of radius \( a \) where \( |n\theta| = 1 \). Beyond that point the outer branch is asymptotic to an Archimedean spiral of index \( 1/n \) and the inner branch to one of index \( -1/n \).

A kinematic interpretation appears in the theory of wheel-road couples.3 When an anallagmatic spiral of parameter \( n \) rolls without slipping on the linear-pursuit curve of the same parameter \( n \) (the classical dog-and-rabbit trajectory first treated by Bouguer in 1732), the pole of the spiral traces either the asymptote of the pursuit curve (\( n > 1 \)) or the tangent at its vertex (\( n < 1 \)). No applications in engineering or the natural sciences have been documented for this particular spiral.
