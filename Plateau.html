Plateau Curve

A trigonometric parametric family producing algebraic curves whose number of branches depends on the ratio of frequencies \(m\) and \(n\).

Plateau Curve parametric equations:
\[ x = a\dfrac{\sin((m+n)t)}{\sin((m-n)t)},\quad y = 2a\dfrac{\sin(mt)\sin(nt)}{\sin((m-n)t)} \]
Circular degeneration when \(m=2n\):
\[ (x-a)^{2}+y^{2}=4a^{2} \]

History and Applications

The Plateau curve1,2,3, named after Joseph Antoine Ferdinand Plateau (1801–1883), a Belgian physicist and mathematician, is a family of parametric plane curves. Plateau is best known for his pioneering experiments with soap films that established Plateau’s laws on the geometry of soap films and for stating Plateau’s problem on minimal surfaces. He also investigated a variety of special plane curves, including this trigonometric parametric family. The curve is defined for positive scaling factor \(a\) and parameters \(m\) and \(n\) where \(m\neq n\) (to avoid division by zero). When \(m=2n\), the curve degenerates to a circle centered at \((a,0)\) with radius \(2a\). For other integer ratios \(m/n\), the curve produces closed algebraic curves whose number of branches depends on the ratio. The parametric form provides a straightforward way to generate and study these shapes using basic trigonometric identities familiar from calculus.

No real-life applications of this specific plane curve in engineering or the natural sciences were identified in primary mathematical references. Its significance remains within the mathematical cataloging of parametric and trigonometric curves.
