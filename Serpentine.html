Serpentine Cubic

A rational cubic, also called the anguinea, having an odd S-shaped curve with an inflection at the origin.

Serpentine Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ y = \dfrac{a \cdot d \cdot x}{x^{2}+d^{2}} \]
Serpentine Curve parametric equations:
\[ x = d \cdot \tan\!\left(\dfrac{t}{2}\right),\quad y = \dfrac{a}{2}\sin(t) \]
Serpentine Curve polar equation:
\[ r^{2} = \dfrac{d}{\cos(\theta)}\left(\dfrac{a}{\sin(\theta)}-\dfrac{d}{\cos(\theta)}\right) \]

History and Applications

The Serpentine Cubic1,2,3, also known as anguinea from the Latin anguis meaning snake, is a rational cubic curve. It was studied by Guillaume de l'Hôpital and Christiaan Huygens in 1692 and later named and classified by Isaac Newton in 1701 as part of his enumeration of cubic curves. Newton’s work on the curve appeared in John Harris’s Lexicon Technicum (1710). As an odd function, it has 180° rotational symmetry about the origin, an inflection point at the origin where the slope changes sign, and the x-axis as a horizontal asymptote; as \(|x|\) grows large the y-values approach zero, a property familiar to engineers modeling bounded transitions.

In engineering the serpentine or S-curve is used in surveying and civil design to create smooth easement transitions that connect parallel roads or railway lines intersecting at small angles.4 It also appears geometrically as the directrix of Plücker’s conoid5 and as a projection of the horopter in models of binocular vision.

(defun C:SERPENTINE (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                       divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb)
  (setq id "Serpentine Cubic"
        iq "y=(a·d·x)/(x²+d²)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions to x-span of division point"
        dct 500
        dpt '(5.0 0.0)
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (half-yₘₐₓ)" 2.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "d (x peak)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", d=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        xc '(eval a2)
        yc '(/ (* fa fb (eval a2)) (+ (* (eval a2) (eval a2)) (* fb fb)))
        paramstep (/ xspan divcount)
        skm '(T T nil)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
