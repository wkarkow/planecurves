Tschirnhausen Cubic

The catacaustic of a parabolic mirror and the negative pedal curve of a parabola taken with respect to the parabola's focus.

Tschirnhausen Cubic polar equation:
\[ r = \cos^{-3}\!\left(\dfrac{\theta}{3}\right) \]
Generalized polar equation:
\[ r = a \cdot \cos^{-p/q}\!\left(\dfrac{q\theta}{p}\right) \]

History and Applications

The curve now known as Tschirnhausen's cubic was first studied by the German mathematician and physicist Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus (1651–1708). In 1690 he published work in Acta Eruditorum in which the curve appeared as the catacaustic (caustic by reflection) produced by a parabolic mirror.1,2 Shortly afterward, in 1696, Guillaume de l'Hôpital examined the same curve.3 It was later investigated by Pierre Bouguer and, in 1832, by Eugène Catalan, who recognized its utility as a trisectrix—a curve that permits the geometric trisection of an arbitrary angle.4 The name “Tschirnhaus cubic” (sometimes spelled Tschirnhausen) was formalized in 1900 by R. C. Archibald in a systematic classification of plane curves; the curve is also known historically as Catalan's trisectrix or l'Hôpital's cubic.1 Algebraically, the classical Tschirnhaus cubic is the negative pedal curve of a parabola taken with respect to the parabola's focus.1 It therefore inherits a close geometric relationship with the parabola itself. When the exponent parameter in the generalized polar form is set to 2, the equation reduces exactly to the standard polar equation of a parabola; when the exponent is set to 1 the curve degenerates to a straight line. These reductions illustrate how the one-parameter family encompasses both the classical trisectrix (exponent 3) and other well-known curves already present in the catalog. If that parameter becomes a negative fraction, the generalized equation creates sinusoidal spirals. See Sinusoidal Spiral image 18 for comparison with the last curve image here.

Real-life applications are concentrated in classical geometry and optics. The trisectrix property, first exploited by Catalan, provides a purely geometric means of trisecting an angle using only the curve and straight lines from the origin.2 The curve's origin as the catacaustic of a parabola links it directly to reflection problems in optics; the same caustic property appears in certain treatments of parabolic mirrors and their focal behavior.4 In modern terms the classical case remains primarily of mathematical and historical interest, while the special case of exponent 2 inherits the extensive engineering applications of the parabola (projectile trajectories, parabolic reflectors and antennas, suspension-bridge catenaries under uniform load, and optical concentrators). The generalized family with arbitrary positive exponent has found occasional use in parametric modeling and approximation studies, but no widespread direct engineering deployment beyond these reductions has been documented.

(defun C:TSCHIRNHAUS (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt xspan restartflag
                        id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm maxdraw a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 rval fa fb fc fd fe ff fg fh fi fj fk)
  (setq id "Tschirnhausen Cubic"
        iq "r=a·cos^{-p/q}((q/p)θ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 720
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale factor)" 1.0)
        fb (get_integer 2 "p (numerator)" 3)
        fc (get_integer 2 "q (denominator)" 1)
        fd (gcd fb fc)
        fe (/ fb fd)
        ff (/ fc fd)
        fh (/ (float fb) (float fc))
        fg 1)
  (if (= ff 1)
      (setq fg 2)
      (progn
        (setq fg 1)
        (while (not (zerop (rem (* fg fe) (* 4 ff))))
          (setq fg (1+ fg)))))
  (if (< fh 1)
      (progn
        (setq fi 0 fj fe)
        (while (= (rem fj 2) 0)
          (setq fj (/ fj 2)
                fi (1+ fi)))
        (setq fg (* fg (expt 2 fi)))))
  (setq fk (* fg dct))
  (princ (strcat "\n" (itoa fk) " segments needed for closure"))
  (setq paramstep (/ (* (* fh 90.0) (/ pi 180.0)) divcount)
        divcount fk
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", p=" (itoa fb) ", q=" (itoa fc))
        rval '(* fa (alt_expt (cos (/ (eval a2) fh)) (* -1 fh) nil))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 20.0)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
