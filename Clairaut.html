Clairaut Curve

A polar trigonometric power curve r = a · sin^b(θ) that produces symmetric multi-lobed patterns for integer b and progressive narrowing or shape variation for non-integer exponents.

Clairaut Curve polar equation:
\[ r = a \cdot \sin^{b} (\theta) \]

History and Applications

The Clairaut curve belongs to the family of polar power curves explored in early analytic geometry. Alexis Claude Clairaut (1713–1765)1,2 a French mathematician and astronomer, presented a memoir on new curves to the Paris Academy of Sciences in 1726 at the age of thirteen. Clairaut later contributed to the theory of differential equations (including the equation that bears his name) and to geodesy, where he confirmed the oblate shape of the Earth through measurements of gravity at different latitudes. Curves of this trigonometric power form arise naturally in the study of polar coordinate systems, which Clairaut helped advance alongside contemporaries such as Euler. For integer values of the exponent b, the curve produces symmetric lobe patterns; non-integer b yields more varied shapes, including narrowing effects as b increases.

No specific real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences were identified for this exact general form in available mathematical references. Special cases for integer b reduce to variants of rose curves, which appear in educational visualizations and mathematical modeling but lack documented engineering or scientific uses beyond that.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:CLAIRAUT (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                     restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 rval fa fb)
  (setq id "Clairaut Curve"
        iq "r=a·sinᵇ(θ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "b (exponent)" 0.25)
        eq_display (strcat "\nr=" (rtos fa 2 4) "·sin^" (rtos fb 2 4) "(θ)")
        rval '(* fa (alt_expt (sin (eval a2)) fb T))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:CLAIRAUT)
