Cardioid Parallel

Parallel (offset) curve of the classical cardioid at fixed normal distance.

Cardioid Parallel parametric equations:
\[ x = a\cos t\,(1+\cos t) + d\cos\frac{3t}{2}, \quad y = a\sin t\,(1+\cos t) + d\sin\frac{3t}{2} \]

History and Applications

The parallel curve of a curve is the locus of points lying a fixed distance \(d\) from the original curve, measured along its normal. Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz studied the construction in 1692.1 For a parametric curve \(M(t)\) with unit normal \(\mathbf{n}(t)\), the parallel at distance \(d\) is \(M(t) + d\,\mathbf{n}(t)\). Parallel curves of an algebraic curve remain algebraic. Cusps appear where the offset distance equals the local radius of curvature; those points lie on the evolute. The parallel curves of a given curve are the involutes of its evolute.2 For the cardioid the evolute is a similar cardioid (homothety of ratio \(-\frac{1}{3}\)). One involute is again a cardioid; the others are self-parallel. MathCurve gives the parallel of the classical cardioid at distance \(d\) by the parametrization below. Because of the cusp, the curve is parallel to itself at distance \(-d\). Consequently the cusps of the parallel travel along the entire evolute.3 Due to self-similarity, the parallel curve draws both the inner and outer parallels regardless of the sign of the offset distance.

In computer-aided design the same construction is called an offset curve. AutoCAD’s OFFSET command produces it: the new entity is generated by displacing every point of the selected object a constant distance along the normal. For drafting convenience OFFSET resolves corners according to the system variable OFFSETGAPTYPE (extend, fillet, or chamfer) and typically trims self-intersections so that the result is a single clean polyline or spline. The mathematical parallel retains the full locus, including cusps and self-intersections.4 Offset curves appear in numerically controlled machining, where they describe the boundary of the material removed by a round cutter whose center follows a programmed path. They are also used in vector-graphics stroking (the outline of a path of finite width) and in tolerance analysis. No specialized engineering or scientific application of the cardioid parallel itself was found beyond these general uses of offset curves.4

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:CARDIOIDPARALLEL (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                            divpt restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb)
  (setq id "Cardioid Parallel"
        iq "x=a·cos(t)(1+cos(t))+d·cos(3t/2), y=a·sin(t)(1+cos(t))+d·sin(3t/2)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 720
        dpt nil
        span 720
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (cardioid scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 0 "d (offset distance)" 0.5)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", d=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        xc '(+ (* fa (cos (eval a2)) (+ 1.0 (cos (eval a2))))
               (* fb (cos (* 1.5 (eval a2)))))
        yc '(+ (* fa (sin (eval a2)) (+ 1.0 (cos (eval a2))))
               (* fb (sin (* 1.5 (eval a2)))))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0.0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:CARDIOIDPARALLEL)
