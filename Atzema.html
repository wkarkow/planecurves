Atzema Spiral

A parametric spiral curve distinguished by its circular catacaustic property under reflection from a point source at the origin; approximates an Archimedean spiral for large θ.

Pritch-Atzema parametric equations:
\[ x = a\left(\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta} - 2\cos\theta - \theta\sin\theta\right),\quad y = a\left(\frac{\cos\theta}{\theta} - 2\sin\theta + \theta\cos\theta\right) \]
Generalized parametric equations:
\[ x = a\left(\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta} - b\cos\theta - c\theta\sin\theta\right),\quad y = d\left(\frac{\cos\theta}{\theta} - e\sin\theta + f\theta\cos\theta\right) \]

History and Applications

The Atzema spiral, also known as the Pritch-Atzema spiral, is a parametric curve distinguished by its unique optical property: when light rays from a point source at the origin reflect off the curve, the envelope of those reflected rays (the catacaustic) forms a perfect circle of radius 2a.1,2 This property was proven by Eisso Atzema (with Yael Pritch) around 2000.2

For large values of the parameter θ, the curve approximates an Archimedean spiral, where the distance from the origin increases linearly with angle.2,3 The curve has an asymptote at x = 0 as θ approaches infinity in certain parameterizations, though it unwinds outward from near the origin.2 The parametric equations have a limiting behavior near θ = 0 yielding finite coordinates after the 1/θ terms are handled (via sinc-like limits: sin(θ)/θ → 1 and cos(θ)/θ → 0 as θ → 0, though adjusted by the coefficients).2

No widespread real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences appear in standard curve references, beyond its theoretical interest in optics as a demonstration of a spiral mirror producing a circular caustic (relevant to understanding reflection envelopes in curved mirrors or wavefronts).1 The property makes it a curiosity in mathematical optics, but practical uses (such as in mirror design for specific focal behaviors) are not documented in sources.
