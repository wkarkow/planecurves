Sigmoid Curve

A member of the sigmoid family, $y = a \cdot \dfrac{x}{\bigl(|x|^t + 1\bigr)^{1/t}}$.

Sigmoid Curve equation (t > 0):
\[ y = a\cdot \dfrac{x}{\bigl(|x|^t + 1\bigr)^{1/t}} \]
Negative-t equation (t < 0):
\[ y = a\cdot \dfrac{x}{\bigl(|x|^t + 1\bigr)^{1/t}} \]

History and Applications

The algebraic sigmoid family catalogued by Robert Ferréol on MathCurve is defined as an odd, differentiable function that increases from −1 to +1 and lies between a pair of horizontal asymptotes equidistant from its inflection point at the origin.1 Members include the hyperbolic tangentoid and tangentoid, among others. The specimen treated here is the one-parameter generalization, $ y=\frac{x}{\bigl(|x|^t+1\bigr)^{1/t}}, t>0 $. It recovers two earlier examples on the same page: the softsign \( x/(|x|+1) \) when \( t=1 \), and the algebraic connection of two hyperbolic branches \( x/\sqrt{x^2+1} \) when \( t=2 \). The same page also lists the transcendental hyperbolic tangentoid \( y=\tanh x \) and the ordinary tangentoid \( y=\tan x \) as further normalized sigmoids. No earlier attribution for the general-\( t \) algebraic form is given; the page is a modern catalogue entry. The restriction \( t>0 \) is required for the horizontal asymptotes. When \( t \< 0 \) the expression becomes unbounded, asymptotic to the line \( y=x \) at infinity, and discontinuous at the origin (the right- and left-hand limits are \(\pm 1\)). The AutoLISP implementation therefore offers the negative-exponent case under a separate “Negative-t” option that is not claimed to be a sigmoid.

The special case \( t=1 \), the softsign curve, was introduced by David L. Elliott in 1993 as an activation function for artificial neural networks.2 It maps the reals onto (−1, 1), is centered at the origin, and requires only absolute value and division; the absence of exponentials makes it cheaper to evaluate than the hyperbolic tangent while retaining a similar saturating shape. Because it approaches its asymptotes only polynomially rather than exponentially, its derivative decays more slowly, which can mitigate vanishing-gradient effects in some architectures. The case \( t=2 \) supplies a purely algebraic approximation to \( \tanh x \) that has been used in matrix-valued optimizers where transcendental functions are undesirable.3

Broader members of the sigmoid class (most often the logistic function) appear in population dynamics, dose-response curves, and the output layers of neural networks. The algebraic family above is used principally for its computational simplicity in the same neural-network setting and in related numerical work that needs a smooth, odd, saturating map without exponential evaluations. No engineering applications peculiar to arbitrary \( t \) beyond these computational uses have been identified.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:SIGMOID (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt
                    xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 maxdraw fa fb)
  (setq id "Sigmoid Curve"
        iq "y=scale·x/(|x|^t+1)^(1/t)"
        dcl "SIGMOID"
        opt '("Sigmoid" "Negative-t")
        kw 0
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions to x-span of division point"
        dct 1000
        dpt '(10.0 0.0)
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 6 "scale" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 6 "t (exponent)" 2.0)
        fb (if (= kw 1) (- fb) fb)
        eq_display (strcat "\nscale=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", t=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        xc '(eval a2)
        yc '(cond ((and (= (eval a2) 0.0) (= kw 0)) 0.0) ((and (= (eval a2) 0.0) (= kw 1)) 1.0)
            (T (* fa (/ (eval a2) (alt_expt (+ (alt_expt (abs (eval a2)) fb nil) 1.0) (/ 1.0 fb) nil)))))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 100)
        paramstep (/ xspan divcount)
        skm '(T T nil)
        a1 0.0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:SIGMOID)
