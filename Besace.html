Besace

A rational quartic Lissajous algebraic curve resembling a saddlebag or double pouch.

Besace implicit equation:
\[ c^{2} y = b x^{2} \pm a x c^{2} - x^{3} \quad (c = a^{2} + b^{2}) \]
Besace parametric equations:
\[ x = a \cos(\theta) - b \sin(\theta), \quad y = -x \sin(\theta) \]

History and Applications

The Besace curve1 was studied and named by the Swiss mathematician Gabriel Cramer in 1750. The French word "besace" means saddlebag or double pouch, with Latin bis- ("twice") + saccus ("bag"), describing the typical shape for many values of the parameters. It is a rational quartic algebraic curve and belongs to the family of Lissajous curves2 (generated from two perpendicular harmonic motions whose frequencies are related). Geometrically it is the orthogonal projection of Viviani's curve1 (the intersection of a sphere and a cylinder of equal radius) onto planes that pass through the cylinder's axis. For large values of a relative to b, the shape approximates a parabola. When b = 0, it reduces exactly to the lemniscate of Gerono.2

No real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences were identified in the standard mathematical references1,2 for this specific curve. Its interest remains primarily theoretical, illustrating properties of algebraic curves and projections of three-dimensional space curves.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:BESACE (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                   restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb)
  (setq id "Besace"
        iq "x=a·cos(θ)–b·sin(θ), y=–x·sin(θ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (cos coefficient)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "b (sin coefficient)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        xc '(- (* fa (cos (eval a2))) (* fb (sin (eval a2))))
        yc '(* (- (sin (eval a2))) (eval xc))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
