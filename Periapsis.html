Periapsis Precession Spiral

A polar plot of a precessing elliptical orbit.

Periapsis Precession Curve polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{p}{1+e\cdot\cos\left(\dfrac{\theta-\omega_{0}}{1+\Delta\omega/360}\right)} \]
Equivalent form with frequency factor \(k\):
\[ r = \dfrac{p}{1+e\cdot\cos\bigl(k(\theta-\omega_{0})\bigr)},\qquad k=\dfrac{1}{1+\Delta\omega/360} \]

History and Applications

Apsidal precession is the gradual rotation of the line connecting the apsides (periapsis and apoapsis) of an elliptical orbit. The Periapsis Precession Spiral provides a simple polar parameterization to visualize and plot such precessing orbits over multiple revolutions, producing rosette-like patterns. Isaac Newton first analyzed this effect mathematically in his Principia Mathematica (1687) via the theorem of revolving orbits, applying it to the Moon and planets under inverse-square forces.1 In 1859 Urbain Le Verrier identified an anomalous excess advance in Mercury’s perihelion (approximately 43 arcseconds per century) after subtracting all Newtonian planetary perturbations.2 This residual was explained exactly by Albert Einstein in 1915 within general relativity, providing one of the earliest quantitative tests of the theory.3

In engineering and the natural sciences the effect is routinely modeled for artificial satellites whose orbits experience apsidal precession from Earth’s oblateness; highly elliptical Molniya orbits, for example, are placed at a critical inclination of 63.4° so that the argument of perigee remains stationary, enabling continuous high-latitude coverage.4 Apsidal precession is also one component of the Milankovitch cycles, where Earth’s orbital parameter changes (including a ~112 000-year apsidal precession cycle) alter the seasonal and latitudinal distribution of solar insolation and have driven long-term climate variations including glacial-interglacial cycles.5

In the AutoLISP routine, \(p\) is the scale (semi-latus rectum, default 1), \(e<1\) is the eccentricity (default 0.25), \(\Delta\omega\) is the periapsis advance in degrees per orbit with the positive sign prograde (default 5), and \(\omega_0\) is the initial periapsis direction in degrees (default 0). The frequency factor is computed as \(k=1/(1+\Delta\omega/360)\). The polar radius uses \(1+e\cos(k(\theta-\omega_0))\) in the denominator; a vanishing denominator is blocked by the usual avoid_zero guard. The trace is drawn in polar mode over one 360° span of the parameter; repeating the command or increasing the drawn span shows the accumulating rosette.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:PERIAPSIS (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                    restartflag xspan id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display rval xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd fe ff)
  (setq id "Periapsis Precession Spiral"
        iq "r=p/(1+e·cos(k(θ-ω₀))), k=1/(1+Δω/360)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "p (scale / semi-latus rectum)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "e (eccentricity, <1)" 0.25)
        fc (get_real 2 "Δω (° per orbit, + = prograde)" 5.0)
        fd (get_real 2 "ω₀ (initial periapsis direction in °)" 0.0)
        fe (/ 1.0 (+ 1.0 (/ fc 360.0)))
        ff (* fd (/ pi 180.0))
        eq_display (strcat "\np=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", e=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", Δω=" (rtos fc 2 4) ", ω₀=" (rtos fd 2 4))
        rval '(/ fa (avoid_zero (+ 1.0 (* fb (cos (* fe (- (eval a2) ff)))))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:PERIAPSIS)
