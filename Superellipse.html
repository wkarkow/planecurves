Superellipse

The Lamé family with shapes varying from star-like to diamond to ellipse to rounded rectangle to near-rectangle.

Superellipse implicit equation:
\[ \left\lvert\dfrac{x}{a}\right\rvert^{n} + \left\lvert\dfrac{y}{b}\right\rvert^{n} = 1 \]
Superellipse parametric equations:
\[ x = \pm a\,\lvert\cos t\rvert^{2/n},\quad y = \pm b\,\lvert\sin t\rvert^{2/n} \]

History and Applications

The superellipse, also known as the Lamé curve1,2, was introduced by Gabriel Lamé (1795–1870) in 18183,4 as a generalization of the ellipse equation in his study of algebraic curves and elliptic coordinates. Lamé examined the family \(\lvert x/a\rvert^{n}+\lvert y/b\rvert^{n}=1\), where the ellipse occurs exactly at \(n=2\). Earlier special cases (diamond at \(n=1\)) were known, but Lamé treated the full parameterized family systematically. For much of its early history the curve remained primarily theoretical, illustrating continuous change in geometric properties with varying exponent.

The term superellipse gained widespread use in applied contexts in the 20th century. The curve provides a single tunable shape spanning circles, ellipses, rounded rectangles, and nearly rectangular profiles without corners or discontinuities. Adjusting \(n\) controls curvature concentration while preserving axial symmetry. In mechanical and structural design superelliptic profiles minimize stress concentration in cutouts, flanges, and load-bearing openings because larger \(n\) yields near-rectangular forms retaining smooth curvature.5 In manufacturing and CAD/CAM the curve simplifies toolpaths, fillets, and finite-element meshing by avoiding sharp vertices. Real-world uses include urban design (Sergels Torg roundabout, Stockholm, \(n=2.5\), \(a/b=6/5\), completed 1967)3, stadium architecture (Azteca Olympic Stadium, Mexico City, 1968)3, and logos (Pittsburg Steelers helmet)2. In natural sciences it approximates certain plant stem cross-sections such as square bamboo.5 Piet Hein also applied the shape to tables, dishes, and the superegg novelty solid.

                         paramstep restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 eq_display fa fb fc)
  (setq id "Superellipse"
        iq "|x/a|ⁿ + |y/b|ⁿ = 1"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (x semi-axis)" 2.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "b (y semi-axis)" 1.0)
        fc (get_real 2 "n (exponent: 2=ellipse, 1=diamond, large→boxy)" 4.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", n=" (rtos fc 2 4))
        xc '(* fa (if (and (< (abs (cos (eval a2))) 1e-9) (> fc 0.0)) 0.0 (if (< (abs (cos (eval a2))) 1e-9) 1e9 (alt_expt (cos (eval a2)) (/ 2.0 fc) T))))
        yc '(* fb (if (and (< (abs (sin (eval a2))) 1e-9) (> fc 0.0)) 0.0 (if (< (abs (sin (eval a2))) 1e-9) 1e9 (alt_expt (sin (eval a2)) (/ 2.0 fc) T))))
        skm '(nil nil T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
