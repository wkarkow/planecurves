Michaelis–Menten Curve

The hyperbolic saturation of enzyme reaction velocity with substrate concentration, together with its double-reciprocal Lineweaver–Burk line.

Michaelis–Menten equation:
\[ v = \dfrac{V_{\max}\, S}{K_{m}+S} \]
Lineweaver–Burk equation:
\[ \dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{K_{m}}{V_{\max}}\,\dfrac{1}{S} + \dfrac{1}{V_{\max}} \]

History and Applications

The Michaelis-Menten equation1,2, named after Leonor Michaelis (1875–1949) and Maud Menten (1879–1960), describes the rate of enzyme-catalyzed reactions as a function of substrate concentration. Michaelis, a German biochemist, and Menten, a Canadian physician-chemist, published the work in 1913, expanding Victor Henri’s 1902 observations on invertase kinetics. Their model assumes rapid, reversible formation of an enzyme-substrate complex followed by irreversible product formation, yielding the hyperbolic relationship shown in the first equation below. At low substrate concentration [S] the initial velocity \(v\) rises nearly linearly; at high [S] it saturates at the maximum velocity \(V_{\max}\). The Michaelis constant \(K_{m}\) equals the substrate concentration at half \(V_{\max}\) and reflects enzyme-substrate affinity.

Hans Lineweaver and Dean Burk introduced the double-reciprocal (Lineweaver-Burk) transformation in 19343, converting the hyperbolic Michaelis-Menten relation into the linear form in the second equation below. This plot of \(\frac{1}{v}\) versus \(\frac{1}{[S]}\) yields a straight line whose y-intercept is \(\frac{1}{V_{\max}}\) and x-intercept is \(-\frac{1}{K_{m}}\), simplifying graphical determination of the kinetic constants with data available at the time.

Real-life applications are widespread in the natural sciences. In biochemistry and pharmacology the equations quantify enzyme efficiency for drug-metabolizing enzymes such as cytochrome P450, enabling prediction of drug clearance rates, dosing regimens, and metabolic drug-drug interactions in clinical practice.4 In medical diagnostics they underpin enzyme assays for liver function tests and disease biomarkers. In biotechnology they guide optimization of industrial enzymes used in food processing, brewing, dairy production, and biofuel manufacture. In environmental engineering the same kinetics model bioremediation processes that employ microbial enzymes to degrade pollutants in soil and water. The framework remains the standard for analyzing enzyme inhibition in research and product development.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:MICHAELIS (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                      divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc p1)
  (setq id "Michaelis"
        iq ""
        dcl "MICHAELIS"
        opt '("Michaelis, parameters" "Michaelis, unknown k" "Lineweaver, parameters" "Lineweaver, unknown k")
        kw 0
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions to division point"
        dct 200
        dpt '(2.0 0.0)
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (cond
   ((eq kw 0)
    (princ "\nMichaelis-Menten: v(S)=Vmax·S/(Km+S), ")
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "\nVmax" 1.0)
          fb (get_real 2 "\nKm" 1.0)
          fc (get_real 6 "\nSmax (end of x-domain)" 10.0)
          yc '(/ (* fa (eval a2)) (+ fb (eval a2)))
          eq_display (strcat "\nVmax=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", Km=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", Smax=" (rtos fc 2 4))))
   ((eq kw 1)
    (princ "\nMichaelis-Menten: solve Km from one point, ")
    (setq fa (get_real 6 "\nVmax" 1.0)
          fc (get_real 6 "\nSmax (end of x-domain)" 10.0)
          p1 (get_boundpt "Pick curve point (S,v)" nil nil basept 0.0 nil nil 0.0 fa nil)
          x1 (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          y1 (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          fb (- (/ (* fa x1) y1) x1)
          eq_display (strcat "\nVmax=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", Km=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", Smax=" (rtos fc 2 4) ", Km solved from point:" "\nS=" (rtos x1 2 4) ", v=" (rtos y1 2 4))
          yc '(/ (* fa (eval a2)) (+ fb (eval a2)))))
   ((eq kw 2)
    (princ "\nLineweaver-Burk:  1/v=(Km/Vmax)(1/S)+1/Vmax, ")
    (setq fa (get_real 6 "\nVmax" 1.0)
          fb (get_real 2 "\nKm" 1.0)
          fc (get_real 6 "\n(1/S)max (end of x-domain)" 10.0)
          eq_display (strcat "\nVmax=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", Km=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", Xmax=" (rtos fc 2 4))
          yc '(+ (* (/ fb fa) (eval a2)) (/ 1.0 fa))))
   ((eq kw 3)
    (princ "\nLineweaver-Burk: solve Km from one point, ")
    (setq fa (get_real 6 "\nVmax" 1.0)
          fc (get_real 6 "\n(1/S)max (end of x-domain)" 10.0)
          p1 (get_boundpt "Pick curve point (S,v)" nil nil basept 0.0 nil nil 0.0 fa nil)
          x1 (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          y1 (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p1)
          fb (* fa (- (/ 1.0 y1) (/ 1.0 fa)) x1)
          eq_display (strcat "\nVmax=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", Km=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", Xmax=" (rtos fc 2 4) ", Km solved from point: S=" (rtos x1 2 4) ", v=" (rtos y1 2 4))
          yc '(+ (* (/ fb fa) (eval a2)) (/ 1.0 fa)))))
  (setq xc '(eval a2)
        paramstep (/ fc divcount)
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:MICHAELIS)
