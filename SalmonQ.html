Salmon Quartic

A plane quartic whose number of real loops, genus, and bitangents change with \(a_{1}\), \(a_{2}\), and \(b\).

Salmon Quartic Curve isotropic Cartesian equation:
\[ (x^{2}-a^{2})^{2}+(y^{2}-a^{2})^{2}=b^{4} \]
Salmon Quartic Curve isotropic parametric equations:
\[ x=\pm\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}\cos t},\quad y=\pm\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}\sin t} \]
Salmon Quartic Curve generalized Cartesian equation:
\[ (x^{2}-a_{1}^{2})^{2}+(y^{2}-a_{2}^{2})^{2}=b^{4} \]
Salmon Quartic Curve generalized parametric equations:
\[ x=\sqrt{a_{2}^{2}+b^{2}\cos t},\quad y=\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+b^{2}\sin t} \]

History and Applications

The Salmon quartic1,2 was presented by George Salmon (1819–1904) in 1852 on page 45 of A Treatise on Higher Plane Curves, his sequel to the earlier Treatise on Conic Sections. Salmon, an Irish mathematician whose textbooks remained standard references for decades, classified many higher-degree plane curves and computed invariants that characterize their geometry. The curve itself is a general quartic of genus 3 when \(b\) differs from both \(a\) and \(a\sqrt[4]{2}\), a decomposed quartic consisting of two ellipses when \(b=a\), and a genus-2 curve when \(b=a\sqrt[4]{2}\). Depending on the relative sizes of \(a_{1}\), \(a_{2}\), and \(b\), it consists of four separate closed loops, two loops, or a single connected component; in certain parameter regimes it attains the maximum of 28 real bitangents possible for any quartic.

No real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences were found. The curve remains of purely theoretical interest as an example in the classification of plane quartics and the study of bitangents.

  (setq id "Salmon Quartic"
        iq ""
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw 0
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions in quadrant"
        dct 500
        dpt nil
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a₁ (x-scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "a₂ (y-scale)" fa)
        fc (get_real 2 "b (scale)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na₁=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", a₂=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fc 2 4)))
  (if (= fa fb)
    (progn
      (princ "\nIsotropic:(x²-a²)²+(y²-a²)²= b⁴")
      (if (<= fc fa)
          (setq fk (* -1.0 pi)
                fl pi)
          (progn
            (setq fd (asin (/ (* fa fa) (* fc fc)))
                  fk (- fd)
                  fl (+ (/ pi 2.0) fd))))
      (setq xc '(alt_expt (if (<= (+ (* fa fa) (* fc fc (cos (eval a2)))) 1e-9) 0.0 (+ (* fa fa) (* fc fc (cos (eval a2))))) 0.5 T)
            yc '(if (<= fc fa) (alt_expt (if (< (abs (sin (eval a2))) 1e-9) (* fa fa) (+ (* fa fa) (* fc fc (sin (eval a2))))) 0.5 T)
                (alt_expt (if (<= (+ (* fa fa) (* fc fc (sin (eval a2)))) 1e-9) 0.0 (+ (* fa fa) (* fc fc (sin (eval a2))))) 0.5 T))))
    (progn
      (princ "\nGeneral: (x²-a₁²)²+(y²-a₂²)²=b⁴")
      (setq ff (max fa fb)
            fg (alt_expt (+ (alt_expt fa 4 nil) (alt_expt fb 4 nil)) 0.25 nil)
            fh (and (> fc ff) (< fc fg)))
      (if fh
          (setq fe (get_keyword 0 "Inner Outer" "Inner or Outer curve?" "Outer"))
          (setq fe nil))
      (if (and fh (eq fe "Inner"))
          (progn
            (setq fi (acos (/ (* -1.0 fb fb) (* fc fc)))
                  fj (asin (/ (* -1.0 fa fa) (* fc fc)))
                  fk (- fi)
                  fl (- pi fj (* 2.0 pi)))
            (princ "\n  (inner closed curve)"))
          (progn
            (setq fk (asin (/ (* -1.0 fa fa) (* fc fc)))
                  fl (if (<= fc fb) (- pi fk) (acos (/ (* -1.0 fb fb) (* fc fc)))))
            (if fh (princ "\n  (outer closed curve)"))))
      (setq xc '(alt_expt (if (<= (+ (* fb fb) (* fc fc (cos (eval a2)))) 1e-8) 0.0 (+ (* fb fb) (* fc fc (cos (eval a2))))) 0.5 T)
            yc '(alt_expt (if (<= (+ (* fa fa) (* fc fc (sin (eval a2)))) 1e-8) 0.0 (+ (* fa fa) (* fc fc (sin (eval a2))))) 0.5 T))))
  (setq paramstep (/ (- fl fk) divcount)
        skm '(nil nil T)
        a1 fk
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
