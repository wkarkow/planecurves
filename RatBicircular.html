Rational Bicircular Quartic

A plane algebraic curve of degree four that passes twice through circular points at infinity and has one additional real singularity at the origin.

Rational Bicircular Quartic Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ (x^{2} + y^{2} - c x - d y)^{2} = a^{2} x^{2} + e b^{2} y^{2} \]
Rational Bicircular Quartic Curve polar equation:
\[ r = c \cdot \cos(\theta) + d \cdot \sin(\theta) \pm \sqrt{a^{2}\cos^{2}(\theta) + e \cdot b^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)} \]

History and Applications

The rational bicircular quartic1,2 is a plane algebraic curve of degree four that passes twice through the circular points at infinity and has one additional real singularity at a finite point (taken here as the origin). It belongs to the family of bicircular quartics, which were systematically classified and studied by John Casey in his 1871 memoir “On Bicircular Quartics.” These curves admit several equivalent geometric constructions: they are the pedals of centered conics with respect to a fixed point, the inverses of conics with respect to a point not on the conic (when the inversion power is zero), the envelopes of circles whose diameters join the fixed point to points on a centered conic, and the cissoids of two circles. Depending on parameters such as the value of e relative to the critical value derived from a, b, and c, the singularity at the origin is acnodal (isolated point), crunodal (node with distinct tangents), or cuspidal. Special cases include the limaçon of Pascal (and its special case, the cardioid) when the deferent conic is a circle, and Booth curves such as the lemniscate of Bernoulli when the fixed point is the center of the conic.

In engineering and the natural sciences, rational bicircular quartics define contour plots of generalized Cassini surfaces used to model full-field primary astigmatism magnitude data in perturbed double-plane symmetric optical systems with circular pupils.3
