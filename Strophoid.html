Strophoid

Given a point \(O\) on a reference line \(L1\), a second point \(F\) off that line, and a second line \(L2\) drawn from \(F\) that intersects the reference line at \(I\), the strophoid is the locus of points \(P\) along \(L2\) where \(IP=IO\) as \(L2\) rotates about \(F\).

Strophoid polar equation:
\[ r = -a \cdot \dfrac{\cos(2\theta - \alpha)}{\cos\theta} \]
Multiple θ strophoid polar equation:
\[ r = -a \cdot \dfrac{\cos\bigl(2\tfrac{m}{n}\theta - \alpha\bigr)}{\cos\bigl(\tfrac{m}{n}\theta\bigr)} \]

History and Applications

The strophoid curve1,2 was studied by Roberval around 1645 as the locus of the focus of a conic section obtained when a plane cutting a cone rotates about the tangent at its vertex. Isaac Barrow (Newton’s professor) examined it in 1670; Jean Bernoulli also investigated the curve. The name “strophoid,” from the Greek strophos meaning string, belt, or braid, was proposed by Enrico Montucci. Quételet studied the general form in 1810 and Chasles contributed later. The right strophoid3 is the symmetric special case (fixed point perpendicular to the reference line); the oblique strophoid is the more general form with angle α between pole and fixed point. The multiple-θ variant extends this by rational multipliers on the angle.

In optics the strophoid is the locus of intersection points of the ray from an observer B to the image A′ of a fixed point A in a mirror rotating about a fixed point O.2 It is also the locus of the foci of certain conics (Quételet focal). In plane kinematics strophoids are circle-point curves, the loci of points on a moving body whose trajectories have instantaneously stationary curvature, appearing in mechanism design and the analysis of Bricard’s flexible octahedra.4 In descriptive geometry they arise as perspective views of particular curve intersections. In structural biology a twisted hyperboloid surface derived from the strophoid approximates the backbone geometry of β-barrels in proteins.5

(defun C:STROPHOID (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                      restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 rval xspan maxdraw fa fb fc fd)
  (setq id "Strophoid"
        iq ""
        dcl "STROPHOID"
        opt '("Oblique Strophoid" "Right Strophoid" "Multiple θ Strophoid")
        kw 0
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (cond
   ((eq kw 0)
    (princ "\nOblique Strophoid: r=-a·cos(2θ-α)/cos(θ)")
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
          fb (* (/ pi 180.0) (get_real 0 "α (degrees)" 30.0))
          fc 1
          fd 1))
   ((eq kw 1)
    (princ "\nRight Strophoid: r=-a·cos(2θ-90°)/cos(θ)")
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
          fb 0.0
          fc 1
          fd 1))
   ((eq kw 2)
    (princ "\nMultiple θ Strophoid: r=-a·cos(2·(m/n)θ-α)/cos((m/n)θ)")
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
          fb (* (/ pi 180.0) (get_real 0 "α (degrees)" 0.0))
          fc (get_integer 2 "m (numerator)" 3)
          fd (get_integer 2 "n (denominator)" 2)
          maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 1e5))
    (prompt (strcat "\n " (itoa (1+ (* divcount fd (if (and (= (rem fc 2) 1) (= (rem fd 2) 1)) 1 2)))) " segments are needed to close the curve."))))
  (setq eq_display (strcat "a=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", α=" (rtos (* fb (/ 180.0 pi)) 2 4) "°, m=" (itoa fc) ", n=" (itoa fd))
        rval '(* (- fa) (/ (cos (- (* 2.0 (* (/ (float fc) (float fd)) (eval a2))) fb)) (avoid_zero (cos (* (/ (float fc) (float fd)) (eval a2))))))
        xc '(* (eval rval ) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval ) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 (/ pi 2.0)
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
