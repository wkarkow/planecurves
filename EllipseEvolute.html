Ellipse Evolute

The locus of the centers of curvature of an ellipse; a stretched astroid.

Ellipse Evolute parametric equations:
\[ x = \dfrac{a^{2}-b^{2}}{a}\cos^{3}(t),\quad y = \dfrac{b^{2}-a^{2}}{b}\sin^{3}(t) \]

History and Applications

The evolute of an ellipse is the locus of its centers of curvature and the envelope of its normals. The study of normals to conic sections, including the ellipse, dates to Apollonius of Perga (c. 262–190 BC) in Book V of his Conics.1,2 With the development of calculus, the evolute received precise treatment as the path traced by the center of curvature. For an ellipse parameterized by \( x = a\cos t \), \( y = b\sin t \), the evolute takes the parametric form given in the equation line above. This curve is a stretched astroid.3 A key property is that the number of normals drawn from a point to the generating ellipse depends on the point’s position relative to the evolute: four normals from any interior point, three from points on the evolute itself (except cusps), and two from exterior points.2

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences include an evolute-based Hough transform method that exploits these geometric properties for robust detection and characterization of elliptical and ellipsoidal shapes in digital images. This technique is used in electron microscopy to analyze polydisperse colloidal particles, in microrheology studies, and in drug-delivery research involving ellipsoidal carriers.4

(defun C:ELLIPSEEVOLUTE (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt
                           xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd)
  (setq id "Ellipse Evolute"
        iq "x=((a²-b²)/a)·cos³(t), y=((b²-a²)/b)·sin³(t)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fc (get_dist 6 "2a (major axis)" 1.0 basept)
        fd (get_dist 6 "2b (minor axis)" 0.5 (list 0.0 (* fc 0.5)))
        fa (/ fc 2.0)
        fb (/ fd 2.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        xc '(* (/ (- (* fa fa) (* fb fb)) fa) (alt_expt (cos (eval a2)) 3 nil))
        yc '(* (/ (- (* fb fb) (* fa fa)) fb) (alt_expt (sin (eval a2)) 3 nil))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
