Freeth Curve

A family of strophoids of a circle with the pole at the center and a fixed point on the circumference.

Freeth’s Nephroid polar equation:
\[ r = a\bigl(1 + 2\sin(\theta/2)\bigr) \]
Generalized polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{a}{n}\dfrac{\sin\bigl(n\theta/(n-1)\bigr)}{\sin\bigl(\theta/(n-1)\bigr)} \]

History and Applications

The Freeth curve, named after the Reverend Thomas J. Freeth (1819–1904)1,2 , an English mathematician and clergyman, was first described by him in a communication to the London Mathematical Society in 1878 titled “The Nephroid, Heptagon, &c.”1,3 Freeth presented it as part of a family of strophoids of a circle, with the pole at the center of the circle and a fixed point on the circumference. The parameter \(n\) generates the family: \(n = 3\) produces the limaçon trisectrix, \(n=4\) yields the kidney-shaped Freeth’s nephroid, and as \(n\) tends to infinity the curve approaches the cochleoid.1,3 A key geometric property of the \(n=4\) case (Freeth’s nephroid) arises at the triple point of self-intersection. A line through the origin parallel to the y-axis intersects the curve again at a point Q such that the angle formed with the x-axis is exactly \( \frac{3\pi}{7} \). This relationship supplies a construction for a regular heptagon that cannot be achieved with compass and straightedge alone.3

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences are documented in two specialized contexts. In robotics kinematics, the nephroid serves as a prescribed trajectory for a 3-RRR planar parallel robot, allowing precise path-tracking studies of position, velocity, acceleration, and jerk.4 In control-systems engineering, the nephroid of Freeth appears as the exact shape of certain Nyquist plots that arise from specific linear transfer functions; engineers use this shape when applying the Nyquist stability criterion to assess closed-loop system margins without further algebraic reduction.5

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:FREETH (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt
                   xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 maxdraw fa fb)
  (setq id "Freeth Curve"
        iq "r=a·sin(nθ/(n-1))/(n·sin(θ/(n-1)))"
        dcl "FREETH"
        opt '("Freeth nephroid" "Limacon trisectrix" "New Values")
        kw 0
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 0 "a (scale): " 1.0))
  (cond
    ((eq kw 0)
       (setq fb 4.0))
    ((eq kw 1)
       (setq fb 3.0))
    ((eq kw 2)
       (setq fb 1.0)
       (while (= fb 1.0)
              (setq fb (get_real 6 "\nn (Loop factor, must not be 1.0, nephroid=4): " 4.0)))))
  (setq eq_display (strcat "\na= " (rtos fa 2 4) ", n= " (rtos fb 2 4))
        rval '(if (eq (sin (/ (eval a2) (- fb 1.0))) 0.0) fa (/ (* fa (sin (/ (* fb (eval a2)) (- fb 1.0)))) (* fb (sin (/ (eval a2) (- fb 1.0))))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 20.0)
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:FREETH)
