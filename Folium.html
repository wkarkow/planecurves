Folium

A collection of polar leaf-shaped curves.

Descartes polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{3a\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\cos^{3}\theta + \sin^{3}\theta} \]
Generalized Descartes polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{3a\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\cos^{b}\theta + \sin^{c}\theta} \]
Kepler polar equation:
\[ r = \cos\theta\,(\sin^{2}\theta - a) \]
Generalized Kepler polar equation:
\[ r = \cos\theta\,(\sin^{b}\theta - a) \]
Rose folium polar equation:
\[ r = a\cos(n\theta) \]
Dürer folium polar equation:
\[ r = a\sin(\theta/2) \]
Parabolic folium polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{a\cos(2\theta) + (b/2)\sin(2\theta)}{\cos^{3}\theta} \]
Generalized parabolic polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{a\cos(c\theta) + (b/2)\sin(c\theta)}{\cos^{d}\theta} \]
Simple folium polar equation:
\[ r = a\cos^{3}\theta \]

History and Applications

Folium curves comprise a family of polar leaf-shaped plots. The Descartes variant was introduced by René Descartes in 1638 in correspondence with Pierre de Fermat as part of an early calculus challenge to determine tangent lines to algebraic curves.1 The Kepler family (bifolium, trifolium, and simple forms) originates from Johannes Kepler’s 1609 work on ovoid shapes and was further studied by Vincenzo Viviani in 1647.2 The Dürer folium is attributed to the geometric constructions of Albrecht Dürer (1471–1528) and represents a special case of the rose curve.3 The parabolic folium was examined by Gérard de Longchamps in 1890.4 The rose family variant is a multi-lobed extension of these polar forms.

Applications in engineering and the natural sciences are limited primarily to mathematical modeling. The Descartes folium has been applied in rock mechanics to relate fracture aperture to strain parameters in brittle rocks under uniaxial compression.5 Kepler’s ovoid variants aid in calculating volumes of solids of revolution in engineering analysis. No specific engineering or scientific applications were identified for the Dürer, parabolic, rose, or simple folium variants beyond their utility in curve plotting and calculus education.
