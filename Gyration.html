Curve of Constant Gyration

Paths of the front and rear wheels of a vehicle that travels at constant speed while the steering angle changes at a constant rate.

Front wheel parametric equations:
\[ x = \frac{a}{k}\int_0^t \cos\Bigl(u + \frac{1-\cos u}{k}\Bigr)\,du,\quad y = \frac{a}{k}\int_0^t \sin\Bigl(u + \frac{1-\cos u}{k}\Bigr)\,du \]
Back wheel parametric equations:
\[ x = \frac{a}{k}\int_0^t \cos\Bigl(\frac{1-\cos u}{k}\Bigr)\cos u\,du,\quad y = \frac{a}{k}\int_0^t \sin\Bigl(\frac{1-\cos u}{k}\Bigr)\cos u\,du \]
Dimensionless parameter:
\[ k = \frac{a\,\omega}{v} \]

History and Applications

The curve of constant gyration is the path traced by the front wheels of a vehicle (a car, or more cleanly a bicycle) that travels at constant speed while the driver turns the steering wheel at a constant angular rate.1 The rear wheels follow a related but distinct path. The fixed distance between front and rear axles is \(a\); the constant steering rate and vehicle speed enter only through the dimensionless parameter \(k = a\omega/v\).1 The curve was examined by Robert Ferréol in 2017 after a question from Franck Rolland, an engineer with the road service of the canton of Vaud.2 Rolland noted that the familiar claim—that a constant steering rate produces a clothoid—is only an approximation, accurate when the steering rate is low relative to the vehicle speed (small \(k\)).2 Ferréol derived the exact parametric integrals for both the front-wheel and rear-wheel trajectories and observed that the front-wheel curve reproduces itself after each full turn of the steering wheel while the rear-wheel curve does so only after a half-turn.1 Animations of the motion were prepared by Alain Esculier.1As \(k\to 0\) the front-wheel path approaches a clothoid.1 For certain discrete values of \(k\) (for example \(k\approx 0.261\)) the curve closes after a finite number of turns.1 The same geometry can be read as a tractrix in which the angle between the “leash” and the tangent is proportional to arc length.1 Note also that there is reflection symmetry through the midpoints of the back wheel curves, regardless of \(k\).

In practice the clothoid remains the standard transition curve for road design precisely because the constant-steering-rate idealization is seldom exact and because the clothoid’s linear curvature-versus-arc-length relation is convenient for construction and for continuous steering.2 The constant-gyration curves supply the exact kinematic paths when that idealization is retained, and they therefore serve as a reference for comparing approximate transition designs, for illustrating off-tracking (the lateral separation between front- and rear-wheel tracks), and for teaching the relationship between steering rate, wheelbase, and path curvature. No further industrial applications of the exact integrals are recorded.

        fe (/ (* fa fd) fb)
        ff (get_keyword 0 "Front Back" "Front or Back curve?" "Front")

        fh (/ (* 2.0 pi) fg)
        fi 0.0
        fj 0.0
        fk 0.0)
  (repeat fg
    (setq fk (+ fk (* 0.5 fh))
          fl (+ fk (/ (- 1.0 (cos fk)) fe))
          fi (+ fi (* (cos fl) fh))
          fj (+ fj (* (sin fl) fh))
          fk (+ fk (* 0.5 fh))))
  (setq fm (atan fj fi)
        fn (* (- fm) (/ 180.0 pi))
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", v=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", ω=" (rtos fc 2 4) " °/s, k=" (rtos fe 2 6) ", " ff)

        fq 0.0

        yc 'fq

        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(progn
              (setq fr fo
                    fo (+ fo paramstep)
                    fs (+ fr (* 0.5 paramstep)))
              (if (eq ff "Front")
                (setq ft (+ fs (/ (- 1.0 (cos fs)) fe))
                      fu (* (/ fa fe) (cos ft))
                      fv (* (/ fa fe) (sin ft)))
                (setq ft (/ (- 1.0 (cos fs)) fe)
                      fu (* (/ fa fe) (cos ft) (cos fs))
                      fv (* (/ fa fe) (sin ft) (cos fs))))
              (setq fp (+ fp (* fu paramstep))
                    fq (+ fq (* fv paramstep)))
              fo)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc)
  (if (and plineents (eq ff "Back")) (command "_.move" plineents "" basept (list (- fa) 0.0)))
  (if (and plineents (= (type plineents) 'PICKSET))
    (progn
      (initget "Yes No")

        (progn
          (setq ss (ssadd)
                i 0)

            (setq e (entnext e)))
