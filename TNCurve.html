TN Constant Curve

The plane curve on which the intersection point T of the tangent with a fixed line remains at constant distance from the intersection point N of the normal with the same line.

TN constant curve differential equation:
\[ y\, y' + \frac{y}{y'} = a \]
TN constant curve parametric equations:
\[ x = a\,(\cos t - \ln(1+\cos t)), \quad y = a\,\sin t \]

History and Applications

The TN constant curve appears in Robert Ferréol’s MathCurve catalogue of conditioned curves.1 It is defined by the geometric condition that the point T (intersection of the tangent with a fixed line) stays at constant distance d from the point N (intersection of the normal with the same fixed line).

No independent historical attribution prior to the MathCurve entry has been located, and no engineering or natural-science applications peculiar to this curve have been identified. The construction is of interest purely as a geometric locus defined by a constant-distance relation between the tangent and normal intercepts on a fixed line.1

References
