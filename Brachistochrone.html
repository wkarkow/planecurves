Brachistochrone

The curve of least-time descent under gravity between two points in a vertical plane; a cycloid segment that also satisfies the tautochrone property for isochronous oscillations.

Brachistochrone parametric equations:
\[ x = r (\theta - \sin\theta),\quad y = -r (1 - \cos\theta) \]

History and Applications

The Brachistochrone problem1,2 (from Greek brachistos meaning “shortest” and chronos meaning “time”) was posed by Swiss mathematician Johann Bernoulli in 1696. He published the challenge in the journal Acta Eruditorum, asking for the curve connecting two points A and B in a vertical plane along which a body, sliding without friction under gravity alone, would descend in the least possible time. The problem drew responses from leading mathematicians of the era, including Isaac Newton, who solved it anonymously in a single evening3. Johann Bernoulli recognized Newton’s solution by its masterful style. Other notable solvers included Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, Guillaume de l’Hôpital, and Jakob Bernoulli, Johann’s older brother. The intense competition, including rivalry between the Bernoulli brothers, helped establish the new field of the calculus of variations. The curve that solves the brachistochrone problem is a cycloid, generated by a point on the circumference of a circle of radius r rolling along a straight line without slipping. The same cycloid also satisfies the tautochrone property: a particle sliding from any point on the curve to the lowest point takes exactly the same time. Christiaan Huygens had earlier discovered this tautochrone property and applied it to improve pendulum clocks. By shaping the path of the pendulum bob along a cycloid using curved “cheeks,” the period became independent of amplitude, leading to more accurate timekeeping.

In engineering, the brachistochrone curve informs the design of roller coaster segments to achieve rapid yet efficient acceleration under gravity. The tautochrone aspect continues to appear in physics education and specialized mechanical systems requiring consistent timing.
