Hourglass Curve

A quartic algebraic curve with a narrow waist at the origin and outward-flaring branches; obtained by transforming the equation of the lemniscate of Gerono.

Hourglass Curve implicit equation:
\[ x^{4} = a^{2}(y^{2} - x^{2}) \]
Hourglass Curve parametric equations:
\[ x = a \cdot \tan(t),\quad y = \dfrac{a \cdot \tan(t)}{\cos(t)} \]
Hourglass Curve polar equation:
\[ r^{2} = -a^{2} \dfrac{\cos(2\theta)}{\cos^{4}(\theta)} \]

History and Applications

The Hourglass Curve is a quartic algebraic curve whose plotted form produces a symmetric shape with a narrow waist at the origin and branches that flare outward. Near the origin the curve behaves approximately like the lines \( y = \pm x \) (slope \(\pm 1\)), while farther out the positive \( x^{4} \) term causes \( |y| \) to grow faster than \( |x| \), creating the characteristic widening that gives the curve its descriptive name. The curve first appeared implicitly in the mathematical literature through transformed lemniscate equations discussed by 19th-century authors such as Gerono and later revisited in modern curve collections (notably MathCurve.com), where it is illustrated as a modification of the equation for the lemniscate of Gerono.1 The name “Hourglass Curve” is due to its visual appearance rather than from classical nomenclature.

No real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences were identified for this particular curve in available mathematical literature. Its geometry is visually suited to modeling sand-timer profiles, optical throat sections, or other symmetric forms with constricted waists and expanding flanks.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:HOURGLASS (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xspan
                      paramstep restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc rval skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 maxdraw fa)
  (setq id "Hourglass Curve"
        iq "r²=-a²·cos(2θ)/cos⁴(θ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 6 "\na (scale)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4))
        rval '(sqrt (max 0.0 (/ (* -1.0 (* fa fa) (cos (* 2.0 (eval a2)))) (avoid_zero (expt (cos (eval a2)) 4.0)))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" (* fa 10.0))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
