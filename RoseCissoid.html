Rose Cissoid Curve

Cissoid of a circle through the origin and a rose of equal amplitude; fills the interior of a cardioid.

RoseCissoid polar equation:
\[ r = a \cdot \bigl(\cos\bigl(\tfrac{p}{q}\theta\bigr) + \cos\theta\bigr) \]
Enclosing cardioid equation:
\[ r = a \cdot (1 + \cos\theta) \]

History and Applications

The RoseCissoid is the cissoid (pole at the origin) of the circle \( r = a\cos\theta \) (diameter \( a \), through the origin) and the rose \( r = a\cos(k\theta) \) of matching amplitude.1,2 The general cissoid construction, dating to Diocles of Carystus (c. 240–180 BC), generates a locus from two curves and a fixed pole: a ray from the pole meets the curves at points \( M_1 \) and \( M_2 \), and the cissoid point \( M \) on the same ray satisfies the vector relation \( \overrightarrow{OM} = \overrightarrow{OM_1} + \overrightarrow{OM_2} \).3 In polar form this reduces exactly to addition of the radial functions. Roses (rhodoneae) were named and studied by Guido Grandi (1671–1742) between 1723 and 1728.4

The family is of modern interest primarily for a geometric observation recorded on MathCurve and credited to Thierry Legay: because \( |\cos(k\theta)| \le 1 \), every member satisfies \( r \le a(1 + \cos\theta) \), with equality wherever \( \cos(k\theta) = 1 \). The cardioid \( r = a(1 + \cos\theta) \) is therefore the outer envelope.5 For rational \( k = p/q \) of sufficiently high order the curves densely fill the cardioid interior. No earlier independent catalogue entry for this specific family has been identified.

No documented applications in engineering or the natural sciences specific to this family were found. The enclosing cardioid itself appears in optics as the catacaustic of a circle with source on the circumference and as the boundary of the central bulb of the Mandelbrot set, but those properties belong to the envelope.6

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:ROSECISSOID (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                        restartflag rval id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 eq_display fa fb fc fe ff)
  (setq id "RoseCissoid Curve"
        iq "r=a·(cos((p/q)θ)+cos θ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_integer 2 "p (integer for (p/q)θ)" 55)
        fc (get_integer 2 "q (integer for (p/q)θ)" 17)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", p=" (itoa fb) ", q=" (itoa fc))
        fe (/ fc (gcd fb fc))
        ff (fix (/ (* (if (and (= (rem (/ fb (gcd fb fc)) 2) 1) (= (rem fe 2) 1)) (* 180 fe) (* 360 fe)) divcount) span))
        divcount (get_integer 2 "divisions to close the curve" ff)
        rval '(* fa (+ (cos (* (/ (float fb) (float fc)) (eval a2))) (cos (eval a2))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
