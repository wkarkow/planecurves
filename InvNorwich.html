Inverse Norwich Spiral

Polar inverse curve with respect to the pole of the Norwich spiral.

Inverse Norwich Spiral parametric equations:
\[ \rho = a \cos^{2} t,\quad \theta = 2t - \tan t,\quad |t| < \pi/2 \]
Curvilinear abscissa:
\[ s = a t \]
Cartesian tangential angle:
\[ \psi = 4t - \tan t + \pi/2 \]
Radius of curvature:
\[ R_{c} = \dfrac{a}{3 - \tan^{2} t} \]
Length:
\[ \pi a \]

History and Applications

The inverse Norwich spiral is the polar inverse, with respect to the pole, of the Norwich spiral. The parent Norwich spiral, a case of the Sturm spiral and a radioid, was studied by Riccati (1712), Euler (1781), Sturm (1857) and Sylvester (1868); Sylvester named it after the 1868 British Association meeting in Norwich at which he presented work on successive involutes of a circle.1,2 The inverse form itself was examined by Christophe Masurel in 2013 in the course of his studies of wheels and grounds under Gregory’s transformation; the designation “inverse Norwich spiral” is a later homemade name given by Robert Ferréol.3,4Its polar parametrization is \(\rho = a\cos^{2} t\), \(\theta=2t-\tan t\), \(|t|<\pi/2\), with curvilinear abscissa \(s=at\) and total length \(\pi a\).4 The ordinary Norwich spiral has the reciprocal radius \(\rho=a/\cos^{2} t\) and the opposite angular sign. The curve has a simple kinematic characterization. When it rolls without slip on a circular road, its hub traces a straight-line tangent to that circle. Conversely, when the same curve rolls on a straight line, its pole traces a cardioid.4,5 These properties place it among the classical wheel–road pairs studied by Masurel and earlier authors. In this AutoLISP implementation, one does not notice the limitations of computation in the full views of these images processed with 18000 segments. However, when zoomed in, those limitations become apparent as the curve tightens.

No independent engineering or scientific applications beyond this kinematic rôle were identified in the sources consulted.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:INVNORWICH (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                      restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb)
  (setq id "Inverse Norwich Spiral"
        iq "ρ=a·cos²(t), θ=2t-tan(t), |t|<π/2"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions per half-turn"
        dct 18000
        dpt nil
        span 180
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_keyword 0 "Fit-curve Discrete" "Fit-curve or Discrete?" "Fit-curve")
        xc '(* fa (expt (cos (eval a2)) 2) (cos (- (* 2.0 (eval a2)) (tan (eval a2)))))
        yc '(* fa (expt (cos (eval a2)) 2) (sin (- (* 2.0 (eval a2)) (tan (eval a2)))))
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4))
        skm (if (eq fb "Fit-curve") '(T T T) '(T T T T))
        a1 0.0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:INVNORWICH)
