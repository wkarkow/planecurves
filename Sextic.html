Rational Sextic

Two indecomposable rational sextics resembling a circle-plus-eight and a circle-plus-cardioid.

Sextic 1 parametric equations:
\[ x = 2\sin 2t,\quad y = \cos t + \cos 3t \]
Generalized Sextic 1 parametric equations:
\[ x = a\cdot\sin\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr),\quad y = \dfrac{a}{2}\bigl[\cos\bigl((\dfrac{p}{q}-1)t\bigr) + \cos\bigl((\dfrac{p}{q}+1)t\bigr)\bigr] \]
Sextic 2 parametric equations:
\[ x = \dfrac{\cos^{3} t}{2\cos^{3} t - 2\cos t + 1},\quad y = \dfrac{\sin 2t\cdot\sin^{2}(t/2)}{2\cos^{3} t - 2\cos t + 1} \]
Generalized Sextic 2a parametric equations:
\[ x = a\cdot\dfrac{\sin\bigl(2\cdot\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr)\sin^{2}\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t/2\bigr)}{2\cos^{3} t - 2\cos t + 1},\quad y = -a\cdot\dfrac{\cos^{3}\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr)}{2\cos^{3} t - 2\cos t + 1} \]
Generalized Sextic 2b parametric equations:
\[ x = a\cdot\dfrac{\sin 2t\cdot\sin^{2}(t/2)}{2\cos^{3}\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr) - 2\cos\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr) + 1},\quad y = -a\cdot\dfrac{\cos^{3}\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr)}{2\cos^{3}\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr) - 2\cos\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr) + 1} \]
Generalized Sextic 2c parametric equations:
\[ x = a\cdot\dfrac{\sin\bigl(2\cdot\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr)\sin^{2}\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t/2\bigr)}{2\cos^{3}\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr) - 2\cos\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr) + 1},\quad y = -a\cdot\dfrac{\cos^{3} t}{2\cos^{3}\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr) - 2\cos\bigl(\dfrac{p}{q}t\bigr) + 1} \]

History and Applications

Robert Ferréol’s MathCurve sextic webpage1 presents a list of rational sextic curves that have been illustrated on this site under their specific names. There are two previously unnamed curves whose sets of equations are given above, along with generalizations here of those equations. Rational sextics are algebraic curves of degree 6 and genus zero. Genus zero means the curve admits a rational parametrization (or a trigonometric parametrization after the Weierstrass substitution \( t = \tan(\theta/2) \)). In the complex projective plane they possess between one and ten singularities that reduce the genus by ten. Only the original curves are sextic, not the generalizations that are of higher powers. The first is an indecomposable rational sextic that nevertheless appears, to the eye, to be the union of a circle and a figure-eight.

The second specimen likewise appears to be composed of a circle and a cardioid. Ferréol records that it arises as the locus of the third vertex of a triangle in which an altitude, a median and an angle bisector are concurrent, the vertex of the altitude being fixed at \( (0,1) \) and the vertex of the median at \( (0,0) \). The triangles themselves were studied by the French civil engineer and geometer Émile Lemoine (1840–1912) in the 1885 volume of Mathesis.1,2 The three coded variants simply move the rational frequency \( p/q \) among the various trigonometric arguments; the common denominator never vanishes. The locus of the concurrency point itself is a rational septic, also listed by Ferréol.3 Lemoine is better known for the symmedian point (the concurrency of the reflections of the medians in the angle bisectors) and for the Lemoine circle, both of which he introduced in the 1870s; the 1885 note on concurrent altitude-median-bisector triangles is a later, narrower contribution to the same “new triangle geometry.”

No engineering or natural-science application peculiar to either of these two rational sextics has been located. They remain catalogue specimens that illustrate the visual and algebraic behaviour of genus-zero sextics and, in the second case, a concrete geometric locus arising from classical triangle geometry.
