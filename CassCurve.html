Cassinian Curve

A plane algebraic curve of degree 2n generalizing the Cassini ovals to n-gon foci.

Cassinian Curve polar equation:
\[ r = a \left( \cos (n \theta) \pm \sqrt{ e^{2 n} - \sin^{2} (n \theta) } \right)^{1/n} \]

History and Applications

The Cassinian curve1,2 is the locus of points P such that the geometric mean of the distances from P to n fixed foci is constant. For n = 2 this reduces to the classic Cassini oval, first investigated in 1680 by Giovanni Domenico Cassini (1625–1712), the Italian-French astronomer and director of the Paris Observatory. Cassini examined the curve while studying the relative motions of the Earth and the Sun, considering whether the Sun might travel around the Earth on such an oval with the Earth at one focus. The general n–focus form was later studied by Serret in 18432. The curve is algebraic of degree 2n and is also known as an isodynamic line1. In the polar formulation used here, the integer parameter n controls the angular frequency of the curve, producing n–fold rotational symmetry. The resulting shapes resemble Cassinian ovals replicated and interwoven around a circle, with lobes evenly spaced at angles 2π/n. This construction represents only a very small and highly idealized subset of possible multi-focus Cassinian-type loci. Arbitrary placement of three or more foci generally produces curves with no closed-form polar representation and with far less symmetry than those shown here.

Real-life applications occur in engineering and the natural sciences3. In nuclear physics, generalized Cassinian ovals model nuclear shapes and the potential-energy surfaces that govern nuclear fission processes3. In radiolocation and acoustics, the geometry describes the detection zones of bistatic radar and sonar systems, where a separated transmitter and receiver define a Cassini-shaped coverage region; this informs the placement of multistatic sensors such as sonobuoys for underwater surveillance3. In industrial engineering the curves guide optimal packing arrangements of objects for storage and transportation3.

(defun C:CASSCURVE (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt xspan restartflag

  (setq id "Cassinian Curve"
        iq "r=a·(cos(nθ)±sqrt(e^(2n)-sin²(nθ)))^(1/n)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"

        dpt nil

        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)

  (cond
    ((< fc 1.0)
     (setq fe (/ (asin (alt_expt fc fb T)) fb)
           ff '(if (<= (eval a2) (* 2.0 fe)) (+ (- fe) (eval a2)) (- (* 3.0 fe) (eval a2)))
           fg '(if (<= (eval a2) (* 2.0 fe)) 1.0 -1.0)

                 (* fb (eval ff))) (/ 1.0 fb) T)) (* fa (alt_expt (+ (cos (* fb (eval ff))) (* (eval fg) (alt_expt fi 0.5 T))) (/ 1.0 fb) T))))
           paramstep (/ (* 4.0 fe) divcount)))
    ((= fc 1.0)
     (setq fe (* 4 fb)
           divcount (* fe (fix (/ (+ divcount fe -1) fe)))
           rval '(if (< (abs (cos (* fb (eval a2)))) 1e-12) 0.0 (* fa (alt_expt (+ (cos (* fb (eval a2))) (alt_expt (- (alt_expt fc (* 2.0 fb) T)
                 (alt_expt (sin (* fb (eval a2))) 2.0 T)) 0.5 T)) (/ 1.0 fb) T)))
           paramstep (/ (* 2.0 pi) divcount)))
    ((> fc 1.0)
     (setq rval '(* fa (alt_expt (+ (cos (* fb (eval a2))) (alt_expt (- (alt_expt fc (* 2.0 fb) T) (alt_expt (sin (* fb (eval a2))) 2.0 T)) 0.5 T)) (/ 1.0 fb) T)))))
