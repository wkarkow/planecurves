Bicuspid Curve

A quartic algebraic plane curve resembling a human bicuspid (premolar) tooth.

Bicuspid implicit equation (standard):
\[ (x^{2} - a^{2})(x - a)^{2} + (y^{2} - a^{2})^{2} = 0 \]
Bicuspid implicit equation (rotated upright for code):
\[ (y^{2} - a^{2})(y + a)^{2} + (x^{2} - a^{2})^{2} = 0 \]

History and Applications

The Bicuspid Curve1 is a quartic algebraic plane curve named for its resemblance to a human bicuspid (premolar) tooth. In the rotated 90° counterclockwise orientation used in this code, it produces the characteristic tooth shape with a broad, smooth upper crown and a sharp downward-pointing central cusp. Although the curve has no single famous discoverer, it appears in modern collections of special plane curves2 and is frequently used in algebraic geometry and curve-tracing exercises to demonstrate implicit quartic equations and the study of cusps and singularities3. It has been documented in mathematical handbooks since at least the mid-20th century.

Real-world applications in engineering and the natural sciences remain very limited. The primary use of the Bicuspid Curve is educational, as a pedagogical example in mathematics courses and as a test case in studies of real algebraic varieties and singularities. No significant practical applications in design, optics, biology, or industry have been documented beyond its visual similarity to a tooth.
