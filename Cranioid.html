Cranioid Curve

A quartic plane curve resembling a skull or eye shape.

Cranioid polar equation:
\[ r = a \cdot \sin(\theta) + b \sqrt{1 - p \cdot \cos^{2}(\theta)} + c \sqrt{1 - q \cdot \cos^{2}(\theta)} \]
Generalized polar equation:
\[ r = a \cdot \sin(\theta) + b \sqrt{1 - p \cdot \cos^{2}(\theta)} + c \sqrt{1 - q \cdot \cos^{d}(\theta)} \]

History and Applications

The Cranioid curve1,2 derives its name from the Greek word κρανίον (kranion), meaning “skull.” It is also known as the eyeball curve. The curve is a quartic plane curve defined in polar coordinates. Standard mathematical references list the equation and examples of its plotted forms but provide no detailed discovery history or attribution to a specific mathematician. As 2DCurves.com2 notes, “It occurs that authors add a term \( [c \sqrt{1−q \cdot cos^2(θ)}] \) to the polar formula of the curve. In my opinion this adds obsolete complexity and parameters to the formula.” In execution, no matter the difference between p and q, the shape created is that of the higher of the two numbers. Therefore, to avoid this, the equation used below has been modified, where d introduces an inequality between the two roots with unexpected results.

No applications in engineering or the natural sciences are documented in primary curve compendia.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:CRANIOID (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep restartflag
                    id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd fe ff)
  (setq id "Cranioid"
        iq "r=a·sin(θ)+b·√(1-p·cos²(θ))+c·√(1-q·cosᵈ(θ))"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fb (get_real 6 "b (horizontal bulge size)" 1.0)
        fc (get_real 6 "c (horizontal bulge size)" 1.0)
        fa (get_boundr 6 "a (vertical height size)" 1.0 nil (+ fb fc) nil nil)
        fd (get_real 6 "d (exponent on cos term)" 2.0)
        fe (get_boundr 6 "p (side flatness)" 0.8 0.0 1.0 T T)
        ff (get_boundr 6 "q (side flatness)" 0.8 0.0 1.0 T T)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", c=" (rtos fc 2 4) ", d=" (rtos fd 2 4) ", p=" (rtos fe 2 4) ", q=" (rtos ff 2 4))
        rval '(+ (* fa (sin (eval a2))) (* fb (sqrt (- 1.0 (* fe (alt_expt (cos (eval a2)) 2 nil))))) (* fc (sqrt (- 1.0 (* ff (alt_expt (cos (eval a2)) fd nil))))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
