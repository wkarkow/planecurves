Ballistic Curve

Trajectory of a projectile under constant gravity with linear drag force proportional to velocity (Stokes regime).

Ballistic Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ y = \frac{b}{a}x + c \ln\left(1 - \frac{x}{a}\right) \]
Ballistic Curve parametric equations:
\[ x = \frac{v_0 \cos\phi_0}{k} (1 - e^{-kt}), \quad y = \left( \frac{v_0 \sin\phi_0}{k} + \frac{g}{k^2} \right) (1 - e^{-kt}) - g t \]

History and Applications

The ballistic curve with linear drag describes the trajectory of a projectile under uniform gravity and a drag force proportional to velocity, \( F_{\text{drag}} = -kv \) (where \( k \) is the drag coefficient, often incorporating mass in simplified models).1 This regime applies at low Reynolds numbers (Re ≪ 1), where viscous forces dominate inertia, leading to Stokes flow.2 Jean Bernoulli, Leonhard Euler, and Adrien-Marie Legendre explored linear resistance in fluid motion during the 1700s.3 George Gabriel Stokes formalized the linear drag law for spheres in 1851 by solving the low-Reynolds-number limit of the Navier–Stokes equations, yielding \( F_{\text{drag}} = 6\pi\eta r v \) (Stokes' law, where \( \eta \) is viscosity, \( r \) is radius).2 This work advanced understanding of creeping flows, though earlier contributions from Newton (quadratic resistance via momentum transfer) laid groundwork for drag modeling. Let \( a = v_0 \cos\phi_0 / k \), \( b = v_0 \sin\phi_0 / k + g / k^2 \), and \( c = g / k^2 \) where initial velocity \( v_0 \), launch angle \( \phi_0 \), drag coefficient \( k \), and gravity \( g \). The curve has a vertical asymptote at \( x = a \). This equation does not apply to larger than microscopic objects. For larger objects, see the Braked Parabola Curve.

Applications abound in engineering and natural sciences: microscopic particles in air or water (pollen grains, oil droplets, fine aerosols, settling dust, microbe motion in fluids), and small water droplets or ice crystals in clouds.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:BALLISTIC (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                      restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd fe ff fg fh fi fj fk)
  (setq id "Ballistic Curve (Linear Drag)"
        iq "x=a(1−e⁻ᵘ), y=b(1−e⁻ᵘ)− c·u, u=k·t"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions to landing point"
        dct 500
        dpt nil
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "v0 (initial speed)" 100.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "φ₀ (launch angle, degrees)" 45.0)
        fc (get_real 2 "k (drag coefficient, 1/s)" 0.25)
        fd (get_real 2 "g (gravity, m/s² downward)" 9.81)
        eq_display (strcat "\nv0=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", φ₀=" (rtos fb 2 4) "°, k=" (rtos fc 2 4) ", g=" (rtos fd 2 4))
        fb (* pi (/ fb 180.0))
        fe (/ fa fc)
        ff (/ fd (* fc fc))
        fg (+ (* fe (sin fb)) ff)
        fh 0.01
        fi (* 0.999 (/ fg ff)))
  (while (> (- fi fh) 1e-9)
    (setq fj (/ (+ fh fi) 2.0)
          fk (- (* fg (- 1.0 (exp (- fj)))) (* ff fj)))
    (if (> fk 0.0)
      (setq fh fj)
      (setq fi fj)))
  (setq paramstep (/ (/ (+ fh fi) 2.0) divcount)
        xc '(* (* fe (cos fb)) (- 1.0 (exp (- (eval a2)))))
        yc '(- (* fg (- 1.0 (exp (- (eval a2))))) (* ff (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0.0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:BALLISTIC)
