Ribaucour Curve

A one-parameter family whose radius of curvature at each point is a constant multiple of the normal distance to the x-axis.

Ribaucour Curve parametric equations:
\[ x = k\,a\int \cos^{k}(u)\,du,\qquad y = a\cos^{k}(t) \]
Defining curvature property:
\[ R_{c} = -k\,N \]

History and Applications

The Ribaucour curve1 takes its name from the French mathematician and engineer Albert Ribaucour (1845–1893). The underlying problem was posed by Jean Bernoulli in 1716 and solved by Brook Taylor in 1717. Ossian Bonnet studied the curve in 1844, and Ribaucour gave a more detailed treatment in 1880. Geometrically the curve’s radius of curvature \(R_{c}\) is proportional to the normal distance \(N\) from the curve to the x-axis: \(R_{c} = -k\,N\) where \(k\) is a constant called the index of the curve. This same property makes the Ribaucour curve the trajectory of a light ray traveling in an inhomogeneous medium according to Fermat’s principle of least time.

The curve reduces to shapes appearing in classical physics and engineering; the catenary for \(k = -1\), the parabola for \(k = -2\), the cycloid (or brachistochrone or curve of fastest descent under gravity) for \(k = 2\), and the circle (a geodesic in the Poincaré half-plane model of hyperbolic geometry) for \(k = 1\). No additional applications were found.
