Polygasteroid

A polar generalization of the focus–directrix conics obtained by replacing θ with nθ.

Polygasteroid Curve polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{a}{1 + e \cdot \cos(n\theta)} \]

History and Applications

The Polygasteroid curve, named by Gino Loria in 1930 in his work on special algebraic and transcendental plane curves, generalizes the family of conic sections in polar form by changing θ to nθ. When n = 1, the equation reduces to the standard polar form of the ellipse (when eccentricity e < 1), parabola (e = 1), and hyperbola (e > 1). Earlier references to curves with n antinodes or n overhangs appear in Laboulaye’s 1849 work. It is also known as the generalized conic (Mamikon, 2012). Polygasteroids are inverses of the conchoids of roses. They also arise as the conical projections of cylindric sine waves onto a plane perpendicular to the cylinder axis (with the center on the axis).1

In mechanical engineering, polygasteroids serve as mating profiles for non-circular gears. When one gear profile is an ellipse with its hub at a focus, the conjugate mating profile that maintains proper rolling contact without slipping follows the polygasteroid equation, enabling mechanisms with prescribed variable angular velocity ratios.2

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:POLYGASTEROID (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                          restartflag rval id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 eq_display fa fb fc)
  (setq id "Polygasteroid"
        iq "r=a/(1+e·cos(nθ))"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "e (eccentricity)" 0.25)
        fc (get_real 6 "n (θ multiplier)" 3.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", e=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", n=" (rtos fc 2 4))
        rval '(/ fa (avoid_zero (+ 1.0 (* fb (cos (* fc (eval a2)))))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 5.0)
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
