Epitrochoid Evolute

The locus of the centers of curvature of an epitrochoid.

Epitrochoid Evolute parametric equations:
\[ x=\dfrac{a \cdot c (a+b)\bigl(c-b\cos(\frac{a\theta}{b})\bigr)\cos\theta+b\bigl(b-c\cos(\frac{a\theta}{b})\bigr)\cos\bigl(\frac{(a+b)\theta}{b}\bigr)}{b^{3}+(a+b)c^{2}-b(a+2b)c\cos(\frac{a\theta}{b})} \]
\[ y=\dfrac{a \cdot c (a+b)\bigl(c-b\cos(\frac{a\theta}{b})\bigr)\sin\theta+b\bigl(b-c\cos(\frac{a\theta}{b})\bigr)\sin\bigl(\frac{(a+b)\theta}{b}\bigr)}{b^{3}+(a+b)c^{2}-b(a+2b)c\cos(\frac{a\theta}{b})} \]

History and Applications

The epitrochoid evolute1 is the locus of the centers of curvature of an epitrochoid, a roulette traced by a point attached to a circle of radius b rolling externally about a fixed circle of radius a with the tracing point offset by distance h from the center of the rolling circle. The underlying epitrochoid was first depicted by Albrecht Dürer in 1525 in his book Instruction in Measurement with Compasses and Straight Edge2, where construction lines for related curves resembled spider webs. Subsequent mathematicians including Girard Desargues (1640), Christiaan Huygens (1679), Gottfried Leibniz, Isaac Newton (1686), and the Bernoulli family investigated roulette curves of this family3. No separate historical account credits a specific individual with first deriving or naming the evolute of the epitrochoid; it follows directly from the general definition of an evolute as the envelope of the normal lines to the parent curve. The radius of curvature for a parametric curve can be computed via basic differential calculus concepts (first and second derivatives with respect to the parameter), and the evolute point is displaced from the original point along the normal by that radius value. The epitrochoid evolute, while rich, was likely too complex for hand calculation or graphic construction in pre-calculus times. It is only with modern algebra and calculus tools that the full parametric form becomes feasible to derive or analyze. In the equations above, \(a\) = fixed radius, \(b\) = rolling radius, \(c\) = offset of the parent epitrochoid.

No real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences were identified for the epitrochoid evolute.
