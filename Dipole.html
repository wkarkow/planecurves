Dipole Curve

Equipotential lines of an electrostatic dipole or orthogonal trajectories of magnetic dipole field lines.

Dipole Curve polar equation:
\[ r^{2} = a^{2} \cos(\theta) \]
Dipole Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ (x^{2} + y^{2})^{3} = a^{4} x^{2} \]

History and Applications

The dipole curve, also known as the curve of equal attraction or [John] Playfair curve, represents the equipotential lines surrounding an electrostatic dipole formed by two equal and opposite charges separated by an infinitesimal distance1. It is a special case of Clairaut’s curve. The curve is orthogonal to the electric field lines of the dipole1,2. In magnetostatics it likewise supplies the orthogonal trajectories to the field lines of a magnetic dipole. This property has direct relevance in modeling the Earth’s magnetic field1,3. It is also the meridian of the surface of equal attraction and thus applies to two-body gravitational potentials in the dipole approximation1. Mathcurve.com points out that the field lines perpendicular to the dipole curves are the double eggs.

Real-life applications exist in engineering and the natural sciences. In electromagnetism and potential theory the curve defines equipotential surfaces used in the design of electrostatic instruments, sensors, and high-voltage equipment. In geophysics the analogous curves aid studies of planetary magnetic fields as well as surfaces of equal gravitational or electrostatic attraction1.

(defun C:DIPOLE (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo
                   paramstep restartflag rval id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 eq_display fa)
  (setq id "Dipole Curve"
        iq "r²=a²·cos(θ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 180
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4))
        rval '(* fa (if (<= (cos (eval a2)) 1e-9) 0.0 (alt_expt (cos (eval a2)) 0.5 nil)))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(nil T T T)
        a1 (- (/ pi 2.0))
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
