Dumbbell Curve

A family of closed two-lobed plane curves.

Dumbbell Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ a^{4} y^{2} = x^{4}(a^{2} - x^{2}) \]
Dumbbell Curve polar equation (classical):
\[ x = a \sin\theta,\quad y = a \sin^{2}\theta \cdot \cos\theta \]
Generalized polar equation:
\[ x = a \sin^{b}\theta,\quad y = a \sin^{c}\theta \cdot \cos^{d}\theta \]

History and Applications

The Dumbbell curve1 is described in Wiktionary as “Any of a family of closed curves having two lobes; they satisfy the general equation \( y^{2} = a^{2}(x^{4} - x^{6}) \).”2 The polar equivalent can be generalized and the shape varied with positive real exponents \( b \), \( c \), and \( d \). When \( b = 1 \), \( c = 2 \), and \( d = 1 \), the curve reduces to the classical Eight curve (also called the Lemniscate of Gerono), a quartic algebraic curve whose Cartesian form is \( x^{4} = a^{2}(x^{2} - y^{2}) \).3 French mathematician Camille-Christophe Gerono (1799–1891) studied this specific case in detail during the 19th century. Earlier examinations of similar figure-eight loci appear in the work of Grégoire de Saint-Vincent (1647) and Gabriel Cramer (1750).4 The more general parametric form belongs to the broader family of trigonometric curves catalogued in standard references for plane algebraic curves; no single discoverer is recorded for the parameterized exponents beyond the base case.

Real-life applications exist in engineering and the natural sciences. In kinematics, mechanical linkages have been designed to trace the path of the Lemniscate of Gerono exactly5; these devices serve in mechanism analysis, demonstration models, and the study of constrained motion. In physics, analogous figure-eight trajectories appear in the modeling of certain periodic orbits and phase-space diagrams.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:DUMBBELL (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt xstep restartflag
                     id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd maxdraw)
  (setq id "Dumbbell Curve"
        iq "x=a·sinᵇ(θ), y=a·sinᶜ(θ)·cosᵈ(θ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "b (exponent on sin for x-component)" 1.0)
        fc (get_real 2 "c (exponent on sin for y-component)" 2.0)
        fd (get_real 2 "d (exponent on cos for y-component)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", c=" (rtos fc 2 4) ", d=" (rtos fd 2 4))
        xc '(* fa (alt_expt (sin (eval a2)) fb nil))
        yc '(* fa (alt_expt (sin (eval a2)) fc nil) (alt_expt (cos (eval a2)) fd nil))
        maxdraw (if (or (< fb 0) (< fc 0) (< fd 0)) (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 1e5))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
