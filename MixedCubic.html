Mixed Cubic

A rational cubic with an isolated point at the origin, the cissoid of a straight line and a parabola.

Mixed Cubic Curve implicit equation:
\[ (x - a)y^{2} = b x^{2} \]
Mixed Cubic Curve polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{a}{\cos(\theta)} + \dfrac{b\cdot\cos(\theta)}{\sin^{2}(\theta)} \]

History and Applications

The mixed cubic, also known as the Longchamps curve or cubic mixte, was studied and named by the French mathematician Gaston Albert Gohierre de Longchamps (1842–1906) in 1890.1 De Longchamps contributed to synthetic geometry and is also known for the de Longchamps point of a triangle. This rational cubic curve has an isolated point at the origin. It can be generated geometrically as the cissoid of a straight line and a parabola: the locus of point M on a variable line through the origin that intersects the parabola at P' and the line at Q' such that MP' = MQ'. It possesses both a linear asymptote and a parabolic asymptote.

No specific real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences are documented for the mixed cubic in standard mathematical references on plane curves.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:MIXEDCUBIC (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                       restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display eq_prompt rval xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb)
  (setq id "Mixed Cubic (Longchamps)"
        iq "r=a/cos(θ)+b·cos(θ)/sin²(θ)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 180
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 0 "a (x-shift)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 0 "b (scale)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        rval '(+ (/ fa (avoid_zero (cos (eval a2)))) (/ (* fb (cos (eval a2))) (avoid_zero (* (sin (eval a2)) (sin (eval a2))))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 50)
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:MIXEDCUBIC)
