Weibull Distribution

Two-parameter lifetime model drawn as four Cartesian graphs: probability density, cumulative distribution, quantile (inverse CDF), and hazard (failure rate).

Weibull Distribution Probability Density Function equation:
\[ y = \dfrac{k}{\lambda}\left(\dfrac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{k-1} e^{-(x/\lambda)^{k}} \]
Weibull Distribution Cumulative Distribution Function equation:
\[ y = 1 - e^{-(x/\lambda)^{k}} \]
Weibull Distribution Quantile Function (inverse CDF) equation:
\[ y = \lambda\left(-\ln(1 - p)\right)^{1/k} \]
Weibull Distribution Hazard Function (failure rate) equation:
\[ y = \dfrac{k}{\lambda}\left(\dfrac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{k-1} \]

History and Applications

The Weibull distribution is named after the Swedish engineer and mathematician Waloddi Weibull (1887–1979).1 He presented a detailed treatment of the distribution in 1939 while studying the strength of materials and fatigue phenomena. Although a similar functional form had been applied earlier by Rosin and Rammler (1933) to particle-size distributions, Weibull emphasized its usefulness for modeling “weakest-link” failures in engineering components.2 He brought the distribution to wider attention in the English-speaking technical community with his 1951 paper “A Statistical Distribution Function of Wide Applicability,” published in the ASME Journal of Applied Mechanics.3 The paper initially met with skepticism from statisticians, but practical adoption was advanced by Dorian Shainin, Leonard Johnson, and the U.S. Air Force, which funded further development of Weibull methods for aircraft reliability through the 1970s.4 The distribution is mathematically identical to the Type III extreme-value distribution for minima, a relationship clarified by Gumbel.5

In engineering the Weibull distribution has become the standard model for time-to-failure and lifetime data. The shape parameter \(k\) (commonly denoted \(\beta\)) has direct physical interpretation: values less than 1 indicate early or infant-mortality failures, \(k = 1\) corresponds to random (constant-hazard) failures, and values greater than 1 indicate wear-out or aging mechanisms.6 This interpretability, together with straightforward graphical plotting techniques on Weibull paper, enables engineers to identify dominant failure modes, forecast remaining life, plan preventive maintenance, and perform warranty and life-cycle cost analyses with relatively small data sets.4 The distribution is routinely applied to mechanical components such as bearings and gears, electronic assemblies, and structural elements whose failure is governed by the weakest-link principle.7 In the wind-energy industry the two-parameter Weibull distribution is the accepted model for characterizing wind-speed variability; fitted parameters are used to estimate annual energy production, select turbine designs, and assess site suitability.8 The same distribution appears in materials science for brittle fracture strength and in medical and actuarial statistics for survival and time-to-event modeling.

(defun C:WEIBULL (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                   divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb skm)
  (setq id "Weibull Distribution"
        iq ""
        dcl "WEIBULL"
        opt '("PDF" "CDF" "Quantile" "Hazard")
        kw 0
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions to x-span of division point"
        dct 500
        dpt '(5.0 0.0)
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (princ (cond ((= kw 0) "\nPDF equation: f(x)=(k/λ)·(x/λ)^(k-1)·e^{-(x/λ)^k}")
               ((= kw 1) "\nCDF equation: F(x)=1-e^{-(x/λ)^k}")
               ((= kw 2) "\nQuantile equation: Q(p)=λ·(-ln(1-p))^(1/k)")
               ((= kw 3) "\nHazard equation: h(x)=(k/λ)·(x/λ)^(k-1)")))
  (setq fa (get_real 2 "k (shape )" 5.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "λ (x-scale)" 2.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\nk=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", λ=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        paramstep (/ xspan divcount))
  (cond ((= kw 0)
         (setq xc '(eval a2)
               yc '(* (/ fa fb) (alt_expt (/ (max (eval a2) 1e-15) fb) (- fa 1.0) nil) (exp (* -1.0 (alt_expt (/ (eval a2) fb) fa nil))))))
        ((= kw 1)
         (setq xc '(eval a2)
               yc '(- 1.0 (exp (* -1.0 (alt_expt (/ (eval a2) fb) fa nil))))))
        ((= kw 2)
         (setq paramstep (/ 1.0 divcount)
               xc '(eval a2)
               yc '(* fb (alt_expt (* -1.0 (log (max (- 1.0 (eval a2)) 1e-12))) (/ 1.0 fa) nil))))
        ((= kw 3)
         (setq xc '(eval a2)
               yc '(* (/ fa fb) (alt_expt (/ (max (eval a2) 1e-9) fb) (- fa 1.0) nil)))))
  (setq skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
