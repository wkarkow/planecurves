Atom Spiral

A polar spiral that winds infinitely many times around a limiting circle of radius a/b, approaching it asymptotically without ever reaching it, while possessing a radial asymptote at θ = c/b.

Atom Spiral polar equation:
\[ r = \frac{\theta}{\theta - a} \]
Atom Spiral generalized polar equation:
\[ r = \frac{a \theta}{b \theta - c} \]

History and Applications

The Atom Spiral, also known as the atomic spiral, was named by the Belgian mathematician Annie van Maldeghem in connection with the Matomium symposium held in Brussels in 2002.1 It belongs to the family of polar spirals documented in collections of named two-dimensional curves.2

The polar curve has two asymptotes. As the polar angle θ tends to infinity, the radius r approaches a/b. The spiral winds infinitely many times around a circle of that radius, approaching it arbitrarily closely without reaching it. There is also a straight-line radial asymptote at θ = c/b, where the denominator becomes zero and the curve has a singularity. The basic form is obtained when the parameters are set such that a = 1 and b = 1.

No documented real-life applications of the Atom Spiral in engineering or the natural sciences have been identified.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:ATOMSPIRAL (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                       restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc rval)
  (setq id "Atom Spiral"
        iq "r=a·θ/(b·θ-c)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw 0
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 1440
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "b (larger = tighter winding)" 1.0)
        fc (get_real 2 "c (larger = looser winding)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", c=" (rtos fc 2 4))
        rval '(/ (* fa (eval a2)) (avoid_zero (- (* fb (eval a2)) fc)))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:ATOMSPIRAL)
