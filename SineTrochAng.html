Sine Trochoid with Angled Offset

Angled-offset variation on the sine trochoid. The offset direction makes a constant angle \(\beta\) with the unit tangent.

Sine Trochoid with Angled Offset parametric equations:
\[ \mathbf{r}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}t\\ \sin t\end{pmatrix}+a'\,\mathbf{V}(t)+d'\,\mathbf{U}(t) \]
Unit tangent, angled offset, and rotated tracer:
\[ \mathbf{T}(t)=\dfrac{(1,\,\cos t)}{\sqrt{1+\cos^{2}t}},\qquad \mathbf{V}(t)=\pm R(\beta)\,\mathbf{T}(t),\qquad \phi=\dfrac{s}{a'},\qquad \mathbf{U}(t)=\mathrm{rot}(\phi)\,\mathbf{V}(t) \]
Arc length and input scaling:
\[ s=\int\sqrt{1+\cos^{2}t}\,dt,\qquad s_{\text{period}}\approx 7.640395578055424,\qquad k=\dfrac{s_{\text{period}}}{2\pi}\approx 1.21600672342498 \]
Scaled radius and tracer distance:
\[ a'=k\cdot a,\qquad d'=k\cdot d \]

History and Applications

This is an angled-offset modification of the sine trochoid curve constructed on the base curve \(y=\sin t\). The direction from the base point to the “center” is the unit vector \(\mathbf{V}\) obtained by rotating the unit tangent \(\mathbf{T}\) through a fixed angle \(\beta\) (degrees). When \(\beta=90^\circ\) the vector \(\mathbf{V}\) coincides with the unit normal, the construction reduces to the classical no-slip sine trochoid, and the page recovers the ordinary sine-trochoid plots. For every other value of \(\beta\) the center does not travel on the geometric normal of the sine wave, instantaneous contact with the base is lost, and the curve is not a roulette of a circle rolling without slip on \(y=\sin x\). The same \(k\)-scaling used on the related sine-trochoid pages is retained so that the illustration values of \(a\) and \(d\) have identical meaning. The finished curve is reflected across the \(x\)-axis to match the orientation of the parent images. The classical theory of roulettes and trochoids dates to the seventeenth-century study of the cycloid and its variants; the sinusoidal base itself is a modern illustrative case.1 Related trochoidal paths appear in the kinematic analysis of mechanisms and in rotary pumps and engines.2,3

No applications specific to a non-normal angled offset on a sinusoidal base were located. The family is a geometric generalization; only the special case \(\beta=90^\circ\) is a classical trochoid.

  2. E. H. Lockwood, A Book of Curves (Cambridge University Press, 1961) (classical treatment of roulettes and trochoids)
