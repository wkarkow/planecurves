Curved Star

A CAD construction that assembles n circular arcs into rotationally symmetric, star-like or flower-like patterns.

Curved Star construction:
\[ \text{Circle centers: } C_k = \left( a \cos\left(\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right), a \sin\left(\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right) \right), \quad k = 0,1,\dots,n-1 \]
Included arc angles:
\[ \theta = \operatorname{atan2}(P_y - C_{0y}, P_x - C_{0x}) \]

History and Applications

The Curved Star curve is a CAD construction that assembles n circular arcs into rotationally symmetric, star-like or flower-like patterns. It is generated by drawing the arcs of n equal circles whose centers lie at equal radial distance a from a common origin in a regular polar array. When a is set to the special default value R / (2 sin(π/n)), where k = n/2 for odd n, the inner arcs (those lying closer to the origin) connecting the outer intersection points of adjacent circles form a Reuleaux polygon1, a curve of constant width2, for odd n. For n = 2 the figure reduces to the vesica piscis or fish bladder3, the lens-shaped region common to two intersecting circles. For n = 3 it creates the triquetra, formed from three intersecting vesica piscis curves.4 These special cases inherit the defining property of constant width: the distance between any pair of parallel supporting lines is invariant. By Barbier’s theorem, every curve of constant width with the same width w has perimeter exactly πw, identical to that of a circle of diameter w.

Although the Curved Star construction itself is modern, the curves it produces when a has the default value have a long history. The concept of curves of constant width, which Franz Reuleaux later called orbiforms, was studied as early as the eighteenth century by Leonhard Euler in his 1771 paper (published 1781) De curvis triangularibus.5 The classic Reuleaux triangle (n = 3) is named after the German engineer and mathematician Franz Reuleaux (1829–1905), who analyzed its kinematic properties and employed it in mechanism design in his influential 1875 treatise The Kinematics of Machinery.6 Reuleaux polygons generalize the triangle to any odd number of sides; each side is a circular arc centered at a vertex of the underlying regular polygon, spanning to the appropriate non-adjacent vertices so that the width remains constant.

Real-life applications in engineering and natural sciences are well documented. In manufacturing, Reuleaux-based drill bits (the Watts square-hole drill, developed in the early twentieth century) produce nearly square holes with rounded corners for specialized fasteners and sockets.7 Manhole covers of constant width cannot fall through their openings when rotated, a safety feature used in utility infrastructure.8 Certain coins, such as the British 20p and 50p pieces, employ constant-width shapes so they function reliably in vending machines and coin validators regardless of orientation. In mechanical engineering, constant-width rotors and cams appear in rotary engines and mechanisms that require smooth contact between parallel surfaces.9

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:CURVEDSTAR (/ basept xaxisangle cd os fa fb fc fd fe ff fg fh fi fj fk fl fm fn fo fp fq fr fs ft fu fv fw fx fy fz ga gb gd ge gf
                       gg gh gi gj gk gl gm gn go gp gq gr gs gt gu gv gw)
  (setq cd (getvar "cmdecho")
        os (getvar "osmode"))
  (setvar "cmdecho" 0)
  (setvar "osmode" 0)
  (setq basept (get_point 0 "New origin (symmetry center)" '(0.0 0.0) nil)
        xaxisangle (get_angle 0 "New X axis in degrees" 0.0 basept)
        fa (get_real 2 "R (radius of each circle)" 2.0)
        fb (get_integer 6 "n (number of arcs/circles, >=2)" 3)
        fc (/ fa (sin (/ pi (float fb))))
        fd (fix (/ fb 2))
        fe (/ fa (* 2.0 (sin (/ (* pi (float fd)) (float fb)))))
        ff (get_boundr 0 (strcat "a (radial distance to centers) < " (rtos fc 2 4)) fe 0.0 fc nil nil))
  (princ (strcat "\nCurved Star: R=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", a=" (rtos ff 2 4) ", n=" (itoa fb)))
  (if (tblsearch "ucs" "CS_TEMP")
      (command "ucs" "delete" "CS_TEMP"))
  (command "ucs" "save" "CS_TEMP")
  (command "ucs" "new" "origin" basept)
  (command "ucs" "new" "z" (angtos xaxisangle))
  (setq fg (/ (* 2.0 pi) (float fb))
        fh (* ff (cos fg))
        fi (* ff (sin fg))
        fj (- fh ff)
        fk fi
        fl (sqrt (+ (* fj fj) (* fk fk)))
        fm (/ fj fl)
        fn (/ fk fl)
        fo (* 2.0 ff (sin (/ pi (float fb))))
        fp (sqrt (max 0.0 (- (* fa fa) (* fo fo 0.25))))
        fq (+ ff (* (/ fo 2.0) fm))
        fr (* (/ fo 2.0) fn)
        fs (+ fq (* fp (- fn)))
        ft (+ fr (* fp fm))
        fu (+ fq (* fp fn))
        fv (- fr (* fp fm))
        fw (sqrt (+ (* fs fs) (* ft ft)))
        fx (sqrt (+ (* fu fu) (* fv fv))))
  (if (> fw fx)
    (progn (setq fy fs fz ft) (setq ga fu gb fv))
    (progn (setq fy fu fz fv) (setq ga fs gb ft)))
  (setq ga fy gb (- fz)
        gd ff ge 0.0
        gf (angle (list gd ge) (list ga gb))
        gg (angle (list gd ge) (list fy fz))
        gh (rem (+ (- gg gf) (* 2.0 pi)) (* 2.0 pi))
        gi (+ gf (/ gh 2.0))
        gi (if (> gi (* 2.0 pi)) (- gi (* 2.0 pi)) gi)
        gj (+ gd (* fa (cos gi)))
        gk (+ ge (* fa (sin gi)))
        gl (sqrt (+ (* gj gj) (* gk gk)))
        gm (- (* 2.0 pi) gh)
        gn (rem (+ gf (- (/ gm 2.0))) (* 2.0 pi))
        gn (if (< gn 0.0) (+ gn (* 2.0 pi)) gn)
        go (+ gd (* fa (cos gn)))
        gp (+ ge (* fa (sin gn)))
        gq (sqrt (+ (* go go) (* gp gp))))
  (if (< gl gq)
    (progn (setq gr ga gs gb gt fy gu fz))
    (progn (setq gr fy gs fz gt ga gu gb)))
  (command "arc" "c" (list gd ge) (list gr gs) (list gt gu))
  (setq gv (entlast)
        gw (ssadd gv))
  (command "array" gw "" "polar" "0,0" (itoa fb) "360" "Y")
  (command "ucs" "restore" "CS_TEMP")
  (command "ucs" "delete" "CS_TEMP")
  (setvar "osmode" os)
  (setvar "cmdecho" cd)
  (princ "\nCurved Star complete."))
(C:CURVEDSTAR)
