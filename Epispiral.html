Epispiral

A polar curve r = a sec(nθ) that is the polar inverse of a rose curve.

Epispiral polar equation:
\[ r = a \cdot \sec(n\theta) \]

History and Applications

The epispiral, also known as Cotes' spiral1,2, is a plane curve in polar coordinates. It was studied by the English mathematician Roger Cotes (1682–1716) in 1722. Cotes, who worked closely with Isaac Newton, explored central force problems in classical mechanics. The curve later received attention from Aubry in 1895. Under an attractive or repulsive central force proportional to the inverse cube of the distance from the origin, particle trajectories follow Cotes' spirals, of which the epispiral is one family.3 While inverse-square forces (such as gravity) produce conic sections, the inverse-cube law yields these spirals.3 The epispiral is mathematically the polar inverse of a rose curve with respect to the pole.1 For particular values of n the curve takes on named forms matching the dialog options: n = 2 produces the crosscurve, n = 3 the rectangular trefoil, n = 1/2 the trisectrix of Delanges, and n = 1/3 the trisectrix of MacLaurin.2 Geometrically, epispirals arise as the intersection of a plane perpendicular to the axis of a sinusoidal cone and as the projection onto a plane of geodesics on a cone of revolution.1

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences are scarce because inverse-cube central forces are not commonly encountered in macroscopic physics. The curve remains primarily of theoretical interest in mathematical physics for modeling orbital motion under specific force laws.1,3

; EPISPIRAL.LSP - started 8/6/25

(defun C:EPISPIRAL (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt
                      xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb rval)
  (setq id "Epispiral"
        iq "r=a·sec(nθ)"
        dcl "EPISPIRAL"
        opt '("Crosscurve" "rectangular Trefoil" "Trisectrix of Delanges" "Trisectrix of MacLaurin" "New Values")
        kw 0
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_dist 2 "a (radius of inner circle)" 1.0 basept))
  (cond
   ((eq kw 0)
	    (setq fb 2.0))
   ((eq kw 1)
	    (setq fb 3.0))
   ((eq kw 2)
	    (setq fb 0.5))
   ((eq kw 3)
      (setq fb (/ 1.0 3.0)))
   ((eq kw 4)						; New Values
     (setq fb (get_real 2 "n Number of branches (2x if even)" (/ 2.0 3.0)))))
  (setq maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" 50)
        eq_display (strcat "\na= " (rtos fa 2 4) ",b= " (rtos fb 2 4))       
        rval '(* fa (sec (* fb (eval a2))))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
