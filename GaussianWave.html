Gaussian Wave Packet

A localized oscillatory waveform formed by a Gaussian envelope modulating a sinusoidal carrier.

Gaussian Wave Packet Cartesian equation:
\[ y = A \, e^{-\dfrac{(x-x_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}} \cos\bigl(k(x-x_{0})+\phi\bigr) \]

History and Applications

The Gaussian wave packet1,2 is a localized wave disturbance formed by a Gaussian envelope modulating a sinusoidal carrier. Wave packet concepts date to the mid-19th century, with William Rowan Hamilton proposing group velocity in 1839 and Lord Rayleigh providing a complete treatment in his 1877 Theory of Sound. Erwin Schrödinger introduced wave packets in 1926 shortly after publishing his wave equation, using them to represent localized quantum states. Charles Galton Darwin analyzed the time evolution of a free Gaussian wave packet for an unbound electron under the Schrödinger equation in 1927. The Gaussian form is analytically tractable because its Fourier transform is another Gaussian, and it saturates the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.

In engineering and the natural sciences, Gaussian wave packets model dispersive propagation and particle localization. In quantum mechanics they serve as initial conditions for simulations of free-particle spreading, scattering, and tunneling.3 In optics they underpin Gaussian beam profiles for laser propagation in precision machining, fiber-optic systems, and medical imaging devices. In medical ultrasound they shape diagnostic pulses to enhance axial resolution in vascular imaging.
