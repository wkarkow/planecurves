Monomolecular Curve

The saturating first-order response approach to chemical equilibrium.

Monomolecular differential equation:
\[ \dfrac{dy}{dt} = k(A - y) \]
Monomolecular Curve equation:
\[ y = A \cdot \left(1 - e^{-k(t-t_{0})}\right) \]

History and Applications

The monomolecular curve describes processes in which the rate of change is proportional to the distance remaining to an asymptotic maximum. It is the solution to the first-order differential equation \(\frac{dy}{dt}=k(A-y)\), a concept accessible through basic calculus as the instantaneous rate of approach diminishing linearly toward zero. The monomolecular curve occupies a central position among saturating growth and response models. Other models include (1) exponential decay that describes the complementary quantity, the remaining gap to equilibrium, decaying toward zero; (2) logistic and Gompertz curves that introduce feedback or time-varying rates and therefore depart from true first-order kinetics; (3) Weibull (stretched exponential) and Avrami models that generalize the monomolecular form by incorporating geometry, nucleation, or distributed time scales; and (4) Michaelis–Menten saturation that describes a similar asymptotic shape but as a function of input rather than time. For this reason, the monomolecular curve is often used as a reference model: when experimental data deviate from it, the deviation signals the presence of additional mechanisms. German agronomist Eilhard Mitscherlich first proposed this functional form in 1909 to model crop-yield response to fertilizer application, thereby formalizing the Law of Diminishing Returns (or Law of Diminishing Increments) in agriculture.1 Mitscherlich generalized the equation to other growth factors beyond fertilizers. An earlier biological application appears in Weber’s 1891 study of tree growth.2 In chemistry the identical form arises from the law of mass action applied to first-order reactions.

Real-life applications abound in engineering and the natural sciences. In agriculture the model quantifies yield increases from added nutrients and guides fertilizer recommendations for optimal production.1,3 In forestry and plant science it describes height, diameter, or biomass accumulation in trees and crops over time.2 In animal science the mathematically equivalent Brody growth function predicts body-weight gain in livestock as animals approach mature size. In plant pathology the monomolecular model fits disease-progress curves, where the infection rate depends on the proportion of healthy tissue remaining.4 In chemical engineering the same equation simulates approach to equilibrium in titration and certain adsorption processes.5

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:MONOMOLECULAR (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xspan paramstep
                          restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd fe ff p1 p2)
  (setq id "Monomolecular Curve"
        iq "y=A·(1-e^{-k(t-t₀)})"
        dcl "MONOMOLECULAR"
        opt '("Known parameters" "Unknown k" "Unknown k and t₀")
        kw 0
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions to division point"
        dct 200
        dpt '(5.0 0.0)
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (cond
   ((eq kw 0)
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "A (Final steady-state value)" 1.0)
          fb (get_real 6 "k (Rate constant, 1/time)" 1.0)
          fc (get_real 0 "t₀ (Step start time)" 0.0)))
   ((eq kw 1)
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "A (Final steady-state value)" 1.0)
          fc (get_real 0 "t₀ (Step start time)" 0.0)
          fd nil)
    (while (not fd)
      (setq p1 (get_point 0 "Curve point" nil basept)
            x1 (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
            y1 (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p1))
      (cond
        ((<= (abs (- x1 fc)) 1e-9) (prompt "\nCannot solve k from a point with t = t0. Pick another point."))
        ((<= (- 1.0 (/ y1 fa)) 1e-12) (prompt "\nPoint must satisfy 1 - y/A > 0 so ln(1 - y/A) is defined. Pick another point."))
        (T (setq fb (- (/ (log (- 1.0 (/ y1 fa))) (- x1 fc))) fd T)))))
   ((eq kw 2)
    (setq fa (get_real 2 "A (Final steady-state value)" 1.0)
          fd nil)
    (while (not fd)
      (setq p1 (get_point 0 "Point 1 on curve" nil basept)
            x1 (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p1)
            y1 (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p1)
            p2 (get_point 0 "Point 2 on curve" nil basept)
            x2 (get_xspan basept xaxisangle p2)
            y2 (get_yspan basept xaxisangle p2))
      (cond
       ((<= (abs (- x2 x1)) 1e-9) (prompt "\nPoints must have different t values. Pick again."))
       ((<= (- 1.0 (/ y1 fa)) 1e-12) (prompt "\nPoint 1 must satisfy 1 - y/A > 0 so ln(1 - y/A) is defined. Pick again."))
       ((<= (- 1.0 (/ y2 fa)) 1e-12) (prompt "\nPoint 2 must satisfy 1 - y/A > 0 so ln(1 - y/A) is defined. Pick again."))
       (T
        (setq fe (log (- 1.0 (/ y1 fa)))
              ff (log (- 1.0 (/ y2 fa)))
              fb (- (/ (- ff fe) (- x2 x1)))
              fc (/ (+ fe (* fb x1)) fb)
              fd T))))))
  (setq eq_display (strcat "A=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", k=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", t₀=" (rtos fc 2 4))
        xc '(eval a2)
        yc '(* fa (- 1.0 (exp (* -1.0 fb (- (eval a2) fc)))))
        paramstep (/ xspan divcount)
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:MONOMOLECULAR)
