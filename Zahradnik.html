Cissoid of Zahradnik

A family of rational cubic curves obtained as the cissoid of a conic section and a straight line with the pole on the conic.

Cissoid of Zahradnik implicit equation:
\[ (c x^{2} + 2 e x y + f y^{2}) x = (2 a + c d) x^{2} + 2 e x y + (b + f d) y^{2} \]
Cissoid of Zahradnik polar equation:
\[ r = \frac{d}{\cos\theta} + \frac{2 a \cos\theta + 2 b \sin\theta}{c \cos^{2}\theta + 2 e \cos\theta \sin\theta + f \sin^{2}\theta} \]

History and Applications

The Cissoid of Zahradnik1,2, named after the Czech mathematician Karel Zahradnik (1848–1916), generalizes the classical cissoid construction. Zahradnik introduced this family in his 1873 paper “Křivky cissoidální.” Instead of limiting the fixed curve to a circle (as in the ancient cissoid of Diocles), Zahradnik considered the cissoid generated by any conic section and a straight line, with the pole (origin) lying on the conic itself. This construction yields all rational cubic curves that possess a singular point at a finite distance and at least one asymptote at finite distance.

These curves belong to the broader class of cissoids of two curves. In the specific case where the conic is a circle, they produce the rational circular cubics. Notable special cases within the Zahradnik family include the folium of Descartes, the trisectrix of Maclaurin, the right strophoid, and the mixed cubic (when the conic is a parabola).

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences are not documented in available sources; the curve family remains primarily of interest in algebraic geometry and the classification of plane cubic curves.
