Lintearia

The equilibrium profile of a rectangular, flexible, inextensible, weightless sheet hung between two parallel bars at the same height and filled with water to the bar level; a member of the elastica family in which curvature is proportional to depth.

Lintearia parametric equations:
\[ x=\dfrac{a}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{\cos u}{\sqrt{\cos u-k}}\,du,\qquad y=-a\sqrt{\cos t-k} \]

History and Applications

The lintearia curve1,2 was posed and solved by Jacques Bernoulli in 1692. He named it lintearia after the Latin linteum, a linen cloth. It describes the equilibrium profile taken by a rectangular, flexible, inextensible, and weightless tarpaulin (or linen sheet) suspended between two parallel horizontal bars at the same height and filled with water up to the level of the supporting bars in a uniform gravitational field. Two vertical planes limit the sideways flow of water. The hydrostatic pressure increases linearly with depth, producing a curvature directly proportional to that depth. In the parametric equations used here, the parameter k controls the sag: smaller k produces deeper, more pronounced curvature. The same curve is identical to a member of the classical elastic-rod family, also known as the elastica.2,3 The elastic curve itself arises when a thin elastic rod is bent in a plane by end moments or forces such that its curvature remains proportional to distance from a fixed directrix line. Bernoulli’s solution for the lintearia thus provided one of the earliest exact treatments of what later became known as the elastica problem.

The radicand \(\cos t-k\) is non-negative only for \(|t|\le\arccos k\) with \(k<1\). The AutoLISP routine remaps that interval onto a fixed parameter span of 180° by setting the integration variable equal to \(\arccos(k)\sin c\) with \(c\) running from \(-\pi/2\) to \(\pi/2\), so the full open profile is drawn without the user having to compute the cutoff angle. Scale factor \(a\) sets overall size; \(k\) sets shape. As \(k\) approaches 1 the sag vanishes and the profile flattens toward the waterline. As \(k\) decreases the trough deepens.

Real-life applications occur where flexible membranes or sheets enclose liquid under gravity or where thin elastic elements bend under load. The primary documented case is the tarpaulin itself: any hanging, water-filled flexible rectangular container assumes this exact profile.1 The underlying mechanics also govern the cross-section of certain hydrostatic loading problems on membranes and the equilibrium shape of related 3D surfaces such as dangling drops. The broader elastic-curve family, of which the lintearia is a special instance, supplies the exact shape assumed by bent elastic rods or splines under controlled end conditions; this has been used in mechanical analysis of flexible members in structures and in the historical development of variational methods that underpin modern spline-based design tools.3

(defun C:LINTEARIA (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                      divpt xspan restartflag maxdraw id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb)

  (defun LIN_Y (a b c / d)
    (setq d (- (cos (* (acos b) (sin c))) b))
    (if (< (abs d) 1e-12) 0.0 (* (- a) (alt_expt (if (< d 1e-12) 1e-12 d) 0.5 T))))
  
  (defun LIN_X (a b c / d e f g h i j k)
    (setq d  (if (< c 0.0) -1.0 1.0)
          e    0.0
          f (abs c)
          k  0.0)
    (while (< e f)
      (setq g (/ paramstep 4.0))
      (if (> (+ e g) f) (setq g (- f e)))
      (setq h (* d (+ e (* 0.5 g)))
            i     (cos (* (acos b) (sin h)))
            j   (- i b))
      (if (< j 1e-12) (setq j 1e-12))
      (setq k (+ k (* (* 0.5 a (/ i (alt_expt j 0.5 T)) (acos b) (cos h)) g)))
      (setq e (+ e g)))
    (* d k))  
  
  (setq id "Lintearia"
        iq "x=(a/2)∫(cos(u)/√(cos(u)-k))du, y=-a√(cos(t)-k)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 180
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 6 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_boundr 0 "k (smaller = deeper, must be < 1)" 0.5 nil 1.0 nil nil)
        eq_display (strcat "\nLintearia: a=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", k=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        xc '(LIN_X fa fb (eval a2))
        yc '(LIN_Y fa fb (eval a2))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 (/ pi -2.0)
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:LINTEARIA)
