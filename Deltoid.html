Deltoid

A three-cusped hypocycloid generated by a point fixed on a circle of radius a rolling inside a fixed circle of radius 3a.

Deltoid Cartesian equation:
\[ (x^{2} + y^{2})^{2} + 18a^{2}(x^{2} + y^{2}) - 27a^{4} = 8a(x^{3} - 3xy^{2}) \]
Deltoid polar equation:
\[ r^{4} + 18a^{2}r^{2} - 27a^{4} = 8ar^{3}\cos(3\theta) \]
Deltoid parametric equations:
\[ x = a(2\cos(t) + \cos(2t)), \quad y = a(2\sin(t) - \sin(2t)) \]

History and Applications

The deltoid curve, also known as the tricuspoid or Steiner hypocycloid, is a three-cusped hypocycloid generated by a point fixed on a circle of radius a rolling inside a fixed circle of radius 3a. Leonhard Euler first considered the curve in 1745 in connection with an optical problem. Jakob Steiner studied the curve in 1856 and showed that it is the envelope of the Simson lines of a triangle inscribed in its circumcircle, the lines through the feet of perpendiculars from P to the triangle sides that sweep out a deltoid shape centered on the triangle’s nine-point (Euler) circle. It is also a classic example of a Kakeya set. It permits the continuous 360° rotation of a unit-length needle entirely within its interior. The needle remains inside the deltoid, typically contacting the boundary at three points (the two endpoints and one tangent point), except at the cusps. Sōichi Kakeya considered the deltoid as a candidate for the minimal-area region allowing such a rotation. The area of the deltoid scaled appropriately for a unit needle is π/8, which is smaller than that of a circle or an equilateral triangle of the same minimal width. Later, Abram Besicovitch proved that Kakeya sets with arbitrarily small area exist.1,2,3,4

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences are found in optics, where the deltoid arises in the study of caustics—the envelopes formed by families of reflected or refracted light rays. Its catacaustic with parallel incident rays from infinity is the astroid. In geometry and drafting, it serves as the envelope of Simson lines, providing a construction tool for analyzing triangle properties. In photonics engineering, deltoid-shaped caustic beams have been explicitly designed and realized experimentally using 3D-printed metasurfaces to produce structured light fields with controlled parabolic propagation trajectories.5 In robotics engineering, the deltoid (as the 3-cusped hypocycloid) is used to generate smooth hypocycloidal paths (SHP) that provide curvature-continuous, jerk-reduced, collision-free trajectories for multi-robot autonomous navigation and path planning.6 Also, as noted on MathCurve, the two tracing points P and Q describe the deltoid, and the line (PQ) stays tangent to the same deltoid. See also the Hypocycloid entry for more generalized options.
