Holditch Ellipse

The locus of the midpoint of a chord of fixed length 2c that slides with its endpoints on an ellipse of semiaxes a and b.

Holditch Ellipse polar equation:
\[ \dfrac{r^{2}}{a^{2} b^{2}} = \dfrac{1}{b^{2}\cos^{2}(\theta)+a^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)} - \dfrac{c^{2}}{b^{4}\cos^{2}(\theta)+a^{4}\sin^{2}(\theta)} \]

History and Applications

Holditch’s theorem1,2 was published in 1858 by Reverend Hamnet Holditch (1800–1867), an English mathematician who served as Senior Wrangler at Cambridge and later President of Gonville and Caius College. The theorem states that if a chord of fixed length slides with its endpoints on a smooth closed convex curve, the locus of a point dividing the chord into fixed segments p and q traces an inner closed curve whose enclosed area is smaller than the original by exactly πpq, the area of an ellipse with semiaxes p and q, independent of the shape or size of the outer curve. When the outer curve is an ellipse with semiaxes a (along x) and b (along y), and the tracing point is the midpoint of a chord of length 2c (with 0 ≤ c ≤ a), the resulting locus is the Holditch ellipse, a quartic algebraic curve of genus 2.

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences for Holditch curves appear in kinematics for analysis of sliding linkages and planar motions. Civil engineering uses include regularization of planar curves for railway line design, division of irregular enclosures into equal areas, lining of excavations, and planning paths for guided vehicles.3
