Teardrop Curve

A pear-shaped parametric curve, also called the Larme curve, drawn in two branches with a loop and hyperbolic branch.

Teardrop Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ (2a)^{n} y^{2} = (a + x)(a - x)^{n+1}, \quad -a \le x \le a \]
Teardrop Curve parametric loop equations:
\[ x = a \cdot \cos(t), \quad y = a \cdot \sin(t) \cdot \sin^{n}\!\left(\frac{t}{2}\right) \]
Teardrop Curve parametric branch equations:
\[ x = a \cdot \cosh(t), \quad y = \pm a \cdot \sinh(t) \cdot \sinh^{n}\!\left(\frac{t}{2}\right) \]

History and Applications

The Teardrop curve (also called the Larme curve) belongs to a family of pear-shaped or tear-drop parametric curves that produce a characteristic pointed tip and rounded body.1 It is presented in two selectable branches. The loop branch generates a closed or unicursal teardrop form whose shape is controlled by the exponent \( n \): for positive integer \( n \ge 1 \) the curve is algebraic of degree \( n + 2 \). Special cases are well-known: \( n = 1 \) recovers the loop of the right parabolic folium, while \( n = 2 \) yields (up to homothety) the classical piriform quartic.2 The hyperbolic branch supplies a real-valued continuation that remains useful when \( n \) is negative or near singularities at the origin in parameter space, employing hyperbolic functions to handle the corresponding blow-up or extension behavior. The piriform (pear-shaped) quartic itself was studied by G. de Longchamps in 1886; the name derives from the Latin pirum (pear) + forma (shape).3 The broader tear-drop family appears in modern curve catalogs as a convenient trigonometric parametrization that captures the essential geometry through the power \( n \) modulating the transverse component. The loop traces a single continuous path; depending on \( n \) the tip may be cusped or smoothly rounded, and the curve is symmetric about the x-axis for the standard orientation.

Real-life applications exist in engineering and the natural sciences. In micro-robotics and biomedical device design, teardrop and pear-shaped profiles are used for the bodies of light-actuated or untethered microbots to optimize drag, propulsion, and maneuverability in low-Reynolds-number microfluidic flows.4 In computer-aided manufacturing, the parametric teardrop form serves as a standard test curve for real-time CNC interpolation algorithms, where curvature variation, chord-error bounds, and feed-rate scheduling are evaluated for precision tool-path generation.5 The piriform/teardrop shape also appears in analyses of flexible rope and net geometries in marine engineering, notably in the dynamics of anchor-seine systems where pear-shaped cross-sections influence deployment tension and shape.6 No specific documented engineering or scientific applications were identified for the hyperbolic branch beyond its role as a mathematical extension for plotting singular or negative-exponent cases.

(defun C:TEARDROP (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                     divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc)
  (setq id "Teardrop Curve"
        iq ""
        dcl "TEARDROP"
        opt '("Teardrop loop" "Hyperbolic branch")
        kw 0
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (princ (if (= kw 0) "\nTeardrop loop: x=a·cos(t), y=a·sin(t)·sinⁿ(t/2)" "\nTeardrop hyperbolic branch: x=a·cosh(t), y=±a·sinh(t)·sinhⁿ(t/2)"))
  (setq fa (get_real 6 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "n (exponent)" 2)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", n=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        xc (if (= kw 0) '(* fa (cos (eval a2))) '(* fa (cosh (eval a2))))
        yc (if (= kw 0) '(progn (setq fc (sin (/ (eval a2) 2.0))) (if (and (< fb 0.0) (< (abs fc) 1e-6)) 1e13 (* fa (sin (eval a2)) (alt_expt (abs fc) fb T))))
           '(progn (setq fc (sinh (/ (eval a2) 2.0))) (if (and (< fb -1.0) (< (abs fc) 1e-6)) 1e13 (* fa 2.0 (cosh (/ (eval a2) 2.0)) (alt_expt (abs fc) (+ fb 1.0) T)))))
        skm (if (= kw 0) '(T T T) '(T nil T))
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
