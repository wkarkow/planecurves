Cornoid Curve

A sextic algebraic plane curve; the orthoptic curve of the Maltese cross.

Cornoid implicit equation:
\[ (x^{2} + y^{2})^{3} + a^{2}(3x^{4} - 6x^{2}y^{2} - 5y^{4}) + 8a^{4}y^{2} - 4a^{6} = 0 \]
Cornoid parametric equations:
\[ x = a \cos(t)\cos(2t),\quad y = a \sin(t)(2 + \cos(2t)) \]

History and Applications

The cornoid curve1,2,3, studied by the Salvadoran engineer and mathematician Alberto Sánchez in 1895, is a sextic algebraic plane curve. Sánchez provided its geometric construction: given a circle of radius a centered at the origin and a horizontal line intersecting that circle at points P and P', the cornoid is the locus of the projection M of P onto the line through P' parallel to the radius OP. The cornoid is also the orthoptic curve of the Maltese cross3, the locus of points from which two perpendicular tangents can be drawn to the Maltese cross. As a sixth-degree algebraic curve it has been examined in classical algebraic geometry, including studies of rational curves and computer-aided geometric design.

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences are not documented in the primary mathematical references. The curve remains of theoretical interest.

(defun C:CORNOID (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt restartflag
                    id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa)
  (setq id "Cornoid Curve"
        iq "x=a·cos(t)·cos(2t), y=a·sin(t)·(2+cos(2t))"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4))
        xc '(* fa (cos (eval a2)) (cos (* 2 (eval a2))))
        yc '(* fa (sin (eval a2)) (+ 2 (cos (* 2 (eval a2)))))
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
