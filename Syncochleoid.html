Syncochleoid

A transcendental spiral curve, the strophoidal branch of the hyperbolic spiral that is complementary to cochleoid.

Syncochleoid polar equation:
\[ r = \frac{a \cos \theta}{\theta} \]
Generalized Syncochleoid polar equation:
\[ r = \frac{a \lvert\cos^{b} (c \theta)\rvert}{\theta^{d}} \]

History and Applications

The syncochleoid is the complementary branch of the strophoidal curve of the hyperbolic spiral. When a point traces a hyperbolic spiral, one of the two points generated by the strophoidal construction traces a cochleoid; the other traces a syncochleoid, whose polar equation is \( r = a \cos\theta / \theta \). The name and the explicit identification appear on MathCurve (Robert Ferréol). No earlier independent study or catalogue entry for the curve under this name has been located. In generalizing this curve, the absolute value of cosine is used if the cosine exponent is not an integer.1,2 The related cochleoid itself is classical (Wallis 1685, Peck 1700, Bernoulli 1726, Cesàro 1878, Falkenburg 1884). The syncochleoid, however, is known only through the strophoidal relation just described. No geometric constructions, kinematic interpretations, or other properties peculiar to the syncochleoid (as distinct from the cochleoid) are recorded in the sources.

No applications in engineering or the natural sciences have been found for the syncochleoid. The parent hyperbolic spiral appears in conical perspective of helices and in certain architectural and galactic models; the cochleoid has been used in kinematic analyses of a point moving along the curve and in classical rectification and angle-division constructions. None of these uses extends to the syncochleoid itself.
