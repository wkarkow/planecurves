Lemoine Septic

Rational septic of degree 7; locus of the concurrence point of an altitude, a median, and a bisector when the altitude vertex is fixed at (0, a) and the median vertex at (0, 0).

Lemoine Septic implicit equation:
\[ x(x^{2}+y^{2})^{3}-(3x^{2}-7y^{2})(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}+x(3x^{2}-19y^{2})(x^{2}+y^{2})-(x^{4}-18x^{2}y^{2}-5y^{4})-7xy^{2}+y^{2}=0 \]
Lemoine Septic parametric equations:
\[ x=a\frac{\cos t\,(3\cos^{2}t-3\cos t+1)}{2\cos^{3}t-2\cos t+1},\qquad y=a\frac{\cos^{2}t\sin t}{2\cos^{3}t-2\cos t+1}\tan^{2}\frac{t}{2} \]

History and Applications

The Lemoine septic1 arises as the locus of the point of concurrence of an altitude, a median, and an angle bisector in a triangle, under the condition that these three lines are concurrent. Émile Lemoine (1840–1912) considered such triangles in 1885. Lemoine, a French civil engineer who later rose to chief inspector responsible for the gas supply of Paris, is best known for initiating the modern study of the triangle through his 1873–74 work on the symmedian point (the Lemoine point, the isogonal conjugate of the centroid).2 In the construction fixed by MathCurve, the vertex from which the altitude is drawn is held at (0, a) and the vertex from which the median is drawn is held at (0, 0); the locus of their common intersection with the bisector is the septic. The curve is rational of degree 7 and possesses a vertical asymptote at \(x = -7a\).

No applications of this particular septic have been identified in engineering or the natural sciences. Lemoine’s broader geometric results (the Lemoine point and the associated circles) remain of interest in pure triangle geometry and in the cataloguing of concurrent cevians, but the septic itself appears only as an algebraic locus answering the 1885 concurrency question.3
