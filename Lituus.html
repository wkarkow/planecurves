Lituus

A plane curve with polar equation \( r = a\,\theta^{-1/2} \), one of the classical power-law spirals; the inverse of Fermat’s spiral with respect to the origin.

Lituus polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{a}{\sqrt{\theta}} \]

History and Applications

The Lituus1,2, named after the ancient Roman lituus (a curved augural staff or bishop’s crosier), was studied by Roger Cotes (1682–1716) and described in his posthumous collection Harmonia Mensurarum (1722). Cotes, a Cambridge professor known for editing Newton’s Principia, introduced the curve. Colin Maclaurin gave it its name in the same volume. The Latin word denotes a bent staff or crook—specifically the curved staff of an augur or a bishop’s crosier—and the curve’s long sweeping arm resembles that shape closely enough that the name remained. Historically, the Lituus is the locus of a point on a rotating radius such that the area of the circular sector it sweeps out remains constant. In the modern classification of spirals, the Lituus is one of the power-law polar spirals of the form \( r = a\theta^{n} \) where \( n = -\frac{1}{2} \). It is the inverse curve with respect to the origin of Fermat’s spiral, \( r = a\sqrt{\theta} \). Geometrically, the Lituus has two branches (for positive and negative \( r \)) which both wind toward the origin as \( \theta \) increases, while the arm flares off to infinity as \( \theta \to 0 \). A frequently cited property is that each additional turn adds less radial change than the previous one, because radius and angle are linked by the inverse square-root law.

Real-life applications in engineering and the natural sciences are limited. The curve appears in the volutes of Ionic capitals in classical architecture.3 No additional uses were identified in standard mathematical references.

Also see the Spiral entry for more generalized spiral options.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:LITUUS (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                   restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display fa fb fc p1 rval xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2)
  (setq id "Lituus"
        iq "r=a·θ^{-0.5}"
        dcl "LITUUS"
        opt '("a value" "curve point")
        kw 0
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (cond
    ((eq kw 0)
     (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (scale)" 1.0)))
    ((eq kw 1)
     (setq p1 (get_point 0 "Point on curve" (polar basept xaxisangle 1.0) basept)
           fb (get_real 0 "# of extra revolutions to point" 0)
           fc (+ (* fb 2.0 pi) (- (angle basept p1) xaxisangle))
           fc (if (eq (alt_expt fc -0.5 T) 0) (alt_expt (+ (* (1+ fb) 2.0 pi) (- (angle basept p1) xaxisangle)) -0.5 T) (alt_expt fc -0.5 T))
           fa (/ (distance basept p1) fc))))
  (setq eq_display (strcat "\na= " (rtos fa 2 4))
        rval '(* fa (alt_expt (eval a2) -0.5 nil))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(T T nil)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:LITUUS)
