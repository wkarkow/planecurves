Parabola Negative Pedal Curve

The envelope of lines through points of the parabola \(x=at^{2}\), \(y=at\) that are perpendicular to the ray from a fixed pedal point.

Parabola Negative Pedal parametric equations:
\[ x = \dfrac{3a^{2}t^{4}+4a^{2}t^{2}-2a\,y_{0}\,t^{3}-a\,x_{0}\,t^{2}-4a\,y_{0}\,t+x_{0}y_{0}\,t+y_{0}^{2}}{a t^{2}-y_{0}t+x_{0}},\quad y = -\dfrac{a^{2}t^{5}-2a\,x_{0}\,t^{3}+a\,y_{0}\,t^{2}-4a\,x_{0}\,t+x_{0}^{2}t+x_{0}y_{0}}{a t^{2}-y_{0}t+x_{0}} \]

History and Applications

The negative pedal curve (also called the antipedal or orthocaustic) of a parabola with respect to a fixed pedal point \((x_{0},y_{0})\) is a rational parametric plane curve obtained by the envelope construction from the parabola \(x=at^{2}\), \(y=at\).1,2 Negative pedal curves reverse the ordinary pedal construction: they are the locus such that the original curve is the pedal of the new curve. The broader theory of pedal curves traces to Colin Maclaurin (circa 1718); the specific study of negative pedal curves of conic sections, including the parabola, appears in A. Ameseder’s 1879 paper on negative fusspunktcurven.3 E. H. Lockwood’s A Book of Curves (1967) also treats the family systematically.4

Special cases of the general form are better known. With the pedal point at the origin the curve reduces to a semicubical parabola. With the pedal point at the focus it becomes the Tschirnhausen cubic.1 The kinematic interpretation is that if one arm of a try-square slides along the original parabola while the other arm passes through the fixed point, the free arm envelopes the negative pedal curve.2

Real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences for the general parabola negative pedal curve (arbitrary \((x_{0},y_{0})\)) are not documented in the available mathematical literature. The curve remains of theoretical interest in classical differential geometry. Special-case reductions (semicubical parabola, Tschirnhausen cubic) appear in the study of caustics in geometrical optics and in the classification of algebraic curves, but no direct practical uses for the general parametric form have been identified.
