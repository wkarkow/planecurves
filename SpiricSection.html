Spiric Section

A bicircular quartic formed by cutting a torus with a plane parallel to its axis.

Spiric Section Cartesian equation:
\[ (x^{2}+y^{2}+r^{2}+c^{2}-a^{2})^{2}=4c^{2}(x^{2}+r^{2}) \]
Spiric Section parametric equations:
\[ x=\sqrt{(c+a\cos t)^{2}-r^{2}},\qquad y=a\sin t \]

History and Applications

The spiric section1,2, also known as the spiric of Perseus, is the plane curve formed by cutting a torus with a plane parallel to its axis of symmetry. Perseus, a Greek mathematician active around 150 BC, first studied these curves while examining sections of a “spira” (torus). The resulting quartic is one of the classical toric sections; its shape depends on the torus parameters (tube radius \(a\), distance from origin to tube center \(c\)) and the cutting-plane offset \(r\). For engineers and draftsmen the curve is most easily understood as the 2-D trace left when a circular tube is sliced by a flat plane. When \(c > a\) the torus is a ring and the section is a single closed loop. When \(c = a\) the tube touches the axis and the section becomes a lemniscate-like figure. When \(c < a\) the torus is a spindle and the section consists of two separate closed loops (outer and inner branches). The parametric form given below directly follows from parametrizing the circular tube cross-section and projecting the intersection back onto the drawing plane; it is algebraically equivalent to the implicit Cartesian equation for all real values of the parameters.

Real-life applications exist in engineering and the natural sciences. In photoelasticity the spiric section models stress patterns visible in transparent models under polarized light.3 In quantum mechanics the same algebraic form describes the probability density contours of electrons confined in double quantum dots.4 The curve also appears in certain optical systems that employ toroidal mirrors or lenses.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:SPIRICSECTION (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt xspan
                          restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd  fe ff fg fh fi fj)
  (setq id "Spiric Section"
        iq "x=√[(c+a·cos(t))²−r²], y=a·sin(t)"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw 0
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions in half-curve"
        dct 720
        dpt nil
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (tube radius)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "c (distance from origin to tube center)" 3.0)
        fc (get_real 0 "r (cutting plane y-offset)" 0.5)
        fg (abs fc)
        ff (/ (- fg fb) fa)
        fe (cond ((<= ff -1.0) pi) ((>= ff 1.0) 0.0) (T (acos ff)))
        fj (and (< fb fa) (<= fg (- fa fb)))
        fd (if fj (get_keyword 0 "Outer Inner" "draw Inner or Outer branch?" "Outer") "Outer")
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", c=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", r=" (rtos fc 2 4) ", branch=" fd)
        xc '(if (<= (- (alt_expt (+ fb (* fa (cos (eval a2)))) 2 nil) (* fc fc)) 1e-9) 0.0 (alt_expt (- (alt_expt (+ fb (* fa (cos (eval a2)))) 2 nil) (* fc fc)) 0.5 nil))
        yc '(* fa (sin (eval a2))))
  (if (eq fd "Outer") (setq paramstep (/ (* 2.0 fe) divcount) a1 (- fe)) (progn (setq fh (/ (- (- fg) fb) fa) fi (acos fh) paramstep (/ (- (* 2.0 pi) (* 2.0 fi)) divcount) a1 fi)))
  (setq a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc)
        skm '(nil T T))
  (cc)
  (princ))
