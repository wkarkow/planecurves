Double Heart Curve

Two curves suggestive of double hearts, one attributed to Saint-Vincent and Cramer, the other to Kerner.

Double Heart Cramer Cartesian equation:
\[ x = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\pm\sqrt{2 a y - y^{2}} \pm \sqrt{2 b y - y^{2}}\bigr) \]
Generalized Cramer Cartesian equation:
\[ x = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\pm\sqrt[c]{2 a y - d\cdot y^{2}} \pm \sqrt[e]{2 b y - f\cdot y^{2}}\bigr) \]
Double Heart Kerner parametric equations:
\[ x = \sin(t)\ln(\sin^{2}(t)),\quad y = -\cos(4 t) \]

History and Applications

The algebraic form of the Double Heart curve was studied by Grégoire de Saint-Vincent and later by Gabriel Cramer.1 Saint-Vincent (1584–1667), a Flemish Jesuit mathematician, is best known for his work on the hyperbola and for anticipating logarithmic concepts through geometric area arguments in his Opus Geometricum (1647). His work represents a transitional stage between classical Greek geometry and the emerging analytic methods of the 17th century. Cramer (1704–1752), a Swiss mathematician from Geneva, extended this line of inquiry into a more systematic study of algebraic curves. In his 1750 treatise, he classified curves by degree and investigated their singularities, helping to organize quartic curves such as this one into a broader algebraic framework. He is most widely remembered today for Cramer’s Rule, but his contributions to curve classification were equally influential in the development of analytic geometry. Mathcurve refers to this curve informally as “rabbit’s ears” because of its paired upward lobes. A second, visually similar form was later introduced by Sébastien Kerner using a parametric construction. Unlike the earlier quartic, Kerner’s version belongs to modern recreational mathematics, where curves are often created for their visual symmetry rather than arising from algebraic classification.

The double hearts are the polyzomal curves associated with two tangent circles. They are obtained by the slider-crank mechanism. In terms of applications, no direct engineering or physical uses have been documented for either form.

(defun C:DOUBLEHEART (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt
                        restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd fe ff fg fh fi)
  (setq id "Double Heart Curve"
        iq ""
        dcl "DOUBLEHEART"
        opt '("Cramer" "Kerner")
        kw 0
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span))
  (cond
    ((= kw 0)
      (princ "\nx=1/2·([t·(4a-c·t)]^(1/d)+fg·[t·(b-e·t)]^(1/f)), y=t")
      (setq fa (get_real 2 "a (eartip width factor)" 2.0)
            fb (get_real 2 "b (eartip height factor)" 4.0)
            fc (get_real 2 "c (first term coefficient)" 1.0)
            fd (get_real 2 "d (first term root)" 2.0)
            fe (get_real 2 "e (second term coefficient)" 1.0)
            ff (get_real 2 "f (second term root)" 2.0)
            fg (if (eq (get_keyword 0 "Inner Outer" "Inner or Outer curve?" "Inner") "Inner") -1.0 1.0)
            fh (* 2.0 fa)
            fi (/ fb 2.0)
            eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", c=" (rtos fc 2 4) ", d=" (rtos fd 2 4) ", e=" (rtos fe 2 4) ", f=" (rtos ff 2 4))
            paramstep (/ (* 2.0 (min fh fi)) divcount)
            skm '(nil T T)
            yc '(if (<= (eval a2) 0.0) 0.0 (eval a2))
            xc '(* 0.5 (+ (alt_expt (* (eval a2) (- (* 2.0 fh) (* fc (eval a2)))) (/ 1.0 fd) nil)
                     (* fg (alt_expt (* (eval a2) (- (* 2.0 fi) (* fe (eval a2)))) (/ 1.0 ff) nil))))))
    (T
      (princ "\nx=sin(t)·ln(sin²(t)), y=-cos(4t)")
      (setq eq_display "\nx=sin(t)·ln(sin²(t)), y=-cos(4t)"
            paramstep (/ (* 2.0 pi) divcount)
            skm '(T T T)
            xc '(* (sin (eval a2)) (log (avoid_zero (* (sin (eval a2)) (sin (eval a2))))))
            yc '(- (cos (* 4.0 (eval a2)))))))
  (setq a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
