Quartic Egg Curve

A plane algebraic egg-shaped curve of degree four generated by a two-stick linkage and a related parametric form obtained by modifying an ellipse.

Quartic Egg Curve implicit equation:
\[ \bigl(y^{2}(1-2a)-x^{2}+(a^{2}b^{2}-1)\bigr)^{2} = 4x^{2}\bigl(1-(1-b)^{2}y^{2}\bigr) \]
Quartic Egg Curve implicit parametric equations:
\[ x = b\cos a_{1} - \cos a_{2},\quad y = b\sin a_{1} + \sin a_{2},\quad \sin a_{2} = a\sin a_{1} \]
Quartic Egg Curve Wassenaar parametric equations:
\[ x = s\bigl(b\cos t - \sqrt{1-a^{2}\sin^{2}t}\bigr),\quad y = s(a+b)\sin t \]

History and Applications

The Quartic Egg Curve1 is a plane algebraic curve of degree four that approximates the shape of a hen’s egg, which is asymmetric with one end noticeably smaller and blunter than the other. One version arises from a mechanical linkage construction in which a fixed stick OP is joined at P to a second stick AQ that slides along the x-axis at A; the path traced by point Q forms the quartic egg curve. A closely related and popular parametric form, often called the Wassenaar egg curve after its proponent Jan Wassenaar, is obtained by modifying an ellipse to make one end smaller, resulting in a simple quartic equation particularly suited to chicken-egg proportions.2

An application occurs in studies of rigid-body dynamics. In 2003–2004, K. Sasaki et al. used the Wassenaar egg curve (and Cartesian and Cassini ovals) to model cross-sections of axisymmetric egg-shaped bodies in their investigation of the spinning behavior of eggs.3 The model helped explain the observed tendency of spinning eggs to rise onto their blunt end by analyzing the slip velocity at the contact point and identifying stable and unstable equilibrium angles, contributing to understanding the dynamics of asymmetric bodies under friction and gyroscopic effects.
