Delaunay Tractory

The path of the rear end of a fixed-length leash whose front end follows a Delaunay roulette (elliptic or hyperbolic).

Delaunay Tractory differential equations:
\[ \begin{cases} (x-x_0)\,dy=(y-y_0)\,dx \\ (x-x_0)^2+(y-y_0)^2=a^2 \\ f(x_0,y_0)=0 \end{cases} \]
Cartesian parametrization:
\[ \begin{cases} x=x_0-a\cos(\varphi_0-\alpha) \\ y=y_0-a\sin(\varphi_0-\alpha) \end{cases} \]
where \(\varphi_0\) is the base tangential angle and
\[ \dfrac{d\alpha}{ds_0}=\dfrac{1}{R_{c0}}-\dfrac{\sin\alpha}{a} \]

History and Applications

The Delaunay tractory is the path traced by the rear end of a leash of fixed length whose front end travels along a Delaunay roulette.1,2 A Delaunay roulette is itself the locus of one focus of a conic that rolls without slipping on a straight line. Charles-Eugène Delaunay (1816–1872), a French astronomer and mathematician, identified these roulettes in 1841 while classifying the surfaces of revolution of constant mean curvature.3 The elliptic case arises from a rolling ellipse and the hyperbolic case from a rolling hyperbola; the parabolic case recovers the ordinary catenary. Lorenz Lindelöf examined the same curves in 1861 and noted their elliptic-integral parametrizations.4

The underlying tractory construction is older. In the 1670s Claude Perrault posed the problem of the curve traced by a watch dragged by a chain of constant length whose free end moves along a straight edge. Christiaan Huygens named the resulting curve the tractrix in 1692 and supplied its analytic description; Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz and the Bernoulli brothers studied it shortly afterward.5 The general tractory replaces the straight base by an arbitrary curve: the leash remains of fixed length and is always tangent to the path of the rear point. When the base is a Delaunay roulette the rear path therefore inherits the periodic undulation of the elliptic roulette or the alternating bulges and necks of the hyperbolic roulette, swinging above and below the base exactly as a rigid stick dragged along a tracing of the roulette will do.

No independent engineering applications peculiar to the tractory of a Delaunay roulette have been recorded. The parent roulettes, however, generate the Delaunay surfaces (unduloids from the elliptic roulette, nodoids from the hyperbolic roulette) that appear as equilibrium shapes of soap films, liquid bridges between plates, and certain capillary or membrane configurations.6,7 Ordinary tractrices (straight base) appear in sheet-metal deep-drawing dies and as the meridian of the pseudosphere; the same tangent-length property governs the rear-wheel tracks of a vehicle whose front wheels follow a prescribed path.8
