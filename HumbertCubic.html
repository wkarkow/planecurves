Humbert Cubic

An elliptic cubic algebraic curve of degree three with 3-fold rotational symmetry of 120 degrees.

Humbert Cubic implicit equation:
\[ x^{3} - 3xy^{2} = a^{3} \]
Humbert Cubic polar equation:
\[ r = \dfrac{a}{\sqrt[3]{\cos(3\theta)}} \]

History and Applications

The Humbert cubic1, studied by French mathematician Marie Georges Humbert (1859–1921) in 1887 and published in the Journal de mathématiques pures et appliquées (p. 377), is a plane algebraic curve of degree three. Humbert, a professor of analysis at the École Polytechnique and member of the Académie des Sciences, contributed to geometry and the theory of elliptic and hyperelliptic functions. This curve is an elliptic cubic (a smooth cubic) and a special case of the sinusoidal spiral, \( r^{n} = a^{n}\cos(n\theta) \), with parameter \( n = -3 \). It is the inverse (with respect to the origin) of the Kiepert curve and is one of the few cubics for which the derivative \( \frac{dy}{dx} \) admits a rational expression in terms of both x and y. It also possesses 3-fold rotational symmetry of 120 degrees, a property shared with only a small number of other cubics such as the equilateral trefoil.

No real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences are documented for the Humbert cubic in primary mathematical references.
