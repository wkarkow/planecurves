Centered Trochoid

Plane curve generated by the vector sum of two uniform circular motions. Closed when the frequency ratio ω₂/ω₁ is rational.

Centered Trochoid parametric equations:
\[ x = r_1 \cos(\omega_1 t) + r_2 \cos(\omega_2 t),\quad y = r_1 \sin(\omega_1 t) + r_2 \sin(\omega_2 t) \]
Meplat condition:
\[ r_1 \omega_1^2 = r_2 \omega_2^2 \]

History and Applications

The centered trochoid is the plane curve generated by the vector sum of two uniform circular motions. When the frequency ratio ω₂/ω₁ is rational the curve is closed. The special meplat sub-family satisfying the equal-centripetal-acceleration condition r₁ω₁² = r₂ω₂² develops flat segments (meplats) at its outermost points.1

Trochoidal curves produced by rolling circles were drawn by Albrecht Dürer in 1525 and examined mathematically by Ole Rømer in 1674.2 The parametric representation as the sum of two circular motions was developed in the classical theory of epitrochoids and hypotrochoids during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries in the context of kinematics and algebraic geometry.

Applications

In mechanical engineering, centered trochoids and their parallel curves are used for cam profiles, non-circular gears, trochoidal rotary pumps, and the epitrochoidal housings of rotary combustion engines.3 Hybrid and higher-order trochoidal profiles are also employed in modern form-fit shaft–hub connections, where they combine high torque capacity with self-centering action.4

In the natural sciences, curves of this family appear in the mathematical description of certain multi-harmonic biological waveforms and in the analysis of wave-interference patterns.5
