Devil's Curve

A quartic curve of genus 2 consisting of a central figure-eight loop and two infinite branches.

Devil's Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ y^{4}-a^{2}y^{2}=x^{4}-b^{2}x^{2} \]
Devil's Curve polar equation:
\[ r^{2}(\sin^{2}\theta-\cos^{2}\theta)=a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta-b^{2}\cos^{2}\theta \]
Devil's Curve parametric equations:
\[ x=\cos\theta\sqrt{\dfrac{a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta-b^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta-\cos^{2}\theta}},\quad y=\sin\theta\sqrt{\dfrac{a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta-b^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta-\cos^{2}\theta}} \]

History and Applications

The Devil's Curve, also known as the Devil on Two Sticks or Cramer's curve, was first investigated by Gabriel Cramer (1704–1752) in 1750 as part of his Introduction à l'analyse des lignes courbes. Cramer, a Swiss mathematician and professor of mathematics at Geneva, is best known today for Cramer's rule in linear algebra but devoted significant effort to the study of algebraic curves. The curve was examined again by Lacroix in 1810 and appeared in the Nouvelles Annales de Mathématiques in 1858. Its name derives from the central lemniscate, which resembles the diabolo (a juggling toy also called "devil on two sticks"), consisting of two cones joined at their tips and spun on a string between two sticks.

The curve is a quartic of genus 2. When the parameter ratio satisfies a specific condition (b/a = 25/24 in the Cartesian form), a special case known as the electric motor curve arises. The Devil's Curve serves primarily as an illustrative example in analytical geometry textbooks and in the presentation of Riemann surface theory. No applications in engineering or the natural sciences beyond these mathematical contexts have been identified.

; DEVILS.LSP - started 7/10/25

(defun C:DEVILS (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep divpt
                   restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc skm a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 rval maxdraw fa fb fc fd fe ff fg)
  (setq id "Devil's Curve"
        iq "x=cos(θ)·√[(a²·sin²θ-b²·cos²θ)/(sin²θ-cos²θ)], y=sin(θ)·√[(a²·sin²θ-b²·cos²θ)/(sin²θ-cos²θ)]"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions in curve"
        dct 720
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "a (vertical scale)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "b (horizontal scale)" 2.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", b=" (rtos fb 2 4))
        rval '(max 0.0 (/ (- (* fa fa (sin (eval a2)) (sin (eval a2))) (* fb fb (cos (eval a2)) (cos (eval a2))))
              (avoid_zero (- (* (sin (eval a2)) (sin (eval a2))) (* (cos (eval a2)) (cos (eval a2)))))))
        xc '(* (cos (eval a2)) (alt_expt (eval rval) 0.5 T))
        yc '(* (sin (eval a2)) (alt_expt (eval rval) 0.5 T))
        a2 (- (/ pi 4.0) paramstep)
        fc (eval xc)
        fd (eval yc)
        a2 (+ (/ pi 4.0) paramstep)
        fe (eval xc)
        ff (eval yc)
        fg (* (max (abs fc) (abs fd) (abs fe) (abs ff)) 0.99)
        a2 0
        maxdraw (get_real 2 "Maximum x and y values allowed?" fg)
        skm '(T T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:DEVILS)
