Atriphtaloid

A sextic algebraic plane curve first studied in 1882 to model sea surfaces on an attractive sphere, featuring vertical asymptotes near the y-axis, endpoint cusps on the x-axis, and an optional detached oval.

Atriphtaloid implicit equation:
\[ x^{4}(x^{2} + y^{2}) - (a x^{2} - b)^{2} = 0 \]
Atriphtaloid Cartesian equation:
\[ y = \pm \frac{\sqrt{(a x^{2} - b)^{2} - x^{6}}}{x^{2}} \]

History and Applications

The atriphtaloid3 is a sextic algebraic plane curve of degree six first studied in 1882 by Haughton and Townsend to model the form of the surface of the seas covering an attractive sphere. It is also referenced by Haughton and Longchamps and known as the atriphtalassic (or atriphtothalassic) curve. The name derives from the Greek atripos (frictionless) and thalassa (sea). The curve is most commonly defined implicitly by \(x^{4}(x^{2}+y^{2})-(a x^{2}-b)^{2}=0\). The atriphtaloid exhibits vertical asymptotic behavior near the y-axis, endpoint cusps on the x-axis, and depending upon the presence versus absence of three real roots of an associated cubic in \(u=x^{2}\), a detached oval. These features made it a useful example in the historical classification of sextic curves by real topology rather than by degree alone.

Real-life applications include this historical theoretical modeling of sea surfaces under gravitational attraction. No other direct engineering or physical governing uses have been identified in standard references.
