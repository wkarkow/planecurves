Stirrup Curve

A plane algebraic curve given by \((x^{2}-1)^{2}=y^{2}(y-1)(y-2)(y+5)\) with eight real branches that together form a stirrup-shaped figure.

Stirrup Curve implicit equation:
\[ (x^{2} - 1)^{2} = y^{2}(y - 1)(y - 2)(y + 5) \]
Stirrup Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ x = \pm\sqrt{1 \pm \sqrt{y^{2}(y - 1)(y - 2)(y + 5)}} \]

History and Applications

The Stirrup Curve1,2 is a quartic plane curve presented in standard references on special algebraic curves. It is defined by the implicit equation shown below. When rearranged as a polynomial in y it becomes a quintic, placing it among other higher-degree examples such as the Burnside curve and butterfly catastrophe curve in collections of plane curves.

It has no known practical applications.
