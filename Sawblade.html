Sawblade Curve

A closed toothed outline generated by a chord that rotates steadily inside a fixed circle while its length oscillates with an absolute-value sine. A tracing point offset from the chord center produces the hook.

Sawblade Curve parametric equations:
\[ \begin{aligned} x &= \sqrt{R^{2}-d\,\lvert\sin(n\theta)\rvert^{2}}\,\cos(\theta)-\alpha\cdot d\,\lvert\sin(n\theta)\rvert\,\sin(\theta)\\ y &= \sqrt{R^{2}-d\,\lvert\sin(n\theta)\rvert^{2}}\,\sin(\theta)+\alpha\cdot d\,\lvert\sin(n\theta)\rvert\,\cos(\theta) \end{aligned} \]

History and Applications

This curve does not appear in the classical literature on roulettes, trochoids, or chord-generated curves. It was developed in 2026 by the author while exploring mechanical constructions based on a chord whose length varies periodically inside a fixed circle. The generating mechanism combines two simple motions: a slow, steady rotation of the chord’s orientation and a rapid oscillation of the chord’s length governed by an absolute-value sine function. A tracing point is placed at a fixed proportional distance from the chord’s center. The absolute-value term produces two length cycles per oscillation, which naturally generates the tooth-like features. Because the length function is not a pure sinusoid, the resulting curve is mathematically distinct from both epitrochoids and hypotrochoids, even though it shares the same underlying circle-and-chord topology. No earlier published description has been identified of this specific combination, slow chord rotation plus absolute-sine length oscillation with an offset tracing point.

At present the curve is presented for illustrative and decorative use.
