Power Function

Cartesian family \(y=k\cdot x^{a}\) with multiplier \(k\) and real exponent \(a\).

Power Function Curve Cartesian equation:
\[ y = k \cdot x^{a} \]

History and Applications

The power function \(y = k \cdot x^{a}\), where \(k\) is a nonzero constant and \(a\) is any real number, is a fundamental family of curves in mathematics. Special cases such as the parabola (\(a = 2\)) and hyperbola (\(a = -1\)) were studied by ancient Greek geometers in conic sections. The general analytic treatment of power functions emerged with the development of analytic geometry in the 17th century.1

Power laws of this form describe many empirical relationships in the natural sciences and engineering. In astronomy, Kepler's third law states that the square of a planet's orbital period \(T\) is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis \(a\) of its orbit (\(T^{2} \propto a^{3}\)). In biology, Kleiber's law (established in the 1930s) shows that an animal's basal metabolic rate scales with body mass \(M\) to approximately the \(\frac{3}{4}\) power.2 In geophysics, the Gutenberg-Richter law models earthquake frequency as a power-law function of magnitude. In engineering applications, the wind-profile power law \(u(z) = u_{r}\bigl(\frac{z}{z_{r}}\bigr)^{\alpha}\) (with \(\alpha\) typically \(\frac{1}{7}\) over flat terrain) is standard for estimating wind speed variation with height in structural design, wind-turbine siting, and atmospheric dispersion modeling.3 Power functions also govern scaling in fluid mechanics (e.g., certain drag forces), material strength versus size in structural engineering, and allometric relationships in biomechanics.

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:POWERFUNC (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo paramstep
                      divpt xspan restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span eq_display xc yc a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 fa fb fc fd)
  (setq id "Power Function"
        iq "y=k·xᵃ"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'param
        dpr "# of divisions to x-span of division point"
        dct 200
        dpt '(2.0 0.0)
        span nil
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 2 "k (multiplier)" 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "a (exponent)" 0.5)
        fc (get_keyword 0 "Real Imaginary" "Real or Imaginary values?" "Real")
        fd (if (= fc "Real") T nil)
        paramstep (/ xspan divcount)
        paramstep (if (= (get_keyword 0 "Positive Negative" "Positive or Negative x values?" "Positive") "Positive") paramstep (- paramstep))
        eq_display (strcat "\nk=" (rtos fa 2 4) ", a=" (rtos fb 2 4) ", " fc " values")
        xc '(eval a2)
        yc '(* fa (alt_expt (eval a2) fb fd))
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:POWERFUNC)
