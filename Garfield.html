Garfield Curve

An informally named polar curve that resembles the face of the cartoon cat Garfield.

Garfield Curve polar equation:
\[ r = \theta \cos \theta \]
Generalized polar equation:
\[ r = a \cdot (b \theta)^{c} \cdot \cos^{e} (d \theta) \]

History and Applications

The Garfield curve1 is an informally named polar curve that first appeared in calculus textbooks as an exercise illustrating graphing in polar coordinates. When the basic form r = θ cos θ is plotted from θ = −2π to 2π, the resulting figure bears a striking resemblance to the face of the cartoon cat Garfield, with two prominent “ears” formed by the lobes of the curve. The name derives solely from this visual similarity rather than from any historical mathematician or classical treatment. The AutoLISP implementation generalizes the curve to the family r = a · (bθ)c · cose(dθ), permitting controlled variation of the five parameters while preserving the essential character of the original plot. No earlier historical development or named predecessors are documented; the curve functions primarily as a modern pedagogical example for demonstrating how the product of a linear term in θ and a trigonometric term in θ generates distinctive polar shapes.

Real-life applications in engineering or the natural sciences have not been documented for the Garfield curve or its generalizations.

References

AutoLISP Code

(defun C:GARFIELD (/ basept xaxisangle cd sa sb sm om os divcount cosangle sinangle x0 y0 plineents segcount baseinfo divpt xstep paramstep
                     restartflag id iq dcl opt kw mode dct dpt span rval xc yc a1 a2 x1 y1 x2 y2 eq_display fa fb fc fd fe maxdraw)
  (setq id "Garfield Curve"
        iq "r=a·(bθ)ᶜ·cos(dθ)ᵉ"
        dcl nil
        opt nil
        kw nil
        mode 'polar
        dpr "# of divisions around circle"
        dct 360
        dpt nil
        span 360
        kw (ca id iq dcl opt kw mode dpr dct dpt span)
        fa (get_real 0 "a (scale): " 1.0)
        fb (get_real 2 "b (angle multiplier): " 1.0)
        fc (get_real 2 "c (angle exponent): " 1.0)
        fd (get_real 2 "d (cosine multiplier): " 1.0)
        fe (get_real 2 "e (cosine exponent): " 1.0)
        eq_display (strcat "\na= " (rtos fa 2 4) ", b= " (rtos fb 2 4) ", c= " (rtos fc 2 4) ", d= " (rtos fd 2 4) ", e= " (rtos fe 2 4))
        rval '(* fa (alt_expt (* fb (eval a2)) fc nil) (alt_expt (cos (* fd (eval a2))) fe nil))
        xc '(* (eval rval) (cos (eval a2)))
        yc '(* (eval rval) (sin (eval a2)))
        skm '(nil T T)
        a1 0
        a2 a1
        x1 (eval xc)
        y1 (eval yc)
        a2 '(+ a1 paramstep)
        x2 '(eval xc)
        y2 '(eval yc))
  (cc))
(C:GARFIELD)
