Parabolic Trifolium

Rational quartic with a triple point at the origin and is asymptotic to a parabola; not a true trifolium.

Cartesian equation (classical):
\[ x^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})=a y(x^{2}-y^{2}) \]
Polar equation (classical):
\[ r=a\dfrac{\sin\theta\cos 2\theta}{\cos^{2}\theta} \]
Cartesian parametrization (classical):
\[ x=a\cos 2t\tan t,\quad y=a\cos 2t\tan^{2}t \quad (t=\theta) \]
Rational Cartesian parametrization (classical):
\[ x=at\dfrac{1-t^{2}}{1+t^{2}},\quad y=tx \quad (t=\tan\theta) \]
Asymptotic parabola (a=1):
\[ y=-x^{2}-2 \]
Generalized polar equation:
\[ r=a\dfrac{\sin\theta\cos(n\theta)}{\cos^{m}\theta} \]

History and Applications

The parabolic trifolium is a rational quartic plane curve studied by Gaston Albert Gohierre de Longchamps in 1888 (Loria, p. 136).1 It possesses a triple point at the origin and an asymptotic parabola, from which the name derives. MathCurve records the classical Cartesian equation \(x^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})=a y(x^{2}-y^{2})\), the polar form \(\rho=a\sin\theta\cos 2\theta/\cos^{2}\theta\), and the two parametrizations shown above. For the scale a=1 the asymptotic parabola is given as \(y=-x^{2}-2\).1 MathCurve notes that, just as the parabolic folium is not a folium, the parabolic trifolium is not a trifolium.1 The ordinary trifolium (three-petaled rose) is a different rational quartic arising as a pedal of the deltoid; the parabolic specimen shares only the historical association with Longchamps and the presence of a triple point.

No applications in engineering or the natural sciences were found for this curve. The constructions remain purely geometric.
